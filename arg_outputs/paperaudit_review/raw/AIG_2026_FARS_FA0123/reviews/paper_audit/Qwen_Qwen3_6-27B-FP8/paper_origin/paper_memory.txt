# Global Summary

This paper investigates whether the "repetition advantage" in long chain-of-thought (CoT) supervised fine-tuning (SFT)—where repeating a small curated dataset outperforms training on significantly more unique data—is a genuine effect or a compute artifact. Previous work (Kopiczko et al., 2026) showed that training on 1,600 samples repeated 32 times beats training on 51,200 unique samples under step-matched conditions. However, the repetition condition processes approximately 2.85% more total tokens due to longer average response lengths, creating a potential confound.

The authors introduce "token-budget matching," a methodology that early-stops the repetition condition when its cumulative response tokens match the baseline's total token budget. Using the Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model on the NuminaMath-CoT dataset, the study finds that the repetition advantage is preserved and amplified under token-matched conditions. The ratio of token-matched to step-matched advantage ($\Delta_{tok}/\Delta_{step}$) is 6.33 on aggregate Pass@k, far exceeding the 0.8 threshold required to refute the compute confound. The mechanism is identified as improved termination rates (87–91% vs. 29–48%) rather than higher conditional accuracy. A caveat is noted that the paper was generated by an automated research system.

# Abstract

- **Problem:** Prior findings suggest repeating a small dataset (1,600 samples x 32 times) outperforms training on 32x more unique data (51,200 samples) in long-CoT SFT.
- **Confound:** Step-matched comparisons give the repetition condition ~2.85% more total tokens because the curated subset contains longer average responses.
- **Method:** Token-budget matching is introduced to isolate the repetition effect by early-stopping the repetition condition to match the baseline's cumulative token count.
- **Key Findings:**
    - The repetition advantage is preserved and amplified 6.33x ($\Delta_{tok}/\Delta_{step}$ ratio on aggregate Pass@k).
    - This definitively refutes the compute confound hypothesis.
    - Mechanism: Repetition training improves termination rates (87–91% vs. 29–48%) rather than conditional accuracy.
- **Caveat:** The paper states it was generated by an automated research system.

# Method

- **Experimental Design:** Three training conditions are compared:
    - **Condition A (Data Scaling):** 51,200 unique long-CoT samples, 1 epoch, 51,200 optimizer steps.
    - **Condition B (Step-Matched Repetition):** 1,600 curated samples, 32 epochs, 51,200 optimizer steps (replicates Kopiczko et al., 2026).
    - **Condition C (Token-Matched Repetition):** 1,600 curated samples, ~32 epochs, early-stopped when cumulative response tokens match Condition A's budget.
- **Data:** NuminaMath-CoT dataset (Li et al., 2024). The 51,200-sample set and 1,600-sample subset are from Kopiczko et al. (2026).
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Training uses bfloat16 precision, 8-bit Adam optimizer, batch size 1, and a constant learning rate of 2 x 10^-5 after 10% warmup.
- **Token-Budget Matching:**
    - Condition C terminates when cumulative response tokens reach Condition A's budget of 146,099,148 tokens.
    - Tolerance is +/- 0.5%.
    - This results in approximately 49,541 training steps (96.8% of Condition B's steps).
- **Compute Verification:**
    - Token budget deviation: -0.50% (within tolerance).
    - FLOPs proxy ($\sum L^2$) deviation: 0.56% from Condition A.
- **Decision Rule:**
    - Defines $\Delta_{step} = \text{Perf}(B) - \text{Perf}(A)$ and $\Delta_{tok} = \text{Perf}(C) - \text{Perf}(A)$.
    - If $\Delta_{tok} / \Delta_{step} \geq 0.8$, the compute confound is refuted.
- **Evaluation:**
    - **Benchmarks:** AIME'24, AIME'25 (30 problems each), GPQA Diamond (198 questions).
    - **Metrics:** Acc@k, Pass@k, Termination rate.
    - **Settings:** k=16 for AIME, k=4 for GPQA. Temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95.
    - **Reproducibility:** 3 random seeds (42, 123, 456). Mean +/- standard deviation reported.

# Experiments

- **Main Results:**
    - Condition C (Token-Matched) achieves the highest performance on all metrics across all benchmarks.
    - **Aggregate Pass@k:** Condition C (66.28%) > Condition B (40.83%) > Condition A (36.05%).
    - **GPQA Diamond Pass@4:** Condition C (64.14%) vs. Condition B (32.66%) vs. Condition A (29.80%).
    - **Termination Rates:** Condition C achieves 87–91% across benchmarks. Conditions A and B remain at 29–55%.
- **Decision Rule Application:**
    - Aggregate Pass@k ratio ($\Delta_{tok}/\Delta_{step}$) is 6.33, far exceeding the 0.8 threshold.
    - All individual metrics show ratios >= 1.0.
    - Conclusion: The repetition advantage is not a compute artifact.
- **Compute Matching Verification:**
    - Condition C uses 145,369,881 response tokens (-0.50% deviation from A).
    - Condition B uses 150,255,360 tokens (+2.85% surplus over A).
    - FLOPs proxy for C is $7.72 \times 10^{11}$ (0.56% deviation from A's $7.68 \times 10^{11}$).
- **Mechanism Analysis:**
    - Accuracy is decomposed into Termination Rate and Conditional Accuracy (Acc|Term).
    - Condition C has significantly higher termination rates (e.g., 86.88% on AIME'24 vs. 47.78% for A).
    - Condition C has *lower* conditional accuracy than Condition A (e.g., 57.21% vs. 59.59% on AIME'24).
    - The advantage operates entirely through improved termination behavior (producing complete chains), not better individual answer quality.

# Appendix

- **Training Dynamics:**
    - **Token Accuracy:** Conditions B and C (repetition training) achieve near-complete memorization with token accuracy exceeding 99.7%.
    - **Condition A:** Plateaus at approximately 73.7% token accuracy due to the larger, more diverse dataset.
    - **Convergence:** Condition C reaches saturation in fewer steps due to early stopping at the token budget.
    - **Implication:** The repetition advantage is not explained by more training steps; Condition C achieves comparable memorization depth with 3.2% fewer steps than Condition B.
