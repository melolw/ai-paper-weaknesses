1) Summary
The paper investigates whether automating import and name resolution fixes improves LLM-based coding agents during feature development. The authors propose interface-aware smoke tests combined with a deterministic import autofix mechanism that inserts missing imports when exactly one unambiguous candidate exists. Evaluated on FeatureBench Lite (30 tasks) using the OpenHands CodeAct agent, three conditions are compared: baseline, diagnose-only, and full autofix. The study reports a negative result: the autofix mechanism yields a 10.0% resolved rate, identical to the baseline, while the diagnose-only variant achieves 16.67%. Analysis reveals that target errors constitute only 4–5% of the agent workflow and safety guards prevent most automated fixes. The authors conclude that diagnostic feedback aids agent reasoning, whereas automated fixes offer no benefit and may interfere with problem-solving.2) Strengths
- **Rigorous Experimental Design Isolating Component Effects**
  - The three-condition setup (Baseline, Diagnose-only, Full Autofix) cleanly separates diagnostic feedback from automated code modification (Section 3.4, Table 1). This matters for experimental rigor because it enables precise attribution of performance changes to specific interventions rather than conflating information provision with code alteration.
  - The inclusion of optimization iterations (C v2, C v3) tests robustness against prompt formatting and guard relaxation (Section 4.2, Table 1). This matters for technical soundness because it demonstrates that the null effect is consistent across variations, ruling out transient implementation artifacts.
  - The pilot study incorporates an explicit stop rule based on addressable overhead before full deployment (Section 3.5). This matters for methodological discipline because it prevents wasted computational resources on interventions with insufficient error frequency to impact outcomes.- **Transparent Reporting of Negative Results with Actionable Insights**
  - The manuscript explicitly frames the work as a negative result while extracting meaningful conclusions about agent behavior (Abstract, Section 5). This matters for scientific impact because it counters publication bias and redirects future research toward higher-yield interventions.
  - Crash analysis quantifies that target errors consume only 4–5% of agent steps and 6–7% of tokens (Section 4.3, Table 2). This matters for technical soundness because it provides a concrete, data-driven explanation for why automating these fixes yields no performance gain.
  - The finding that diagnostic feedback alone improves the resolved rate by 66.7% relative to baseline is clearly highlighted (Section 4.2, Table 1). This matters for practical impact because it offers a low-cost, non-invasive alternative that enhances agent reasoning without modifying source code.- **Well-Defined Safety Mechanisms and Mechanistic Analysis**
  - The autofix applies only under strict conditions (single candidate, non-test file, safe insertion) to prevent erroneous modifications (Section 3.3). This matters for technical soundness because it ensures system reliability and avoids introducing regressions during complex feature development.
  - Detailed statistics show safety guards correctly skipped 8 out of 11 diagnostic events, primarily due to test-file occurrences (Section 4.4, Table 3). This matters for experimental rigor because it validates the design choice to prioritize safety over aggressive automation.
  - Analysis reveals that most errors occur in test files requiring feature implementation rather than source files needing imports (Section 5). This matters for novelty because it clarifies a fundamental mismatch between the proposed intervention and the actual bottleneck in feature-level coding tasks.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Statistical Power and Generalizability**
  - Evaluation relies on only 30 tasks, with performance differences between conditions amounting to 1–2 resolved tasks (Section 4.1, Table 1). This matters for experimental rigor because the small sample size makes it difficult to claim statistical significance or rule out stochastic variation inherent to LLM outputs.
  - A single agent architecture (OpenHands CodeAct) and model (Qwen3-Coder-480B) are used without cross-validation (Section 4.1). This matters for generalizability because results may not transfer to other agent paradigms (e.g., ReAct, multi-agent systems) or models with different reasoning capabilities.
  - No ablation is performed on the strict safety guards to quantify their impact on potential fix success rates (Section 3.3, Section 4.4). This matters for technical soundness because it remains unclear whether relaxing guards would yield meaningful benefits or merely introduce harmful regressions.- **Incomplete Analysis of Resource and Workflow Impact**
  - Token and step usage differences are reported but not analyzed for cost-benefit trade-offs (Table 1, Section 4.2). This matters for practical impact because the diagnose-only variant increases input/output tokens by ~18% and steps by ~15%; the paper does not justify whether this overhead is acceptable for the marginal gain.
  - The pilot study design and stop rule are mentioned but lack quantitative results or methodology details (Section 3.5). This matters for experimental rigor because readers cannot verify how the 10% threshold was established or whether it was empirically met.
  - No discussion addresses how the 200-step budget constraint interacts with the diagnostic feedback loop (Section 4.1). This matters for technical soundness because agents might be terminating early due to budget exhaustion rather than task completion, potentially confounding the resolved rate metric.- **Methodological and Implementation Gaps**
  - The deterministic resolver relies on simple pattern matching (`def S(` or `class S()`) without handling aliases, nested scopes, or dynamic imports (Section 3.3). This matters for technical soundness because this limitation restricts the approach's applicability to more complex, real-world Python codebases.
  - The claim that automated fixes "may interfere with the agent’s problem-solving process" lacks qualitative traces or case studies (Section 5). This matters for clarity and impact because the interference hypothesis remains speculative without concrete examples of agent confusion or degraded reasoning trajectories.
  - Lack of comparison to existing error recovery or APR baselines beyond the standard agent loop (Section 2, Section 4). This matters for novelty because it is unclear how the proposed method compares to established techniques like Reflexion or CodeCureAgent in similar error-recovery settings.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope and Statistical Rigor**
  - Increase the task count to at least 100–200 tasks or apply bootstrapping/confidence intervals to assess the statistical significance of the 1–2 task differences observed in Table 1.
  - Evaluate the approach across at least two additional agent architectures (e.g., SWE-agent, multi-agent setups) and model scales to verify whether the findings generalize beyond OpenHands CodeAct (Section 4.1).
  - Conduct an ablation study on the safety guards (e.g., relax the non-test file constraint or allow multiple candidates with ranking) to measure the trade-off between fix success rate and introduced regressions (Section 3.3, Section 4.4).- **Deepen Resource Analysis and Clarify Experimental Details**
  - Perform a cost-benefit analysis comparing the token/step overhead of the diagnose-only variant against the 6.67% absolute improvement, discussing practical deployment thresholds and efficiency trade-offs (Table 1, Section 4.2).
  - Provide the pilot study’s quantitative results, including the exact percentage of fixable crashes observed and the empirical calibration process for the 10% stop rule (Section 3.5).
  - Analyze the distribution of agent termination reasons (budget exhaustion vs. task completion vs. error loops) to ensure the 200-step limit does not bias the resolved rate metric (Section 4.1).- **Strengthen Methodological Transparency and Qualitative Evidence**
  - Enhance the symbol resolver to handle common Python patterns (aliases, `__init__.py` exports, dynamic imports) or explicitly document these limitations with concrete failure cases (Section 3.3).
  - Include 2–3 qualitative case studies with agent interaction traces demonstrating how diagnostic feedback improves reasoning versus how autofix causes confusion or suboptimal subsequent actions (Section 5).
  - Add a brief comparison or discussion contrasting the proposed approach with existing error recovery methods (e.g., Reflexion, CodeCureAgent) to contextualize the findings within the broader automated program repair literature (Section 2, Section 5).5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper presents a well-structured negative result with clear experimental isolation (Section 3.4, Table 1), but limited statistical power and generalizability constrain its broader impact.
- Novelty (10): 5 — The combination of interface-aware smoke tests with deterministic autofix is incremental, though the finding that diagnostics outperform automation offers a useful practical insight (Section 5, Table 1).
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The methodology is sound and safety-conscious (Section 3.3), yet the small dataset, lack of ablation on safety guards, and missing pilot details weaken technical rigor (Section 3.5, Section 4.4).
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-organized, with clear figures (Figure 1), concise tables (Tables 1–3), and straightforward explanations of the experimental setup and results.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence in the assessment due to clear experimental reporting and accessible methodology, though limited by the small task count and single-model evaluation.
