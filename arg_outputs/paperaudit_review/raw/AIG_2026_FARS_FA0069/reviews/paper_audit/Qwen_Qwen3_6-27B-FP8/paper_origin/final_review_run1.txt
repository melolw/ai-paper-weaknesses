1) Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of handling truncated rollouts in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The authors propose "timeout bootstrapping," a method grounded in classical RL theory that treats token-limit truncation as a timeout rather than a terminal failure, bootstrapping the reward from the critic's value estimate at the truncation point. The authors conduct a pre-registered comparison of this method against standard truncate-as-failure and DAPO overlong shaping on the MATH-500 dataset using a DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B model. While timeout bootstrapping fails to meet pre-registered success criteria on average due to critic value collapse (where value estimates degenerate to -1.0), it demonstrates promise on stable training runs (+2.20pp over baseline). The paper provides a mechanistic analysis identifying critic miscalibration as the primary barrier to success.2) Strengths
*   **Strong Theoretical Grounding**
    *   The proposed method is derived from established RL theory regarding time limits (Pardo et al., 2017), providing a principled justification for treating truncation as a non-terminal state (Section 3.3, Equation 5). This strengthens the technical soundness by moving beyond heuristic reward shaping.
    *   The distinction between "terminal" and "timeout" terminations is clearly articulated, offering a novel conceptual lens for analyzing LLM training dynamics (Section 1, Section 3.2). This clarifies the motivation and frames the problem rigorously.
    *   The theoretical framework explains *why* standard truncate-as-failure might be suboptimal (biasing against long-horizon reasoning), adding depth to the motivation (Section 3.3). This enhances the conceptual impact of the work.
*   **Rigorous and Transparent Experimental Design**
    *   The study employs pre-registered success criteria, enhancing the credibility of the negative results and preventing p-hacking (Section 4.1). This demonstrates high experimental rigor and scientific integrity.
    *   The comparison includes relevant baselines (Truncate-as-Failure and DAPO Overlong Shaping) and uses multiple random seeds to assess stability (Table 1). This ensures a fair and comprehensive evaluation.
    *   The evaluation focuses on a specific "long subset" of problems where truncation is most relevant, ensuring the metric aligns with the problem setting (Section 4.1). This improves the validity of the reported performance gains.
*   **Deep Diagnostic and Mechanistic Analysis**
    *   Rather than simply reporting failure, the authors perform a thorough analysis of *why* the method failed, identifying critic value collapse as the key issue (Section 4.3, Figure 2). This provides actionable insights for the community.
    *   The mechanistic analysis reveals that the critic is fundamentally miscalibrated, exhibiting negative correlation with correctness (AUROC < 0.5), which is a significant insight for the community (Section 4.4, Table 2). This highlights a critical bottleneck in long-horizon RLVR.
    *   The analysis of entropy dynamics highlights the trade-off between preventing collapse and causing explosion, providing valuable practical insights (Section 4.3, Figure 2a). This aids future engineering efforts in stabilizing training.
*   **Nuanced Reporting of Results**
    *   The authors honestly report that while the method fails on average, it shows promise on stable runs, avoiding a binary "success/failure" narrative (Section 4.2). This maintains scientific honesty and guides realistic expectations.
    *   The discussion clearly separates fundamental flaws from engineering issues (e.g., entropy hyperparameter sensitivity), guiding future work effectively (Section 5). This improves the clarity and utility of the paper's conclusions.3) Weaknesses
*   **Limited Model Scale and Generalizability**
    *   The experiments are conducted solely on a 1.5B parameter model, which may lack the representational capacity for accurate value estimation in complex reasoning tasks, limiting the generalizability of the findings (Section 4.1, Section 5).
    *   With only two random seeds per condition, the statistical power is limited, and the results may be sensitive to initialization (Section 4.1, Section 5). This reduces the robustness of the average performance claims.
    *   The study does not test the method on larger models (e.g., 7B or 14B) where critic calibration might behave differently, leaving open the question of whether the failure mode persists at scale (Section 5).
*   **Unresolved Critic Training Strategy**
    *   The manuscript contains a direct contradiction regarding critic supervision: Section 3.4 states truncated rollouts are included in the critic loss at 10% weight, whereas Section 5 claims they were excluded to avoid self-fulfilling targets. This inconsistency undermines the mechanistic analysis of critic collapse (Section 4.3, Figure 2c).
    *   The proposed "delayed bootstrapping" (waiting 10 steps) is a heuristic fix that is not ablated to determine its optimal duration or necessity (Section 3.4). This limits the reproducibility and optimality of the implementation.
    *   The paper suggests critic pre-training as a future direction but does not implement it, leaving the primary failure mode (critic collapse) unaddressed in the experimental results (Section 5).
*   **Entropy Instability and Hyperparameter Sensitivity**
    *   There is an inconsistency in applying the pre-registered collapse criteria: Section 4.1 defines collapse strictly (e.g., entropy below 0.2 nats for 200 steps), Section 4.2 reports a 1/2 collapse incidence based on divergence, and Section 5 admits the criteria could not formally trigger within the 100-step horizon. This ambiguity affects the reliability of the stability metrics.
    *   The reliance on a fixed entropy bonus coefficient (0.001) leads to divergence in one seed, indicating high sensitivity to hyperparameters (Section 4.3, Figure 2a). This demonstrates a lack of robustness in the proposed method.
    *   The paper mentions EntroPIC as a potential solution but does not integrate it, leaving the entropy control mechanism as a weak point in the implementation (Section 2, Section 5).
*   **Lack of Ablation on Critic Miscalibration Causes**
    *   While the paper identifies negative correlation between critic values and correctness, it does not ablate potential causes (e.g., reward sparsity, horizon length, or model capacity) to pinpoint the root of the miscalibration (Section 4.4).
    *   The analysis does not explore whether the critic is failing due to the specific nature of mathematical reasoning or if this is a general issue with value estimation in long-horizon LLM training (Section 5).
    *   The paper does not test auxiliary objectives or intermediate supervision signals that could mitigate the miscalibration, leaving the diagnostic findings somewhat isolated from potential remedies (Section 5).4) Suggestions for Improvement
*   **Expand Model Scale and Seed Diversity**
    *   Conduct experiments on larger models (e.g., 7B or 14B) to assess whether critic calibration improves with increased representational capacity.
    *   Increase the number of random seeds (e.g., to 5 or more) to provide more robust statistical estimates and better characterize the failure rate.
    *   Test the method on different reasoning domains (e.g., coding or logical reasoning) to evaluate generalizability beyond mathematics.
*   **Clarify and Experiment with Critic Training Strategies**
    *   Explicitly reconcile the contradiction between Section 3.4 and Section 5 regarding whether truncated rollouts were included in the critic loss, and clarify how this impacts the observed collapse in Figure 2c.
    *   Ablate the "delayed bootstrapping" period to determine the optimal duration and whether it is strictly necessary for stability.
    *   Implement critic pre-training on completed rollouts before RL begins, as suggested in the discussion, to provide better initial value estimates.
*   **Standardize Collapse Metrics and Adopt Robust Entropy Control**
    *   Reconcile the pre-registered collapse definition (Section 4.1) with the reported incidence (Section 4.2) and limitations (Section 5) to ensure metric consistency and transparency.
    *   Integrate a more sophisticated entropy control mechanism, such as EntroPIC (Yang et al., 2025), to stabilize training without manual hyperparameter tuning.
    *   Perform a sensitivity analysis on the entropy coefficient to identify a safe range that prevents both collapse and explosion.
*   **Deepen Analysis of Critic Miscalibration**
    *   Conduct ablation studies to isolate the causes of critic miscalibration (e.g., varying horizon length, reward sparsity, or model size).
    *   Analyze the critic's value estimates on different types of reasoning errors (e.g., calculation errors vs. logical errors) to understand what features the critic is learning.
    *   Explore auxiliary objectives for the critic, such as predicting intermediate step correctness, to improve calibration on long horizons.5) Score
- Overall (10): 5 — The paper provides valuable mechanistic insights into RLVR truncation handling, but internal contradictions in experimental reporting and unresolved stability issues limit its immediate impact.
- Novelty (10): 7 — Applying classical RL timeout theory to LLM truncation is conceptually sound, though the core bootstrapping mechanism is an adaptation of standard RL techniques.
- Technical Quality (10): 5 — The experimental design is rigorous, but the contradiction between Sections 3.4 and 5 regarding critic training, alongside inconsistent collapse criteria application, reduces technical soundness.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The paper is well-structured and transparent about limitations, though the conflicting statements on critic supervision require careful reading to reconcile.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence based on clear presentation of methods, results, and the identifiable internal inconsistencies that were verified against the manuscript text.
