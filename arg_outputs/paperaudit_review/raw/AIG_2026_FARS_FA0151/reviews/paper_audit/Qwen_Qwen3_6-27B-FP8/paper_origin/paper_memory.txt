# Global Summary

The paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in continual learning for Large Language Models (LLMs) when using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The authors propose MidPC (Middle Principal Components), a method that initializes LoRA adapters using intermediate singular value decomposition (SVD) slices rather than the top (PiSSA) or bottom (MiLoRA) components. The hypothesis is that the middle spectral region offers a superior stability-plasticity trade-off.

Evaluation is conducted on the FOREVER Standard CL benchmark using the Qwen3-0.6B model. MidPC achieves an Overall Performance (OP) of 84.8% and a Backward Transfer (BWT) of -1.7%. This significantly outperforms PiSSA (66.7% OP, -15.0% BWT) and MiLoRA (81.6% OP, -5.0% BWT). Spectral analysis indicates that MidPC maintains stable subspace alignment and moderate spectral imbalance. Learning rate matching experiments confirm that 91.9% of the performance advantage over MiLoRA is a genuine spectral effect rather than a conditioning artifact. The authors note the paper was generated by an automated research system.

# Abstract

- Problem: Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning with LoRA.
- Method: MidPC initializes LoRA adapters from intermediate singular components.
- Key Results (FOREVER Standard CL, Qwen3-0.6B):
  - MidPC: 84.8% OP, -1.7% BWT.
  - PiSSA: 66.7% OP, -15.0% BWT.
  - MiLoRA: 81.6% OP, -5.0% BWT.
- Analysis: MidPC maintains stable subspace alignment and moderate spectral imbalance.
- Verification: 91.9% of the advantage over MiLoRA is retained after learning rate matching.
- Caveat: The paper includes a warning that it was generated by an automated research system.

# Preliminaries

- Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA): Introduces trainable low-rank matrices $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}$ to frozen pretrained weights $W$. The effective weight is $W' = W + \frac{\alpha}{r}BA$. Standard LoRA initializes $A$ with random Gaussian values and $B$ with zeros.
- SVD-based Initialization: Based on $W = U\Sigma V^\top$.
  - PiSSA: Uses top $r$ singular components (largest singular values).
  - MiLoRA: Uses bottom $r$ singular components (smallest singular values).
- Motivation: Top slices (PiSSA) risk damaging shared representations. Bottom slices (MiLoRA) represent poorly-conditioned directions that may become unstable. Intermediate components are hypothesized to balance stability and plasticity.

# Method

- MidPC Formulation: Initializes LoRA adapters from an intermediate slice of the singular spectrum defined by start index $s$ and rank $r$.
- Slice Definition: $\Delta W_s = U_{s:s+r} \Sigma_{s:s+r} V_{s:s+r}^\top$.
- Initialization: LoRA factors are initialized to preserve original model behavior ($W_p + \frac{\alpha}{r} BA = W$).
  - $A = \sqrt{\frac{r}{\alpha}} \Sigma_{s:s+r}^{1/2} V_{s:s+r}^\top$
  - $B = \sqrt{\frac{r}{\alpha}} U_{s:s+r} \Sigma_{s:s+r}^{1/2}$
- Unified Framework:
  - PiSSA corresponds to $s = 0$.
  - MiLoRA corresponds to $s = r_{\max} - r$.
  - MidPC uses $s = \lfloor r_{\max}/2 \rfloor$.
- Intuition: Intermediate slices have moderate variance, avoiding the high sensitivity of top components and the instability of bottom components.

# Experiments

- Setup:
  - Benchmark: FOREVER Standard CL (5 sequential tasks: Yelp Reviews, AG News, Amazon Reviews, DBpedia, Yahoo Answers).
  - Model: Qwen3-0.6B.
  - LoRA Config: Rank $r = 8$, scaling factor $\alpha = 32$, dropout 0.05.
  - MidPC Setting: $s = 508$ (middle of 1024-dimensional spectrum).
  - Training: 10 epochs per task, learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$, AdamW optimizer, cosine schedule.
  - Metrics: Overall Performance (OP, average final accuracy) and Backward Transfer (BWT, average change in earlier task performance).
  - Runs: Averaged over 2 task orders and 3 random seeds (6 runs total).

- Main Results (Table 1):
  - MidPC: OP 84.8% ($\pm 1.3$), BWT -1.7% ($\pm 2.0$).
  - MiLoRA: OP 81.6% ($\pm 2.7$), BWT -5.0% ($\pm 2.5$).
  - PiSSA: OP 66.7% ($\pm 4.9$), BWT -15.0% ($\pm 6.5$).
  - Comparison: MidPC improves OP by 18.1 points over PiSSA and 3.2 points over MiLoRA.

- Spectral Analysis (Figure 2):
  - Subspace Alignment: MidPC maintains high alignment (0.995 to 0.974, drop of 0.021). PiSSA shows significant degradation (0.943 to 0.845, drop of 0.098).
  - Spectral Imbalance: MidPC achieves moderate imbalance (~0.32). MiLoRA has high imbalance (~0.72). PiSSA has low imbalance (~0.25).

- Learning Rate Matching (Table 2):
  - Purpose: Verify if improvements are due to spectral properties or conditioning differences.
  - Result: 91.9% of MidPC's BWT advantage over MiLoRA is retained after matching effective learning rates. 35.2% retained over PiSSA.

- Robustness (Table 3):
  - MidPC shows consistent BWT of -1.7% across two different task orders.
  - MiLoRA BWT varies from -7.2% to -2.8%.
  - PiSSA BWT varies from -12.8% to -17.2%.

- Implementation Details (Appendix A):
  - Library: PEFT.
  - Hardware: NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
  - Precision: bfloat16.
  - Runtime: Approximately 70 minutes per 5-task sequence run.
