1) Summary
The paper addresses context window saturation and unproductive looping in LLM-based terminal agents during long-horizon CLI tasks. It introduces LASCon, a training-free scaffold comprising a Deterministic CLI Condenser (DCC) for rule-based observation compression and a Progress-Conditioned Loop Controller (PLC) for action-space shaping. DCC extracts critical CLI tokens (commands, exit codes, error lines) while offloading full logs, and maintains a structured scratchpad. PLC monitors action-error-state triples to block repetitive commands that fail to change environment state. Evaluated on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with Qwen3-32B, LASCon achieves a 68.8% completion rate (+21.2pp over baseline) and eliminates timeout failures. Ablations indicate DCC is the primary performance driver, while PLC acts as a conservative safety net with minimal intervention. The work highlights the efficacy of deterministic context management over LLM-based summarization for CLI environments.2) Strengths
- **Effective Deterministic Context Management**
  - Evidence: Section 3.2 and Table 1 show DCC-Only achieves 70.0% completion, substantially outperforming the LLM-based baseline (47.5%). Why it matters: Demonstrates technical soundness by proving that rule-based, domain-specific compression reliably preserves operationally critical CLI tokens without generative hallucination.
  - Evidence: Figure 1 illustrates the structured extraction pipeline (command, exit code, CWD, error lines, head/tail outputs) and scratchpad maintenance. Why it matters: Enhances clarity and reproducibility by providing a transparent, auditable data flow that replaces opaque LLM summarization.
  - Evidence: Section 4.3 confirms DCC eliminates all timeout failures (0 vs 32 in baseline B). Why it matters: Validates experimental rigor by showing that compact context representation directly resolves a primary failure mode in long-horizon agent trajectories.
- **Conservative and Safe Loop Control Mechanism**
  - Evidence: Section 3.3 details the PLC's use of canonicalized action/error signatures and SHA-256 state versioning to detect unproductive repetition. Why it matters: Strengthens technical soundness by grounding loop detection in deterministic environment fingerprints rather than heuristic token matching.
  - Evidence: Table 2 reports a 0.12% block rate with zero deadlock events across 806 proposed actions. Why it matters: Confirms impact and safety by proving the intervention prevents over-blocking while maintaining a recovery-oriented safety net.
  - Evidence: Section 4.5 notes that blocked entries expire after 20 steps, allowing re-attempts with warnings. Why it matters: Improves practical utility by balancing exploration enforcement with agent autonomy, avoiding rigid termination policies common in prior work.
- **Transparent and Rigorous Ablation Design**
  - Evidence: Table 1 systematically compares five conditions (Default, +Loop Prompt, Full, DCC-Only, PLC-Only) to isolate component contributions. Why it matters: Enhances experimental rigor by clearly attributing performance gains to specific architectural modules rather than conflating effects.
  - Evidence: Section 4.4 explicitly quantifies loop-induced failures (2.5% in baseline) and demonstrates that a simple system prompt eliminates them. Why it matters: Strengthens novelty assessment by honestly contextualizing the PLC's role as a secondary safety mechanism rather than a primary driver.
  - Evidence: Section 4.3 openly acknowledges the context window confound (40K vs 131K) between conditions. Why it matters: Improves clarity and scientific integrity by transparently reporting experimental limitations that could otherwise mislead interpretation.3) Weaknesses
- **Confounding Context Window Variations in Ablations**
  - Evidence: Section 4.1 states condition C (Full) used a 40K token limit while ablation variants used 131K. Why it matters: Undermines experimental rigor by preventing fair comparison of component synergy and obscuring whether the full system's lower score stems from architectural inefficiency or arbitrary constraints.
  - Evidence: Table 1 shows PLC-Only achieves 75.0% completion, but Section 4.3 admits this is confounded by the larger context budget. Why it matters: Limits technical soundness because the reported ablation hierarchy cannot be reliably used to guide system design or resource allocation.
  - Evidence: No direct evidence found in the manuscript regarding how the 40K limit was selected or whether it artificially constrains the full system's potential. Why it matters: Reduces impact by leaving readers uncertain about the true performance ceiling of the proposed scaffold under standardized conditions.
- **Limited Generalization and Model Evaluation**
  - Evidence: Section 4.1 specifies evaluation only on Qwen3-32B with temperature 0. Why it matters: Restricts novelty and impact by failing to verify whether deterministic condensation rules transfer to models with different reasoning patterns or tool-use interfaces.
  - Evidence: Section 5 (Limitations) explicitly states generalization to other models remains untested. Why it matters: Weakens technical soundness because regex-based error extraction and loop thresholds may require recalibration for different LLM families or decoding strategies.
  - Evidence: No direct evidence found in the manuscript regarding performance on other agent frameworks beyond OpenHands or other CLI benchmarks beyond Terminal-Bench 2.0. Why it matters: Limits practical impact by leaving interoperability and cross-benchmark robustness unverified, reducing confidence in broad deployment.
- **Evaluation Rigor and Reporting Inconsistencies**
  - Evidence: Section 4.1 notes Docker unavailability prevented Pass@1 evaluation with automated test verification. Why it matters: Undermines experimental rigor because completion rate alone may mask partial successes, incorrect solutions, or false positives in task verification.
  - Evidence: Section 4.1 cites "Qwen3-32B (Yang et al., 2024b)", but the reference list identifies citation 2024b as the "Qwen2.5 technical report". Why it matters: Reduces technical soundness by introducing factual ambiguity regarding the exact model architecture and capabilities used in the experiments.
  - Evidence: The Abstract and Section 4.2 claim a "+21.2pp" improvement, but Table 1 data (68.8% − 47.5%) yields 21.3pp. Additionally, Table 1 shows PLC-Only still suffers 19 timeouts despite the module's design goal. Why it matters: Limits clarity and impact by suggesting carelessness in reporting and obscuring the true efficacy of the loop controller under standardized conditions.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Standardize Context Windows Across All Conditions**
  - Conduct a controlled experiment where all conditions (Full, DCC-Only, PLC-Only) operate under identical context limits (e.g., 40K or 131K) to isolate architectural effects from budget constraints.
  - Report token utilization efficiency (e.g., tokens per successful step or compression ratio) across standardized windows to quantify the true information density benefit of DCC.
  - Provide a sensitivity analysis showing how completion rates scale with context window size for each configuration, clarifying the trade-off between compression and context availability.
- **Expand Evaluation Across Models and Frameworks**
  - Test LASCon on at least two additional LLM backends (e.g., Llama-3-70B, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) to verify the robustness of deterministic condensation across different reasoning capabilities.
  - Evaluate the system on alternative agent frameworks (e.g., SWE-agent, AutoGen) to demonstrate interoperability and validate the drop-in deployment claim beyond OpenHands.
  - Include cross-benchmark validation using a secondary CLI or code-execution dataset to assess the transferability of the DCC and PLC modules to diverse task distributions.
- **Enhance Evaluation Rigor and Correct Reporting Details**
  - Resolve Docker infrastructure constraints to enable automated Pass@1 verification, providing a more reliable measure of task success than self-reported completion status.
  - Correct the model citation mismatch in Section 4.1 to accurately reflect the base model version, ensuring reproducibility and precise attribution of performance characteristics.
  - Reconcile the numerical discrepancy in the reported improvement (+21.2pp vs. 21.3pp) and provide a qualitative breakdown of the 19 timeouts in the PLC-Only condition to clarify the module's actual impact on timeout mitigation.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper presents a practical and well-motivated scaffold for terminal agents, but experimental confounds (Section 4.1) and unverified completion metrics (Section 4.1) temper its broader impact.
- Novelty (10): 6 — The combination of deterministic CLI condensation and conservative loop control is incremental, building on established masking and stuck-detection paradigms (Section 2, Section 3.2).
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The method is clearly specified and ablated (Table 1, Section 4.3), though the context window mismatch, lack of Pass@1 verification (Section 4.1), and citation inconsistencies reduce experimental rigor.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear architectural diagrams (Figure 1) and transparent reporting of limitations (Section 5), facilitating easy comprehension.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence in the reported results and ablations (Table 1, Table 2), though the context confound, single-model evaluation, and reporting discrepancies slightly limit certainty about generalizability.
