1) Summary
The paper addresses the vulnerability of LLM agent memory stores to paraphrase re-injection attacks following naive ID-based deletion of poisoned content. The authors propose Hazard-Signature Tombstones (HST), a commit-time forget lockout policy that extracts discrete hazard signatures from deleted records and blocks future writes exhibiting fuzzy set-containment with these signatures. Evaluated on a paraphrase re-injection benchmark using the MemoryGraft corpus, HST reduces the poisoned retrieval proportion to 0.0 while maintaining perfect benign recall, outperforming retrieval-time filtering baselines that suffer from index pollution and slot wasting. The method demonstrates high hazard-signature stability (92%) under paraphrase compared to exact matching (30%), effectively preventing semantic reformulation attacks at the commit stage.2) Strengths
- **Clear Problem Formulation and Motivation**
  - The paper explicitly quantifies the paraphrase re-injection threat by demonstrating that naive ID-deletion increases poisoned retrieval from 0.67 to 0.94 (Section 1, Table 1), which strongly motivates the need for a semantic defense mechanism.
  - The distinction between commit-time and retrieval-time filtering is rigorously articulated, highlighting the "slot wasting" phenomenon where poisoned entries crowd retrieval neighborhoods even when filtered at query time (Section 1, Figure 2).
  - The formal problem statement in Section 3.1 precisely defines the memory store, forget requests, and adversary capabilities, providing a mathematically sound foundation that clarifies the threat model and evaluation boundaries.
- **Effective and Interpretable Method Design**
  - The use of discrete hazard signatures (Equation 1) instead of continuous embeddings offers high interpretability, allowing operators to trace blocked writes to specific safety-relevant labels rather than opaque vector distances (Section 3.2).
  - The fuzzy set-containment matching criterion (Equation 3) elegantly handles classification variance under paraphrase by tolerating subset/superset relationships, which empirically boosts signature stability to 92% and ensures robust blocking (Section 3.5, Table 1).
  - The commit-time lockout mechanism (Figure 1) prevents index pollution entirely, preserving retrieval capacity for benign content without requiring complex backfill strategies or post-retrieval filtering pipelines (Section 3.4, Figure 2).
- **Comprehensive and Rigorous Experimental Evaluation**
  - The evaluation includes multiple strong baselines (No-Forget, ID-Delete, Retrieval-Time Exact/Fuzzy) and clearly demonstrates HST's superiority across all key metrics, establishing a strong empirical baseline for future work (Table 1).
  - The backfill ablation study (Figure 2) quantitatively proves the inefficiency of retrieval-time filtering, showing that even aggressive backfill recovers only 1.0 results/query compared to HST's 3.0, validating the architectural choice.
  - The embedding ablation in Appendix A (Table 2) confirms that HST's performance is retrieval-backend-agnostic, validating the method's robustness across different vector models and strengthening generalizability claims.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Scope of Hazard Taxonomy and Generalizability**
  - The fixed hazard taxonomy (Equation 1) contains only five categories tailored to data analysis agents, which may not cover diverse safety risks in other domains like healthcare, finance, or creative coding (Section 3.2).
  - The paper acknowledges this limitation but provides no empirical analysis or protocol for adapting the taxonomy to new domains, leaving cross-domain generalizability unverified and potentially limiting real-world adoption (Section 5).
  - No discussion is provided on how the taxonomy handles evolving threat landscapes or novel attack vectors that fall outside the predefined labels, raising concerns about long-term maintenance and scalability (Section 3.2, Section 5).
- **Computational Overhead and Latency Concerns**
  - HST requires an LLM call for hazard signature extraction on every incoming write, which introduces significant latency and cost overhead for high-throughput memory systems operating at scale (Section 5).
  - The manuscript does not report inference times, throughput benchmarks, or cost analysis for the signature extraction step, making it difficult to assess practical deployment feasibility in production environments (Section 4.1, Section 5).
  - No optimization strategies (e.g., caching, lightweight classifiers, or batch processing) are proposed or evaluated to mitigate the per-write LLM dependency, leaving a critical engineering gap unaddressed (Section 3.2, Section 5).
- **Evaluation Constraints and Adversary Assumptions**
  - The paraphrase generation uses deterministic decoding (temperature=0) with a single model (DeepSeek-V3.2), which may not reflect the diversity of real-world adversarial paraphrasing techniques or multi-model evasion strategies (Section 4.1).
  - The attack simulation assumes a static set of 50 paraphrases generated offline, rather than modeling adaptive, multi-turn adversaries that iteratively refine inputs based on rejection feedback to evade detection (Section 4.1, Section 5).
  - The false positive rate (3%) is reported as acceptable, but the paper lacks a detailed analysis of which benign records are incorrectly blocked and whether these false positives degrade critical agent functionalities or user experience (Table 1, Section 4.4).4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Taxonomy Adaptability and Cross-Domain Validation**
  - Introduce a protocol or few-shot prompting strategy to dynamically extend the hazard taxonomy based on domain-specific safety guidelines, and evaluate its performance on at least one additional agent domain to verify transferability.
  - Provide a sensitivity analysis showing how the false positive rate and blocking efficacy change when the taxonomy size is expanded or contracted, to guide practitioners in label selection and threshold tuning.
  - Discuss potential integration with existing safety taxonomies or standardized risk categories to demonstrate how HST can leverage community-driven hazard definitions rather than relying on a fixed, narrow set.
- **Quantify Overhead and Propose Efficiency Optimizations**
  - Report detailed latency and throughput metrics for the hazard signature extraction step under varying write rates, comparing the overhead against the baseline memory insertion pipeline to establish practical deployment bounds.
  - Evaluate lightweight alternatives for signature extraction, such as fine-tuned small language models or rule-based heuristics, and compare their stability and false positive rates against the full LLM approach to identify cost-effective trade-offs.
  - Implement and benchmark a caching mechanism for frequently occurring hazard signatures or semantic clusters to reduce redundant LLM calls during high-volume write operations, directly addressing the stated latency concern.
- **Strengthen Adversarial Robustness and False Positive Analysis**
  - Extend the paraphrase generation to include multiple LLMs, varied temperature settings, and iterative refinement loops to simulate more realistic and adaptive adversarial behavior, ensuring the defense holds under diverse evasion tactics.
  - Conduct a multi-turn evasion experiment where the adversary observes rejection feedback and modifies subsequent write attempts, measuring HST's resilience against incremental semantic drift and adaptive learning.
  - Provide a qualitative breakdown of the 3% false positives, categorizing the blocked benign records by intent and analyzing whether these blocks degrade specific agent capabilities, thereby grounding the acceptability claim in empirical impact analysis.5) Score
- Overall (10): 7 — The paper presents a well-motivated and effective commit-time defense against paraphrase re-injection, though practical deployment concerns regarding latency and taxonomy generalizability require further validation (Sections 3, 4, 5).
- Novelty (10): 8 — The adaptation of distributed tombstone concepts to semantic hazard signatures with fuzzy set-containment matching offers a fresh perspective on memory unlearning and security (Sections 2, 3.3, 3.5).
- Technical Quality (10): 7 — The method is mathematically sound and empirically validated across multiple baselines and embedding backends, but lacks latency benchmarks and adaptive adversary testing (Sections 3, 4, Appendix A).
- Clarity (10): 9 — The manuscript is exceptionally well-structured, with clear problem formulation, intuitive system diagrams, and precise metric definitions that facilitate easy comprehension (Sections 1, 3.1, Figure 1).
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence in the evaluation of the proposed method against static paraphrase attacks, though lower confidence regarding real-world deployment feasibility due to missing overhead analysis (Sections 4, 5).
