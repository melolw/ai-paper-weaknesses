# Global Summary

This paper addresses the vulnerability of long-context language models to "hard distractors"—semantically similar but misleading passages that cause systematic errors. The authors argue that standard self-consistency fails in this setting because errors are context-driven rather than decoding-driven, leading to correlated wrong answers. They propose Context Bagging (CoBag), an inference-time ensembling method that allocates compute to context diversity (via subset sampling and order shuffling) rather than decoding diversity. Evaluated on a noisy split of MuSiQue (2,314 examples) using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, CoBag significantly outperforms self-consistency (+3.12 EM, $p < 0.001$). However, ablation studies reveal that order diversity is the dominant mechanism (+1.44 EM), while subset diversity provides only marginal benefit (+0.40 EM), suggesting simple order shuffling may be sufficient for many applications.

# Abstract

- **Problem:** Long-context models struggle with hard distractors. Self-consistency fails because all samples converge to the same wrong answer due to context-driven errors.
- **Method:** Context Bagging (CoBag) samples $K$ diverse context subsets using relevance-weighted selection, randomly permutes paragraph order, generates answers via greedy decoding, and aggregates via majority voting.
- **Results:** On MuSiQue with hard distractors, CoBag outperforms self-consistency by +3.12 EM ($p < 0.001$).
- **Ablation:** Order diversity is the dominant mechanism (+1.44 EM), while subset diversity provides marginal additional benefit (+0.40 EM).

# Method

- **Problem Setup:** The task is long-context QA with a question $q$ and retrieved paragraphs $P$. Hard distractors are defined as paragraphs semantically similar to the question/evidence but leading to incorrect answers. These distractors rank highly by relevance and survive standard filtering.
- **Why Self-Consistency Fails:** Self-consistency samples multiple decoding trajectories from the same context. Diagnostic analysis shows self-consistency has an agreement rate of 0.85 and wrong-answer concentration of 0.82, indicating correlated errors.
- **CoBag Procedure:**
    - **Step 1: Relevance Scoring:** Computes relevance score $s_i$ using cosine similarity between embeddings (BGE-M3 model).
    - **Step 2: Subset Sampling:** For $K$ ensemble members, constructs an $m$-paragraph context. Always includes top-$r$ paragraphs (core set). Samples remaining $m-r$ paragraphs with probability proportional to $\exp(s_i/\tau)$.
    - **Step 3: Order Shuffling:** Randomly permutes the order of the $m$ selected paragraphs to mitigate positional bias.
    - **Step 4: Majority Voting:** Generates answers via greedy decoding ($T=0$). Aggregates via majority vote. Ties are broken by selecting the answer from the context with the highest mean relevance score.
- **Design Rationale:** The core set ensures critical evidence is retained. Order shuffling varies positional context. Greedy decoding ensures improvements stem from context diversity, not output entropy. Hard distractors in the top-$r$ are included in 100% of contexts, proving gains are not due to distractor exclusion.

# Experiments

- **Dataset:** MuSiQue noisy split with 2,314 examples. Each example contains 20 paragraphs. Hard distractors are the highest-scoring non-supporting paragraphs (top-4 by relevance) placed at the end of the sequence to induce recency bias.
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- **Baselines:**
    - Filtered-Single: Deterministic top-$m$ selection, greedy decoding.
    - Self-Consistency: Fixed context, $K$ temperature-sampled trajectories ($T=1.0$).
    - Permute-Vote: Fixed top-$m$ paragraphs, $K$ random order permutations, greedy decoding.
- **Hyperparameters:** Default $K=5$, $m=12$, $r=6$, $\tau=1.0$.
- **Main Results:**
    - CoBag-Vote achieves EM 0.2939.
    - Outperforms Filtered-Single (+3.03 EM, $p=0.0002$) and Self-Consistency (+3.12 EM, $p=0.0001$).
    - Does not significantly outperform Permute-Vote (+0.39 EM, $p=0.641$).
    - Hard distractor is present in 100% of CoBag contexts (11,570/11,570).
- **Ablation Study:**
    - Order shuffling contributes +1.44 EM (CoBag-Vote vs. CoBag-NoShuffle).
    - Relevance-weighted sampling contributes +0.40 EM (CoBag-Vote vs. CoBag-Uniform).
    - CoBag-NoShuffle (subset diversity only) underperforms Permute-Vote by -2.44 EM.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:**
    - Context size $m$: Performance improves monotonically from $m=8$ (EM 0.2615) to $m=16$ (EM 0.2982).
    - Ensemble size $K$: Diminishing returns observed. $K=3 \rightarrow 5$ yields +2.42 EM; $K=5 \rightarrow 7$ yields +0.17 EM.
- **Diagnostic Analysis:**
    - CoBag-Vote reduces wrong-answer concentration to 0.64 (vs. 0.82 for Self-Consistency), indicating more diverse error patterns that enable effective majority voting.
