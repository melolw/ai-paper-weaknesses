1) Summary
The paper addresses the computational bottleneck of LongCodeZip, a state-of-the-art code context compression method that relies on full forward passes for perplexity-based relevance scoring. The authors propose ShallowPPL, an early-exit approach that utilizes the logit lens technique to approximate relevance scores at intermediate transformer layers ($L < N$). Evaluated on Long Code Completion and RepoQA benchmarks against pre-registered success criteria ($\ge$1.5$\times$ speedup, $\le$1.0 point quality drop), the study demonstrates that the hypothesis does not hold: ShallowPPL achieves only 1.05$\times$ speedup with significant quality degradation. Ablation studies reveal that coarse function ranking is the primary bottleneck and that quality scales nonlinearly with depth, with the final layers encoding critical information not present in intermediate representations.2) Strengths
*   **Rigorous Evaluation with Pre-Registered Criteria**
    *   The authors establish clear success criteria before evaluation ($\ge$1.5$\times$ speedup, $\le$1.0 point quality drop), which is a high standard for experimental rigor (Section 3.4).
    *   The paper transparently reports a negative result, explicitly stating that the hypothesis does not hold, which saves the community from pursuing a dead end (Abstract, Section 4.2).
    *   The evaluation covers two distinct benchmarks (Long Code Completion and RepoQA) and multiple programming languages, ensuring robustness (Section 4.1, Table 2).
*   **Deep Diagnostic Insights via Ablation**
    *   The ablation study effectively isolates the source of failure, identifying coarse function ranking as the primary bottleneck rather than fine-grained scoring (Section 4.3, Table 3).
    *   The discovery of nonlinear quality scaling with depth is a significant finding; the paper quantifies that the last two layers contribute 3.83 percentage points of accuracy on RepoQA (Section 4.3, Table 4).
    *   The hybrid configuration analysis provides actionable insight, showing that retaining full-depth scoring for coarse ranking recovers 81% of the quality gap (Section 4.3, Table 3).
*   **Clear Methodological Comparison**
    *   Figure 1 provides a clear visual comparison between the LongCodeZip baseline and the proposed ShallowPPL pipeline, making the architectural changes easy to understand.
    *   The method clearly defines the logit lens projection process, including the application of final layer normalization to reduce representational drift (Section 3.2).
    *   The comparison against a RAG baseline provides context, showing that even the degraded ShallowPPL outperforms embedding-based retrieval, highlighting the value of perplexity-based scoring (Section 4.2, Table 1).3) Weaknesses
*   **Superficial Analysis of Speedup Limitations**
    *   The claim that "per-forward-pass overhead" limits speedup is asserted but not empirically verified with profiling data (Section 4.4).
    *   The paper mentions "embedding lookup, attention setup, and KV cache initialization" as fixed costs but does not provide a breakdown of time spent on each component to support this conclusion (Section 4.4).
    *   Without a detailed profiling analysis, the conclusion that alternative approaches like KV cache reuse are "more promising" remains speculative rather than evidence-based (Section 5).
*   **Limited Scope of Early-Exit Techniques**
    *   The method only tests the standard logit lens (projection via unembedding matrix) and does not explore other early-exit strategies mentioned in related work, such as the "tuned lens" or learned adapters (Section 3.2, Related Work Section 2.3).
    *   The paper does not investigate whether different normalization strategies or layer selection heuristics could improve the intermediate representations' quality (Section 3.2).
    *   By focusing solely on perplexity approximation, the paper misses the opportunity to explore other intermediate-layer signals (e.g., attention patterns) that might be more robust for ranking (Section 2.3).
*   **Lack of Generalizability Across Models**
    *   All experiments are conducted on a single model (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct), raising questions about whether these findings generalize to other architectures or model sizes (Section 4.1).
    *   The nonlinear depth dependency might vary across different model families; testing only one model limits the broader applicability of the conclusion (Section 4.1).
    *   There is no discussion on how the number of layers in the model (28 layers) specifically impacts the feasibility of early exit compared to deeper or shallower models (Section 4.1).4) Suggestions for Improvement
*   **Provide Detailed Profiling Data**
    *   Include a breakdown of inference time components (e.g., embedding, attention, projection, overhead) to empirically support the claim that fixed costs dominate speedup limitations.
    *   Add a table or figure showing the time distribution for full-depth vs. early-exit passes to quantify the "per-forward-pass overhead."
    *   Discuss specific implementation details (e.g., batch sizes, KV cache management) that might influence these overhead costs.
*   **Expand Early-Exit Methodology**
    *   Investigate the "tuned lens" approach (Belrose et al., 2023) mentioned in related work to see if learned projections yield better intermediate representations than the standard unembedding matrix.
    *   Explore alternative early-exit criteria, such as using attention scores or hidden state norms, to determine if perplexity approximation is the limiting factor.
    *   Test different normalization strategies for intermediate hidden states to see if they reduce representational drift more effectively than the final layer normalization.
*   **Assess Generalizability and Baseline Context**
    *   Conduct a small-scale ablation on a different model family or size to verify if the nonlinear depth dependency and speedup limitations hold generally.
    *   Discuss the "quality per second" metric more deeply, comparing ShallowPPL not just to LongCodeZip but to the RAG baseline to determine if there is any practical regime where ShallowPPL is preferable.
    *   Clarify the impact of model depth (28 layers) on the results and speculate on how these findings might translate to models with significantly different depths.5) Score
*   Overall (10): 5 — The paper provides a rigorous negative result with valuable diagnostic insights, but the analysis of speedup limitations is superficial and the scope is limited to a single model.
*   Novelty (10): 4 — Applying logit lens to code compression is a natural extension, and the novelty lies primarily in the rigorous falsification of the hypothesis rather than a new method.
*   Technical Quality (10): 6 — The experimental setup is strong with pre-registered criteria, but the lack of profiling data to support the speedup analysis weakens the technical depth.
*   Clarity (10): 8 — The paper is well-written, with clear figures and a logical structure that effectively communicates the negative result and ablation findings.
*   Confidence (5): 4 — The reviewer is confident in the assessment based on the clear presentation of methods, results, and ablation studies.
