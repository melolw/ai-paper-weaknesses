1) Summary
- The manuscript addresses permutation sensitivity in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems, where document ordering induces position bias and output inconsistency. The authors propose Citation-Consistent Voting (CCV), a training-free inference-time method that aggregates responses by mapping citations to permutation-invariant document IDs and selecting answers with the highest citation agreement among evidence-valid outputs. Core contributions include a three-stage pipeline (permutation sampling, evidence filtering, citation-consistent aggregation), empirical validation showing monotonic performance scaling with permutation count, and diagnostic analysis confirming that citation consistency significantly correlates with answer correctness. On NaturalQuestions with Qwen3-8B and Contriever retrieval, CCV achieves 46.37% SubEM at K=20 permutations, outperforming majority voting by +0.19 points.2) Strengths
- **Novel Aggregation Paradigm Leveraging Citation Structure**
  - Shifts the voting signal from answer frequency to document-ID agreement (Section 3.3, Equations 1–2), which matters for novelty by introducing a grounding-aware aggregation mechanism that directly addresses the limitations of frequency-based self-consistency.
  - Introduces permutation-invariant document mapping to align citations across different orderings (Section 3.3, Figure 1), which matters for technical soundness by ensuring the aggregation logic remains robust regardless of retrieval position bias.
  - Formalizes evidence validity filtering through quote grounding and index validation (Section 3.2), which matters for experimental rigor by preventing hallucinated or ungrounded generations from contaminating the voting pool.
- **Rigorous and Scalable Experimental Validation**
  - Demonstrates monotonic performance scaling with the number of permutations K (Table 2, Figure 2), which matters for experimental rigor by providing strong empirical evidence that the citation signal becomes more reliable with increased sampling.
  - Includes a targeted ablation on quote constraint relaxation (Table 3, Section 4.4), which matters for clarity and technical soundness by isolating the primary driver of performance gains and confirming that document-ID agreement outweighs strict verbatim verification.
  - Conducts diagnostic analysis linking citation agreement scores to prediction accuracy (Section 4.5, p = 1.14 × 10⁻⁵), which matters for impact by offering causal insight into why the method works and validating the core hypothesis.
- **Practical and Training-Free Design**
  - Requires no model fine-tuning or weight access (Section 1, Section 3.4), which matters for impact by lowering adoption barriers and ensuring compatibility with closed-source or proprietary LLMs.
  - Maintains computational parity with existing multi-permutation baselines in terms of forward passes (Section 3.4), which matters for technical soundness by ensuring the method does not introduce prohibitive architectural overhead.
  - Targets specifically unstable queries where frequency voting is least reliable (Section 4.5, 19.89% intervention rate), which matters for impact by demonstrating efficient resource utilization and focused aggregation behavior.3) Weaknesses
- **Modest Absolute Performance Gains and Limited Benchmarking**
  - The reported improvement over majority voting is only +0.19 SubEM at K=20 (Table 1, Section 4.2), which matters for impact because the marginal gain may not justify the computational overhead in high-stakes or latency-sensitive applications.
  - Evaluation is restricted to a single dataset (NaturalQuestions) and a single model (Qwen3-8B) (Section 4.1), which matters for experimental rigor because it limits generalizability claims across different domains, retrieval qualities, and model architectures.
  - Lacks comparison against other advanced inference-time aggregation or self-consistency variants (Section 2.3, Section 4.1), which matters for novelty and technical soundness because it weakens the competitive positioning of CCV within the broader RAG robustness literature.
- **Fragility of Evidence Filtering and Citation Extraction**
  - Relies on strict JSON-constrained generation and exact substring matching for quotes (Section 3.2, Section 4.4), which matters for technical soundness because it may fail with models that struggle with structured output or heavily paraphrase, reducing real-world robustness.
  - The ablation shows that removing quote verification significantly boosts coverage and performance (Table 3, Section 4.4), which matters for experimental rigor because it suggests the stated grounding mechanism may be counterproductive, undermining the method's core design premise.
  - No analysis of citation hallucination rates or false-positive document mappings (Section 3.3), which matters for technical soundness because it leaves open the risk that incorrect but consistent citations could systematically bias the voting outcome.
- **Computational and Latency Overheads in Practice**
  - Requires K forward passes per query, with K=20 used for peak performance (Section 3.4, Table 2), which matters for impact because a 20x latency multiplier is often prohibitive for real-time or interactive RAG deployments.
  - Claims negligible aggregation overhead but does not report end-to-end latency or throughput metrics (Section 3.4), which matters for experimental rigor because it lacks practical deployment feasibility analysis, making it difficult to assess real-world viability.
  - Suggests KV-cache sharing but provides no empirical validation of this optimization (Section 3.4), which matters for technical soundness because it leaves a key efficiency claim unsubstantiated, reducing the completeness of the practical evaluation.
- **Inconsistent Experimental Reporting and Citation Anomalies**
  - Direct numerical contradiction at K=5 between Table 2 (Citation SubEM = 45.81%) and Table 3/Section 4.4 (Citation Vote with quote = 45.73%, no quote = 46.37%), which matters for experimental rigor because it undermines confidence in the ablation results and scaling analysis, suggesting potential copy-paste errors or mislabeled runs.
  - Inconsistent baseline reporting for MoA Majority Vote at K=5 (45.82% in Tables 1/2 vs. 45.71% in Table 3), which matters for clarity because it suggests potential inconsistencies in experimental runs or data extraction across tables.
  - Citation of a future-dated reference (Zhang et al., 2026) in Sections 1, 2.4, and the References list, which matters for credibility as it indicates careless metadata handling or automated generation artifacts that compromise bibliographic accuracy.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope and Benchmarking**
  - Report results on additional QA datasets (e.g., TriviaQA, HotpotQA) and diverse model families (e.g., Llama, Mistral) to validate cross-domain generalizability and address the single-dataset limitation.
  - Include comparisons with recent inference-time robustness methods (e.g., permutation self-consistency, self-reflection voting) to establish a stronger competitive baseline and contextualize the +0.19 SubEM gain.
  - Provide a cost-benefit analysis quantifying the SubEM gain per additional forward pass to justify the computational trade-off and guide practitioners on optimal K selection.
- **Strengthen Evidence Filtering and Citation Robustness**
  - Replace exact substring matching with semantic similarity or LLM-based verification to handle paraphrased quotes and improve coverage, directly addressing the fragility noted in the ablation study.
  - Conduct a detailed error analysis on citation hallucinations, reporting false-positive rates and their impact on voting accuracy to mitigate the risk of systematic bias from incorrect but consistent citations.
  - Introduce a fallback mechanism for queries where evidence filtering yields insufficient valid responses, ensuring graceful degradation and improving robustness across diverse model behaviors.
- **Quantify Latency and Validate Efficiency Claims**
  - Report end-to-end inference latency, throughput, and memory usage for K=5, 10, and 20 to provide actionable deployment metrics and address the lack of practical feasibility analysis.
  - Empirically evaluate the proposed KV-cache sharing strategy, measuring actual speedup and memory savings across permutations to substantiate the efficiency claims made in the practical considerations section.
  - Explore early-stopping or adaptive K selection strategies that dynamically adjust permutation count based on citation convergence, reducing unnecessary computation while preserving performance gains.
- **Rectify Reporting Inconsistencies and Verify References**
  - Reconcile the K=5 performance metrics across Table 2 and Table 3/Section 4.4, clarifying whether the ablation was run at K=5 or K=20 and correcting any copy-paste errors to ensure internal consistency.
  - Standardize baseline reporting across all tables, ensuring consistent values or explicitly noting variance sources for the MoA Majority Vote at K=5 to improve reproducibility.
  - Verify and correct the publication year for Zhang et al. (Stable-RAG) in the reference list and in-text citations to ensure bibliographic accuracy and maintain scholarly rigor.5) Score
- Overall (10): 5 — The method introduces a principled aggregation signal but offers modest gains (+0.19 SubEM, Table 1) and contains internal reporting inconsistencies (Table 2 vs. Table 3) that reduce confidence in the results.
- Novelty (10): 7 — Shifting from answer-frequency to document-ID agreement is a clear conceptual advance (Section 3.3, Equations 1–2), though it builds directly on existing permutation self-consistency frameworks.
- Technical Quality (10): 5 — The pipeline is well-defined and empirically validated (Figure 1, Table 2), but the reliance on strict quote matching, unverified efficiency claims (Section 3.4), and numerical contradictions in the ablation (Table 3) limit robustness and trustworthiness.
- Clarity (10): 7 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear mathematical formulations (Equations 1–2) and comprehensive figures (Figure 1, Figure 2), though inconsistent table values and a future-dated citation (References) detract from presentation quality.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence due to clear method description and reproducible experimental setup, though limited to a single model/dataset (Section 4.1) and reporting anomalies reduce certainty about broader applicability and result accuracy.
