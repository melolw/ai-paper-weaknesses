# Global Summary
The paper addresses the vulnerability of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems to document ordering, where position bias causes inconsistent outputs across different permutations of retrieved documents. The authors propose Citation-Consistent Voting (CCV), a training-free inference-time method that aggregates answers based on document-ID agreement rather than answer frequency. CCV prompts the generator for structured citations, filters for evidence validity, maps citations to permutation-invariant document IDs, and selects the answer with the highest citation consistency. Evaluated on the NaturalQuestions dataset using Qwen3-8B and Contriever retrieval, CCV achieves 46.37% SubEM at K=20 permutations, outperforming majority voting by +0.19 SubEM and +0.17 F1. The improvement scales monotonically with the number of permutations. Diagnostic analysis confirms a significant correlation between citation agreement and answer correctness (p = 1.14 × 10⁻⁵). The authors note limitations including modest absolute gains, evaluation on a single dataset and model, and the computational cost of K forward passes.

# Abstract
- Problem: RAG systems exhibit position bias, leading to inconsistent outputs when retrieved documents are permuted.
- Method: Citation-Consistent Voting (CCV) is a training-free approach that aggregates answers based on document-ID agreement. It prompts for citations, maps them to permutation-invariant IDs, and selects the most citation-consistent answer.
- Evaluation & Results: Tested on NaturalQuestions with Qwen3-8B and Contriever retrieval. CCV achieves 46.37% SubEM at K=20 permutations, surpassing majority voting by +0.19 points. Gains scale monotonically with K.
- Key Claims: Citation agreement correlates significantly with answer correctness (p = 1.14 × 10⁻⁵). CCV requires no additional training and is compatible with any RAG system capable of producing structured citations.

# Method
- Problem Setup: Given a query and N retrieved documents, standard RAG uses a fixed order. Multi-permutation RAG samples K random permutations and generates K responses. The challenge is aggregating these responses reliably.
- Evidence Filtering: The generator is prompted to output JSON containing an answer, a document index, and a supporting quote. A response is evidence-valid if the document index is valid, the quote appears in the cited document, and the answer appears within the quote. Invalid responses are filtered out.
- Citation-Consistent Aggregation: Cited document indices are mapped to underlying, permutation-invariant document IDs. Responses are grouped by answer. For each unique answer, the modal document ID is identified. The citation-consistency score is the count of evidence-valid responses citing this modal document. The final answer is selected by maximizing this score, with ties broken by raw answer frequency.
- Practical Considerations: Relaxing the strict quote verification to only require a valid document index improves citation path coverage while preserving benefits. Computational cost matches standard multi-permutation methods (K forward passes), with negligible overhead for filtering and aggregation. The method supports batched inference and KV-cache sharing.

# Experiments
- Setup: Evaluated on NaturalQuestions (3,610 test queries). Retrieval uses Contriever (top-5 Wikipedia passages). Generator is Qwen3-8B with greedy decoding (temperature=0). Metrics: Substring Exact Match (SubEM) and token-level F1. Baselines: Vanilla RAG and Mixture-of-Agents (MoA) Majority Vote. Permutations tested: K ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20}.
- Main Results: At K=20, CCV achieves 46.37% SubEM and 46.83% F1, outperforming MoA majority voting by +0.19 SubEM and +0.17 F1. Vanilla RAG (standard prompt) scores 48.98% SubEM but 41.10% F1, attributed to output format differences rather than method quality. Multi-permutation methods with JSON citation format consistently outperform single-pass generation.
- K-Scaling Analysis: Improvement over MoA scales monotonically with K. Differences (Δ) are -0.01 at K=5, +0.10 at K=10, +0.11 at K=15, and +0.19 at K=20. Larger K provides more reliable citation agreement signals.
- Ablation (Quote Constraint): At K=5, requiring exact quote matches yields 45.73% SubEM, 96.37% citation path coverage, and 4.12 average valid runs per query. Removing the quote constraint increases coverage to 99.70%, valid runs to 4.96, and SubEM to 46.37% (+0.64 improvement). Document-ID agreement is more critical than strict quote verification.
- Diagnostic Analysis: Correct predictions have a significantly higher mean agreement score than incorrect ones (3.04 vs. 2.85, Mann-Whitney p = 1.14 × 10⁻⁵). CCV primarily targets unstable queries (producing ≥3 unique answers across permutations), which comprise 699 queries (19.4% of the test set). CCV selects a different answer than MoA in 19.89% of these unstable cases, leaving stable queries unchanged.
