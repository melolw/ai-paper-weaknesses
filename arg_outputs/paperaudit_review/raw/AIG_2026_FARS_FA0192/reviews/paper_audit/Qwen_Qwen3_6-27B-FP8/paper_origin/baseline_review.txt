1) Summary
This paper investigates whether autoregressive-order (AR-order) reinforcement learning post-training compromises the inherent order robustness of diffusion language models (dLLMs). The authors evaluate LLaDA-8B-Instruct (diffusion base), its JustGRPO-fine-tuned variant, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (AR anchor) on ReasonOrderQA and GSM8K under CoT-First and Answer-First prompting conditions. Using a robustness ratio metric, they demonstrate that AR-order RL significantly reduces order robustness (drops of 0.192 and 0.138, respectively) while substantially improving CoT-First accuracy, revealing an accuracy-robustness trade-off. The degradation is concentrated at medium difficulty levels and amplified by longer generation contexts. The work provides the first empirical analysis of how AR-biased reward signals affect the non-autoregressive generation capabilities of diffusion LMs.2) Strengths
- **Clear and Well-Motivated Research Question**
  - The paper explicitly identifies a critical gap in dLLM post-training literature regarding the impact of AR-biased rewards on non-AR generation capabilities (Section 1, Section 2). This matters because it highlights a potentially overlooked trade-off in current diffusion model optimization pipelines.
  - Order robustness is formally defined with a quantifiable metric, the robustness ratio $r = \text{Acc(AF)}/\text{Acc(CF)}$ (Equation 1, Section 3.1). This matters as it provides a standardized, interpretable measure for evaluating generation order flexibility across architectures.
  - The investigation is grounded in recent theoretical debates, specifically referencing the "flexibility trap" hypothesis (Section 2, Ni et al., 2026). This matters because it positions the empirical findings within an active research discourse on generation order and reasoning limits.- **Rigorous and Controlled Experimental Design**
  - The three-model comparison isolates the RL training variable while controlling for architecture and parameter scale (Section 3.2, Table 1). This matters because it allows causal attribution of robustness changes specifically to AR-order RL rather than architectural differences.
  - Deterministic generation settings (temperature=0.0) and bitwise identical verification across runs eliminate sampling variance (Section 3.4, Section 4.3). This matters because it ensures the observed robustness differences are exact measurements, enhancing reproducibility and statistical reliability.
  - Evaluation spans two distinct reasoning benchmarks, ReasonOrderQA and GSM8K (Section 3.3, Table 1). This matters because it demonstrates that the observed accuracy-robustness trade-off generalizes beyond synthetic arithmetic to natural language math problems.- **Insightful Granular Analysis**
  - The per-difficulty breakdown reveals that robustness degradation is concentrated at medium difficulty levels (D2–D3) (Figure 2, Section 4.2). This matters because it identifies the specific reasoning regime where AR-order bias is most detrimental, guiding targeted mitigation strategies.
  - The canvas length ablation demonstrates that longer generation contexts amplify robustness loss (Table 2, Section 4.3). This matters because it provides actionable insights into how generation hyperparameters interact with RL-induced sequential dependencies.
  - The "gap coverage" calculation quantifies that JustGRPO sits approximately 53% between the diffusion base and AR anchor (Section 4.1). This matters because it offers a nuanced characterization of partial bias internalization rather than a binary robustness/failure outcome.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Scope of RL Methods and Reward Signals**
  - Only JustGRPO is evaluated as the AR-order RL method, despite other diffusion RL approaches being mentioned (Section 3.2, Section 2). This matters because findings cannot be generalized to alternative RL formulations like MDPO or Inpainting-Guided PO.
  - No alternative reward formulations (e.g., order-agnostic or parallel rewards) are tested (Section 1, Section 5). This matters because it leaves open whether the observed trade-off is inherent to RL optimization or specific to AR-order reward design.
  - Lacks comparison with non-RL post-training methods such as SFT on mixed-order data (Section 3.2). This matters because it prevents disentangling whether degradation stems from RL dynamics or merely from AR-ordered training data exposure.- **Metric and Threshold Limitations**
  - The robustness ratio $r = \text{Acc(AF)}/\text{Acc(CF)}$ is mathematically sensitive to low CoT-First accuracy, which can artificially inflate $r$ in harder subsets (Equation 1, Section 3.1). This matters because it may obscure true robustness differences when baseline CF accuracy drops significantly.
  - The decision threshold of $\Delta r \geq 0.10$ is defined without statistical validation or sensitivity analysis (Section 3.1). This matters because an arbitrary threshold lacks empirical justification for what constitutes a practically significant robustness drop.
  - Does not account for answer extraction reliability under Answer-First prompts (Section 3.3, Section 4.1). This matters because lower AF accuracy might stem from parsing/formatting failures rather than genuine reasoning degradation, confounding the robustness metric.- **Insufficient Analysis of Failure Modes**
  - Claims degradation stems from "sequential dependencies learned during AR-order RL" but provides no qualitative error analysis (Section 4.3, Section 5). This matters because the absence of concrete examples weakens the mechanistic explanation for how AR-order training breaks reverse-order reasoning.
  - Attributes the D4 floor effect to "exceeding reasoning capacity" without investigating prompt formatting or token masking constraints (Section 4.2). This matters because it overlooks potential implementation artifacts in diffusion generation under strict Answer-First constraints.
  - Does not analyze whether JustGRPO improves Answer-First accuracy on easier problems or only degrades it (Table 1, Section 4.1). This matters because it misses the opportunity to identify conditions where AR-order RL might preserve or enhance order flexibility.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand RL Methodology and Reward Comparisons**
  - Include at least one additional RL method for diffusion LMs (e.g., MDPO or Inpainting-Guided PO from Section 2) to test whether the robustness degradation generalizes across optimization algorithms.
  - Implement and evaluate an order-agnostic reward signal that penalizes neither CoT-First nor Answer-First generation orders to isolate the impact of reward structure.
  - Add a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baseline trained on mixed-order data to disentangle RL optimization effects from data ordering biases.- **Refine Robustness Metric and Validation**
  - Introduce a complementary metric, such as the absolute accuracy difference $\text{Acc(CF)} - \text{Acc(AF)}$, to mitigate ratio sensitivity at low accuracy levels and provide a more stable robustness estimate.
  - Conduct a sensitivity analysis on the $\Delta r$ threshold by reporting confidence intervals or using statistical tests across multiple random seeds or prompt variations to justify the 0.10 cutoff.
  - Implement a robust answer extraction pipeline for Answer-First outputs and report extraction success rates to isolate reasoning failures from formatting or parsing artifacts.- **Incorporate Qualitative and Mechanistic Analysis**
  - Provide concrete failure examples from D2–D3 Answer-First prompts to illustrate how AR-order RL specifically disrupts reverse-order reasoning and token generation patterns.
  - Analyze token generation order statistics (e.g., average position of answer tokens vs. reasoning tokens) to quantify how JustGRPO shifts the model's implicit generation policy.
  - Investigate whether adjusting diffusion generation parameters (e.g., remasking strategy, block length) can mitigate the observed robustness degradation without sacrificing CoT-First accuracy.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper addresses a timely and important question regarding AR-order RL effects on diffusion LMs (Section 1), but is limited by a narrow evaluation scope and lack of qualitative analysis (Section 3.2, Section 4.3).
- Novelty (10): 7 — The investigation of order robustness degradation under AR-order RL is a novel empirical contribution (Section 1, Section 4.1), though the underlying tension between AR rewards and non-AR generation is implicitly suggested in prior work (Section 2, Ni et al., 2026).
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The experimental design is controlled and deterministic (Section 3.4, Section 4.3), but the robustness metric lacks sensitivity analysis and the threshold is arbitrary (Equation 1, Section 3.1), while the single RL method limits generalizability (Section 3.2).
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear definitions, concise tables, and logical flow (Section 3.1, Table 1, Figure 1), making the methodology and findings easy to follow.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence in the empirical findings due to deterministic verification and clear benchmarks (Section 4.3, Table 1), but moderate confidence in broader claims due to the limited scope of RL methods and reward signals tested (Section 3.2).
