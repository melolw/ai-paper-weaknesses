# Innovation & Motivation Report

## 1. The Authors' Claimed Contribution
- **Core Problem**: Determining whether applying autoregressive-order (AR-order) reinforcement learning post-training (specifically JustGRPO) to diffusion language models (dLLMs) compromises their inherent "order robustness" (the ability to solve reasoning problems regardless of whether the reasoning chain precedes or follows the final answer).
- **Claimed Gap**: *"The dominant post-training method, JustGRPO, uses AR-order (left-to-right) reward signals, raising concerns about compromising dLLM order flexibility."* Prior RL/LLM literature focuses heavily on accuracy gains, alignment, and computational efficiency, largely ignoring the architectural trade-offs imposed by sequential reward signals on non-autoregressive, parallel generative models.
- **Proposed Solution**: A controlled empirical stress-test comparing a diffusion base model, a JustGRPO-fine-tuned diffusion model, and a pure AR anchor across CoT-First and Answer-First prompt conditions. The authors introduce a novel "robustness ratio" metric ($r = Acc(AF)/Acc(CF)$), establish a quantitative decision threshold ($\Delta r \ge 0.10$), and conduct per-difficulty and canvas-length ablations to isolate and quantify the accuracy-robustness trade-off.

## 2. Comparative Scrutiny (The "Trial")

### vs. Group Distributionally Robust Optimization-Driven Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning
- **Identified Overlap**: Both operate within the GRPO/RLVR post-training paradigm for LLM reasoning. The similar work optimizes GRPO's computational efficiency and accuracy for heterogeneous reasoning data; this manuscript applies the same GRPO framework to diffusion models to probe architectural compatibility.
- **Manuscript's Defense**: The manuscript explicitly positions itself as a diagnostic investigation into *architectural compatibility* rather than algorithmic optimization. It isolates the reward signal's directional bias (AR-order) as the independent variable, contrasting with the similar work's focus on difficulty-aware sampling and rollout allocation. The authors state their goal is to evaluate *"whether AR-order RL training compromises order robustness,"* not to improve GRPO's training dynamics.
- **Reviewer's Assessment**: The distinction is valid and well-defended. The manuscript successfully pivots from "how to train GRPO better" to "what does GRPO do to non-AR architectures?" This shifts the contribution from methodological improvement to phenomenon characterization. The overlap does not undermine novelty; rather, it highlights that the manuscript uses GRPO as a diagnostic probe rather than a target for optimization.

### vs. Improving Variable-Length Generation in Diffusion Language Models via Length Regularization
- **Identified Overlap**: Both address how dLLMs' native flexibility is compromised by implicit structural biases inherent to their fixed-canvas inference design. The similar work tackles length-induced confidence bias; this manuscript tackles order-dependency bias.
- **Manuscript's Defense**: The manuscript differentiates by focusing on *reasoning order* rather than *generation length*. It explicitly conducts a "Canvas Length Ablation" (testing `L=64` vs `L=512`) to demonstrate that longer contexts amplify robustness degradation, acknowledging the canvas constraint but arguing that AR-order RL introduces a distinct, orthogonal bias that interacts with canvas size. The authors frame order robustness as a *"property fundamentally absent in AR models,"* carving out a separate axis of flexibility from length uncertainty.
- **Reviewer's Assessment**: Strong differentiation. While both probe dLLM canvas limitations, the manuscript successfully isolates a novel failure mode (order dependency) that is not reducible to length bias. The ablation study effectively bridges the two concepts without conflating them, demonstrating that the authors are aware of parallel canvas-bias literature and have deliberately scoped their investigation to a distinct structural dimension.

### vs. Mixing Classifiers to Alleviate the Accuracy-Robustness Trade-Off
- **Identified Overlap**: Both frame and quantify a fundamental accuracy-robustness trade-off in deep learning systems under performance-driven optimization. The similar work addresses adversarial robustness in classifiers; this manuscript addresses prompt-order robustness in generative models.
- **Manuscript's Defense**: The manuscript contextualizes the trade-off specifically within *generative language modeling architectures*. It introduces a domain-specific metric (robustness ratio $r$) and a strict decision threshold ($\Delta r \ge 0.10$) tailored to reasoning flexibility, rather than adversarial perturbation bounds. Crucially, the authors employ a *"fully deterministic (temperature=0.0)"* evaluation protocol with bitwise-identical verification across 4,000 outputs to isolate the trade-off from sampling variance, a rigor not typically required in classifier robustness studies.
- **Reviewer's Assessment**: The conceptual overlap is significant, but the manuscript successfully adapts the trade-off framework to a novel domain with appropriate methodological rigor. The deterministic verification and thresholding mechanism strengthen its defense against claims of being a mere conceptual transplant. The finding that JustGRPO covers *"~53% of the robustness gap to the AR anchor"* provides a quantifiable continuum that mirrors the similar work's mixing paradigm, but in a generative, non-adversarial context.

## 3. Novelty Verdict
- **Innovation Type**: Application-Oriented (with Substantive Diagnostic Framing)
- **Assessment**: The paper successfully survives comparative scrutiny by reframing an existing RL methodology (JustGRPO) as a diagnostic tool to expose a previously unquantified architectural trade-off in dLLMs. It does not claim algorithmic novelty; instead, it delivers a rigorous empirical stress-test that maps the boundary conditions of AR-order RL on non-autoregressive models. The existence of the similar works does not weaken the motivation; they provide the theoretical scaffolding (RLVR dynamics, canvas biases, accuracy-robustness tensions) that the manuscript effectively leverages to isolate a new phenomenon. However, the contribution remains purely diagnostic.
  - **Strength**: Exceptional experimental rigor (deterministic bitwise verification, clear thresholding, difficulty stratification, canvas ablation). The introduction of the robustness ratio metric and the quantification of the "53% gap" provide actionable benchmarks for the dLLM community. The focus on a critical blind spot (architectural compatibility of RL signals) is highly timely.
  - **Weakness**: Purely descriptive/diagnostic; lacks a proposed mitigation strategy, architectural modification, or alternative RL objective to preserve order robustness. The reliance on a single RL method (JustGRPO) and two benchmarks limits generalizability. The "decision threshold" of 0.10 appears somewhat arbitrary without theoretical grounding or sensitivity analysis.

## 4. Key Evidence Anchors
- **Introduction/Abstract**: Explicit gap statement: *"JustGRPO applies Group Relative Policy Optimization with left-to-right token-level rewards, raising concerns about compromising dLLM order flexibility."*
- **Experiments (Problem Formulation)**: Definition of robustness ratio $r = Acc(AF)/Acc(CF)$ and the quantitative decision rule: *"robustness degraded if $r_{JustGRPO} \leq r_{Base} - 0.10$."*
- **Experiments (Main Results)**: Quantified degradation: ReasonOrderQA $\Delta r = -0.192$, GSM8K $\Delta r = -0.138$, and the calculated continuum metric: *"JustGRPO covers 53.5% of the robustness gap to the AR anchor."*
- **Experiments (Per-Difficulty Analysis)**: Evidence of structural concentration: *"Degradation is concentrated at D2–D3 difficulty levels... D2 drops from 0.635 to 0.407 ($\Delta r=-0.228$)."*
- **Experiments (Canvas Length Ablation & Deterministic Verification)**: Context-dependent amplification (*"Gap ratio=0.756, indicating longer generation contexts amplify robustness degradation"*) and variance isolation (*"4,000 total outputs... verified bitwise identical across runs. Zero sampling variance."*)
