# Global Summary
This paper addresses catastrophic forgetting during sequential continual fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs). The authors propose RazorSFT, an on-policy supervised fine-tuning method that samples candidate responses from the current model, filters them via a task verifier, and selects the highest log-probability correct response as the training target. This KL-minimal selection keeps training targets close to the current policy distribution, reducing distributional shift. Evaluated on a 3-stage sequential curriculum (IFEval → MMLU → Countdown) using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, RazorSFT reduces forgetting by 60.4 percentage points compared to offline SFT (Forgetting Measure FM: -0.039 vs. -0.643) and outperforms GRPO on average accuracy (0.616 vs. 0.515) and current-task adaptation on Countdown (0.628 vs. 0.261). Ablation studies indicate that on-policy data generation alone accounts for 76% of the forgetting improvement, supporting theoretical claims that on-policy training, rather than reward optimization, drives forgetting mitigation. The paper notes it was generated by an automated research system and provides public code. Training uses full-parameter FSDP, learning rate 1e-5, and 2 epochs per stage, with results averaged over 3 random seeds.

# Abstract
- Problem: Sequential fine-tuning of LLMs causes catastrophic forgetting. RL methods like GRPO mitigate forgetting but introduce substantial complexity (reward models, policy gradients, hyperparameter tuning).
- Method: RazorSFT samples k candidate responses from the current policy, filters them through a task verifier, and selects the highest log-probability correct response as the training target. This KL-minimal selection ensures targets remain close to the current policy distribution.
- Evaluation: 3-stage continual learning benchmark.
- Key Results: RazorSFT reduces forgetting by 60.4 percentage points vs. offline SFT (FM -0.039 vs. -0.643). Outperforms GRPO on average accuracy (0.616 vs. 0.515) and task adaptation on Countdown (0.628 vs. 0.261).
- Ablation Claim: On-policy data accounts for 76% of the forgetting improvement over offline SFT.
- Caveat/Note: Paper generated by an automated research system; code is publicly available.

# Method
- Problem Setup: Continual learning involves sequential fine-tuning on tasks T_1 to T_T. Each task has a training dataset with prompts and reference targets. Training proceeds sequentially, using the previous checkpoint as initialization.
- Metrics: 
  - Average Accuracy (AvgAcc): Mean accuracy across all tasks after the final training stage.
  - Forgetting Measure (FM): Average performance drop from peak to final stage. FM is non-positive; values closer to zero indicate less forgetting.
- RazorSFT Procedure: For each prompt x_i at epoch e:
  1. Sample k candidate responses from the current policy π_{θ_{e-1}}.
  2. Filter candidates using a task-specific verifier V_t(x, y) to obtain accepted set A_i.
  3. Select training target y_i^* = argmax_{y in A_i} log π_{θ_{e-1}}(y | x_i) if A_i is non-empty; otherwise, fallback to offline reference y_i^{ref}.
  4. Train using standard cross-entropy loss on (x_i, y_i^*).
- Theoretical Motivation: Standard SFT minimizes forward KL divergence to a fixed target distribution, causing large distributional shifts. On-policy RL methods implicitly bias toward solutions with smaller KL shift from the initial model. RazorSFT approximates this by selecting high-probability correct on-policy targets, minimizing KL[δ_{y^*} || π_θ]. This aligns with recent theoretical work (Chen et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2025) indicating that on-policy data, not reward optimization or KL regularization, is the primary driver of forgetting mitigation.

# Experiments
- Experimental Setup: 
  - Curriculum: 3-stage sequential training: Stage 1 IFEval (instruction-following), Stage 2 MMLU (multiple-choice knowledge), Stage 3 Countdown (arithmetic equation construction).
  - Base Model: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
  - Baselines: Zero-shot, Best-of-8 (inference-time verifier selection), Offline SFT, GRPO.
  - Training Config: Full-parameter fine-tuning with FSDP, learning rate 1e-5, 2 epochs per stage. RazorSFT uses k=8 candidates for stages 1-2, k=16 for stage 3, temperature 0.8-1.0. Results averaged over 3 random seeds.
- Main Results (Table 1):
  - RazorSFT-8: AvgAcc 0.616±0.002, FM -0.039±0.007. Task accuracies: IFEval 0.546±0.006, MMLU 0.673±0.009, Countdown 0.628±0.010.
  - Offline SFT: AvgAcc 0.121±0.003, FM -0.643±0.001. MMLU accuracy drops to 0.000±0.000 after Stage 3.
  - GRPO: AvgAcc 0.515±0.033, FM -0.023±0.017. Countdown accuracy 0.261±0.092.
  - RazorSFT improves FM by 60.4 percentage points over Offline SFT and outperforms GRPO on AvgAcc by +10.1pp and on Countdown by 2.4x.
- Ablation Study (Table 2):
  - Offline SFT: FM -0.642, AvgAcc 0.119.
  - OnPolicy-SFT (trains on all correct on-policy samples, no selection): FM -0.179, AvgAcc 0.260. On-policy data alone accounts for 76% of the total FM improvement.
  - First-Accepted (selects first correct sample): FM -0.042, AvgAcc 0.598.
  - RazorSFT-8 (KL-minimal selection): FM -0.032, AvgAcc 0.619. KL-minimal selection provides additional benefit over random/first acceptance.
- Forgetting Dynamics (Figure 2): Offline SFT shows catastrophic drops (IFEval accuracy falls sharply in Stage 2; MMLU collapses to ~0.01 in Stage 3). RazorSFT maintains stable performance across stages. GRPO shows intermediate retention but weaker adaptation on Countdown.
- Replacement Rates (Figure 3): The fraction of training samples where RazorSFT selects an on-policy target over the offline reference ranges from 76.2% to 94.7% across stages and epochs. High replacement rates indicate the model consistently generates correct solutions closer to its current distribution than fixed teacher targets.
