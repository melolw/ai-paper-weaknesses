1) Summary
The paper addresses catastrophic forgetting during sequential fine-tuning of large language models. It introduces RazorSFT, an on-policy supervised fine-tuning method that samples candidate responses from the current model, filters them via a task verifier, and selects the highest log-probability correct response as the training target. This KL-minimal selection aims to keep training targets close to the current policy distribution, reducing distributional shift. Evaluated on a 3-stage sequential curriculum (IFEval → MMLU → Countdown) using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, RazorSFT reduces the forgetting measure by 60.4 percentage points compared to offline SFT and outperforms GRPO in average accuracy and task adaptation. Ablation studies indicate that on-policy data generation accounts for 76% of the forgetting mitigation, demonstrating that RL-like continual learning benefits can be achieved within a simplified SFT framework.2) Strengths
- **Clear and Well-Motivated Methodological Design**
  - Evidence: Section 3.2 and Equation (1) formally define the three-stage pipeline (sampling, verification, KL-minimal selection) with a deterministic fallback mechanism. Rationale: This provides a reproducible, implementation-friendly alternative to complex RL pipelines, enhancing technical soundness and practical adoption.
  - Evidence: Section 3.3 explicitly links the argmax log-prob selection criterion to minimizing forward KL divergence between the target and current policy. Rationale: Grounding the empirical procedure in distributional shift theory strengthens the methodological novelty and clarifies why the approach mitigates forgetting.
  - Evidence: The fallback to offline references when the accepted set is empty (Equation 1) ensures every prompt yields exactly one training target. Rationale: This design choice maintains consistent training intensity across methods, addressing a common practical flaw in on-policy sampling strategies.
- **Rigorous and Informative Experimental Evaluation**
  - Evidence: Section 4.1 and Table 1 evaluate a 3-stage curriculum spanning instruction-following, knowledge retrieval, and arithmetic, with results averaged over 3 random seeds. Rationale: The diverse task composition and statistical reporting enhance experimental rigor and validate the method’s stability across distinct capability domains.
  - Evidence: Figure 2 visualizes per-task accuracy trajectories across training stages, contrasting RazorSFT with Offline SFT and GRPO. Rationale: These dynamics clearly demonstrate how on-policy target selection preserves prior knowledge while enabling new-task adaptation, directly supporting the core impact claim.
  - Evidence: Table 1 reports both Average Accuracy and Forgetting Measure alongside per-task breakdowns. Rationale: Providing complementary metrics rather than a single aggregate score improves transparency and allows readers to assess the plasticity-stability trade-off accurately.
- **Insightful Ablation and Mechanism Analysis**
  - Evidence: Table 2 isolates the contributions of on-policy data versus KL-minimal selection, showing on-policy data drives 76% of the FM improvement. Rationale: This quantitative decomposition clarifies the primary mechanism behind the method’s success, aligning with recent theoretical insights and strengthening technical soundness.
  - Evidence: Figure 3 reports replacement rates (76–95%) across stages and epochs, indicating consistent preference for on-policy targets. Rationale: This empirical validation confirms that the model reliably generates correct, distributionally aligned solutions, directly supporting the KL-minimal hypothesis.
  - Evidence: The comparison with the "First-Accepted" variant in Table 2 shows KL-minimal selection improves FM from −0.042 to −0.032 and AvgAcc from 0.598 to 0.619. Rationale: Demonstrating measurable gains over naive on-policy sampling justifies the algorithmic complexity and highlights the value of explicit probability-based filtering.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Scope of Evaluation and Benchmark Diversity**
  - Evidence: Section 4.1 restricts evaluation to a single custom 3-stage curriculum, despite mentioning TRACE (Wang et al., 2023) in Section 2. Rationale: Lack of standardized benchmark comparison limits generalizability claims and reduces experimental rigor relative to contemporary continual learning literature.
  - Evidence: Only Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is used as the base model (Section 4.1). Rationale: Single-model evaluation leaves open questions about architectural robustness, scalability, and whether the method’s benefits transfer to smaller or larger parameter regimes.
  - Evidence: The selected tasks rely on exact-match or programmatic verification (IFEval, MMLU, Countdown). Rationale: Missing open-ended generation or domain-shift scenarios restricts impact assessment, as forgetting often manifests differently in unconstrained generation settings.
- **Insufficient Analysis of Computational and Practical Overhead**
  - Evidence: Section 3.2 requires sampling k candidates per prompt per epoch, but Section 4.1 provides no training time, FLOPs, or memory metrics. Rationale: Without computational profiling, it is impossible to assess the practical trade-off between forgetting mitigation and resource efficiency, weakening technical soundness for deployment.
  - Evidence: Section 3.2 assumes task-specific verifiers but does not discuss handling tasks without deterministic checkers. Rationale: This omission limits clarity on real-world applicability, as many continual learning scenarios lack exact-match validation.
  - Evidence: The candidate count k is set to 8 for stages 1–2 and 16 for stage 3 (Section 4.1) without justification or ablation. Rationale: Heuristic hyperparameter selection without sensitivity analysis reduces reproducibility and obscures the optimal computational budget for KL-minimal selection.
- **Theoretical Claims Lack Formal Rigor**
  - Evidence: Section 3.3 asserts that selecting the highest log-probability correct target minimizes KL divergence, but no formal derivation or gradient bound is provided. Rationale: Qualitative theoretical motivation without mathematical grounding limits novelty claims and leaves the KL-minimal property empirically rather than theoretically verified.
  - Evidence: The manuscript cites Chen et al. (2025) and Shenfeld et al. (2025) to support on-policy benefits but does not formally connect cross-entropy loss on selected targets to RL’s KL regularization terms. Rationale: Missing formal linkage reduces technical soundness and makes it difficult to distinguish RazorSFT’s mechanism from implicit RL behaviors.
  - Evidence: Equation 1 introduces a mixture distribution via the offline fallback, yet Section 3.3 does not analyze how this affects the claimed KL-minimal property. Rationale: Ignoring the fallback’s distributional impact creates a gap between the theoretical motivation and the actual training objective, affecting clarity and rigor.
- **Internal Consistency and Reproducibility Concerns**
  - Evidence: Table 1 reports RazorSFT-8 AvgAcc as 0.616 and FM as −0.039, while Table 2 reports 0.619 and −0.032 for the same method. Offline SFT FM also differs (−0.643 vs. −0.642). Rationale: Unexplained numerical discrepancies across tables undermine reproducibility and suggest potential reporting errors or uncontrolled experimental variations.
  - Evidence: Section 4.1 specifies $k=8$ for stages 1–2 and $k=16$ for stage 3, yet Table 1 labels the method "RazorSFT-8". Rationale: This naming convention is misleading and obscures the actual hyperparameter configuration used for the main results, affecting clarity and reproducibility.
  - Evidence: Section 2 cites "Shenfeld et al., 2025" and "Shenfeld et al., 2026", but the reference list only includes entries for 2024. Similarly, "Lai et al., 2025" is cited in text but listed as 2024 in references. Rationale: Citation-year mismatches and references to non-existent years indicate a lack of careful proofreading, which raises concerns about the overall rigor and reliability of the manuscript.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope and Benchmark Coverage**
  - Incorporate results on standardized continual learning benchmarks such as TRACE or SuperCLUE to validate performance across broader task distributions and evaluation protocols, directly addressing the single-curriculum limitation in Section 4.1.
  - Conduct experiments on at least two additional model scales (e.g., 1.5B and 14B) to demonstrate architectural robustness and clarify whether the on-policy benefits scale consistently with parameter count.
  - Include at least one open-ended or domain-shift task (e.g., code generation or scientific QA) to test whether the verifier-dependent pipeline generalizes beyond exact-match settings and to assess impact in unconstrained generation.
- **Quantify Computational Costs and Verifier Dependencies**
  - Report wall-clock training time, GPU hours, and peak memory usage for RazorSFT, Offline SFT, and GRPO to provide a clear efficiency-complexity trade-off analysis, directly addressing the missing overhead metrics in Section 4.1.
  - Discuss strategies for tasks lacking deterministic verifiers, such as using LLM-as-a-judge or heuristic scoring, and provide a small-scale experiment demonstrating feasibility to improve practical clarity and deployment readiness.
  - Add an ablation study varying k (e.g., k=4, 8, 16, 32) to identify the optimal candidate count and establish a principled guideline for computational budget allocation, replacing the heuristic choices in Section 4.1.
- **Strengthen Theoretical Grounding and Formal Analysis**
  - Provide a formal derivation or empirical gradient norm analysis showing how KL-minimal target selection reduces parameter update magnitudes compared to offline SFT, directly addressing the qualitative claims in Section 3.3.
  - Explicitly connect the cross-entropy objective on selected targets to the KL regularization terms in RL formulations, clarifying the mathematical equivalence or divergence to enhance technical soundness and novelty positioning.
  - Analyze the impact of the fallback mechanism on the overall training distribution, potentially introducing a mixture KL bound or empirical analysis of fallback frequency and its effect on forgetting, to resolve the gap between Equation 1 and Section 3.3.
- **Resolve Internal Inconsistencies and Improve Reproducibility**
  - Reconcile the numerical discrepancies between Table 1 and Table 2 by clarifying whether they stem from different random seeds, training runs, or reporting errors, and ensure consistent values across the manuscript.
  - Rename the method in Table 1 (e.g., to "RazorSFT" or "RazorSFT-8/16") to accurately reflect the variable candidate count $k$ described in Section 4.1, or explicitly state the naming convention to avoid confusion.
  - Carefully proofread the manuscript to align in-text citations with the reference list, correcting the year mismatches for Shenfeld et al. and Lai et al. to ensure bibliographic accuracy and maintain scholarly rigor.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper presents a practical and well-motivated on-policy SFT method with strong empirical results on a 3-stage curriculum (Table 1, Section 4.2), though broader benchmark coverage, computational analysis, and internal consistency issues temper the overall impact.
- Novelty (10): 7 — The KL-minimal target selection within an SFT framework offers a clear simplification of RL-based continual learning (Section 3.2, Eq 1), building effectively on recent on-policy insights (Section 3.3).
- Technical Quality (10): 5 — Experiments are rigorous with clear ablations (Table 2, Figure 3), but the lack of computational overhead reporting, limited model/task diversity (Section 4.1), and unexplained numerical discrepancies between Table 1 and Table 2 reduce technical completeness and reproducibility.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The methodology, pipeline diagram (Figure 1), and experimental setup are clearly articulated, with well-structured ablations and dynamic visualizations (Figure 2, Section 4.4), though citation mismatches in Section 2 require correction.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence due to clear methodological description and reproducible experimental setup (Section 3.2, Section 4.1), though unreported computational costs, single-model evaluation, and table inconsistencies slightly reduce certainty.
