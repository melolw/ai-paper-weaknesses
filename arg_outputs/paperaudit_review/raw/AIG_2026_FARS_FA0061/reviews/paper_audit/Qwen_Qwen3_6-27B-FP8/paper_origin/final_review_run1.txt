1) Summary
The paper investigates whether entropy dynamics, previously successful in diagnosing reasoning errors in mathematical problems (EDIS), can serve as execution-free selection signals for code generation. The authors adapt EDIS into nEDIS, incorporating length and scale normalizations, and establish pre-registered success criteria requiring outperformance of confound baselines on HumanEval and MBPP. Using DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-Instruct with N=32 sampling, experiments yield a definitive negative result: nEDIS and its optimized variant nEDIS_v2 fail the pre-registered criteria and underperform trivial baselines like random first-sample selection. The authors attribute this failure to extreme entropy sparsity (88.3% zero values) in instruction-tuned code models and note that optimizing nEDIS_v2 requires inverting the original selection logic, suggesting it captures length bias rather than meaningful instability dynamics. The work contributes a rigorous negative result and open-source code to prevent redundant exploration of this direction.2) Strengths
- **Rigorous Experimental Design with Pre-Registered Criteria**
  - The authors explicitly define a success criterion requiring nEDIS to significantly outperform both confound baselines on both benchmarks at p < 0.05 (Section 3.4), which ensures methodological rigor and prevents post-hoc hypothesis tuning.
  - Statistical significance is rigorously tested using one-sided paired bootstrap with 1000 resamples (Section 4.3, Table 2), providing robust confidence in the observed performance gaps and strengthening experimental validity.
  - The inclusion of multiple confound baselines (Length-only, CV_H, Mean_H) isolates whether spike-structure provides unique signal beyond simple heuristics (Section 3.5), which enhances the technical soundness of the negative conclusion.
- **Clear Identification of Fundamental Failure Modes**
  - The discovery that 88.3% of per-token entropy values are exactly zero directly explains why spike detection fails (Section 4.4), offering a concrete, data-driven explanation that improves clarity and diagnostic value.
  - The "optimization paradox" analysis demonstrates that improving nEDIS requires inverting the selection direction and multiplying by length, contradicting the original EDIS hypothesis (Section 4.4, Equation 4), which highlights a critical theoretical mismatch and strengthens the impact of the negative result.
  - The comparison against trivial baselines (Greedy, First-Sample) in Table 1 clearly contextualizes the magnitude of the failure, showing that entropy-based ranking actively harms selection, which reinforces the experimental rigor and practical relevance.
- **High Reproducibility and Community Value**
  - The complete methodology, including hyperparameters (window size w=4, thresholds τ_b=1.36, τ_r=1.33) and generation settings, is detailed in Section 4.1 and Appendix A, enabling exact replication and supporting technical soundness.
  - The authors explicitly release code and data to prevent others from pursuing this direction (Abstract, Section 1), which is a valuable contribution that maximizes community impact for negative-result papers.
  - The pipeline is broadly visualized in Figure 1, mapping candidate generation and score computation steps, which aids readers in understanding the adaptation from EDIS to nEDIS and improves overall clarity.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Model and Benchmark Scope**
  - The evaluation relies solely on DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-Instruct (Section 4.1), which limits generalizability and reduces experimental rigor since other code models may exhibit different entropy distributions.
  - Only HumanEval and MBPP are used (Section 4.1), both consisting of short, self-contained tasks, which narrows the impact of the findings and leaves open whether the failure is task-specific.
  - No analysis is provided on how entropy sparsity varies across different programming languages or problem complexities, limiting the clarity of the 88.3% zero-entropy finding (Section 4.4) and its broader applicability.
- **Insufficient Exploration of Alternative Entropy Formulations**
  - The paper tests only nEDIS and nEDIS_v2 without exploring smoothed entropy trajectories or semantic entropy (Section 3.3, Section 2), which reduces technical soundness by leaving viable mitigation strategies unexamined.
  - The optimization to nEDIS_v2 is presented as a post-hoc fix rather than a principled adaptation, and no ablation studies justify why multiplying by T and inverting the direction was chosen over other normalization strategies (Equation 4, Section 3.3), weakening methodological rigor.
  - The failure of Mean_H and CV_H baselines (Table 1) is noted, but the paper does not investigate whether combining entropy dynamics with syntactic features could rescue the signal, limiting the novelty and practical impact of the negative result.
- **Lack of Theoretical or Empirical Analysis on Entropy Sparsity Causes**
  - While entropy sparsity is identified as a key failure mode (Section 4.4), the paper does not investigate whether it stems from temperature settings, top-p filtering, or inherent model calibration issues, which reduces the diagnostic clarity and actionable value of the finding.
  - No comparison is made with self-certainty or calibration metrics (Spiess et al., 2024) to determine if the zero-entropy phenomenon correlates with overconfidence or correct token prediction, limiting the technical depth of the analysis.
  - The claim that nEDIS_v2 captures "length bias" (Section 4.4) is asserted but not formally validated; a correlation analysis between nEDIS_v2 scores and sequence length across candidates is missing, which weakens the empirical rigor of this interpretation.
- **Inconsistency Between Text and Figure 1 Visualization**
  - Figure 1 diagram explicitly labels the selection step as "argmin nEDIS" with "Lower = More Reliable", directly contradicting the textual description of nEDIS_v2 which uses "argmax selection" (Section 3.3), which undermines clarity and could mislead readers.
  - The diagram displays the original nEDIS formula without the length multiplication ($\cdot T$) defined in Equation 4 for nEDIS_v2, failing to visually represent the actual method evaluated in the experiments.
  - This visual-text mismatch reduces the reproducibility of the pipeline, as practitioners relying solely on the figure would implement the incorrect selection logic and scoring formula.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope and Generalizability Analysis**
  - Include at least two additional code generation models of varying sizes and training paradigms (e.g., StarCoder2, CodeLlama) to verify whether entropy sparsity and nEDIS failure are model-agnostic or specific to DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-Instruct.
  - Evaluate on longer-form or multi-file code generation benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench Lite or APPS) to determine if entropy dynamics become more informative in complex, multi-step coding tasks.
  - Provide a breakdown of entropy sparsity rates across different problem categories or difficulty levels to clarify whether the 88.3% zero-entropy finding holds uniformly or varies by task structure.
- **Systematically Explore Alternative Entropy Aggregation Strategies**
  - Implement and test smoothed entropy trajectories (e.g., moving averages or exponential smoothing) to mitigate the impact of discrete zero-entropy tokens before spike detection, comparing performance against nEDIS_v2.
  - Conduct an ablation study on the nEDIS_v2 formulation, testing alternative length normalizations (e.g., log(T), sqrt(T)) and selection directions to identify whether the observed improvement stems from length correlation or genuine instability signal.
  - Investigate hybrid scoring functions that combine entropy dynamics with lightweight syntactic checks (e.g., AST validity, indentation consistency) to determine if structural priors can compensate for sparse entropy signals.
- **Deepen Diagnostic Analysis of Entropy Sparsity and Length Bias**
  - Perform a correlation analysis between per-token entropy values and token prediction accuracy (using held-out test tokens or self-consistency checks) to determine whether zero-entropy tokens correspond to correct predictions or model overconfidence.
  - Quantify the relationship between nEDIS_v2 scores and sequence length by computing Pearson/Spearman correlations across all candidates, providing empirical evidence for or against the length bias hypothesis.
  - Analyze the impact of generation hyperparameters (temperature, top-p) on entropy sparsity rates, as lower temperatures or stricter top-p thresholds may artificially inflate zero-entropy counts, offering practical guidance for future uncertainty-based selection methods.
- **Correct Figure 1 and Align Visuals with Textual Descriptions**
  - Update the "Best-of-N Selection" box in Figure 1 to explicitly show "argmax nEDIS_v2" and "Higher = More Reliable" to match the methodology described in Section 3.3.
  - Revise the formula in the diagram to include the length multiplication ($\cdot T$) as specified in Equation 4, ensuring the visual accurately represents the evaluated method rather than the original nEDIS.
  - Add a brief caption note clarifying the transition from original nEDIS (argmin) to nEDIS_v2 (argmax) to prevent reader confusion and improve reproducibility.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper delivers a methodologically rigorous negative result with clear pre-registered criteria and reproducible code (Section 3.4, Appendix A), but its impact is limited by narrow model/benchmark scope and a notable inconsistency between the text and Figure 1.
- Novelty (10): 5 — The core contribution is a well-executed negative result testing an existing method (EDIS) on a new domain (Section 1, Section 3.3), which is valuable but does not introduce a new algorithm or theoretical framework.
- Technical Quality (10): 7 — The experimental design, statistical testing, and failure mode analysis are strong (Table 2, Section 4.4), though the lack of ablation on nEDIS_v2 and deeper diagnostic analysis of entropy sparsity slightly reduces technical depth.
- Clarity (10): 7 — The manuscript is well-structured with explicit success criteria and logical flow (Section 3.1, Section 4.2), but the contradiction between the argmax text description and the argmin diagram in Figure 1 detracts from overall clarity.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence in the validity of the negative result and experimental setup (Section 4.1, Table 1), though limited by single-model evaluation and the need to verify the corrected pipeline visualization.
