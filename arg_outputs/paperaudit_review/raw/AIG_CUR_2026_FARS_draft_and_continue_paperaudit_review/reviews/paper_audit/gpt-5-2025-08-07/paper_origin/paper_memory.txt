# Global Summary
Problem: Self-consistency (SC) boosts LLM reasoning by sampling multiple chain-of-thought (CoT) paths and majority voting, but generates many full solutions, increasing token cost. The paper asks whether adaptive compute allocation can improve efficiency by identifying promising reasoning paths early and continuing only those.

Core approach: Draft-and-Continue Self-Consistency (DCS), a two-stage branch-budgeting method. Stage 1 samples B = 7 short drafts (token limit T_draft = 1024), extracts interim answers, and builds a vote histogram. Stage 2 continues k = 3 branches for T_cont = 1024 tokens using a hedge allocation that reserves one continuation for the second-most-voted answer. Final prediction uses majority vote over all completed responses.

Evaluation scope: MATH-500 (500 math problems, exact match), model Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct. Baselines: Greedy CoT (temperature 0, max tokens 2048), Uniform SC (n = 5, temp 0.8, top-p 0.95), CGES-LNS (confidence-guided early stopping; b_max = 5, confidence threshold γ = 0.95). Metrics: accuracy, average generated tokens/problem, average API calls/problem. Results reported over 3 seeds (42, 123, 456) as mean ± std.

# Abstract
- Goal: Reduce token costs of self-consistency by adaptively allocating continuation budget to promising reasoning paths identified early.
- Method: Draft-and-Continue Self-Consistency (DCS) samples short drafts, forms interim-answer vote histograms, and continues high-vote branches.
- Results on MATH-500 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct: DCS accuracy “76.8%,” matching baselines, but with “19.6%” more tokens than standard self-consistency and “109%” more than CGES-LNS; CGES-LNS Pareto-dominates DCS.
- Analysis: About “96%” of drafts complete within the token limit; continuation rarely activates meaningfully.

# Method
- Problem setup: Self-consistency samples n independent reasoning chains {(r_i, a_i)} and predicts by majority vote: â = argmax_a Σ_i 1(a_i = a).
- DCS pipeline:
  - Stage 1 (Draft Sampling): Sample B drafts, each limited to T_draft tokens. Extract interim answers a_i^{draft}. Non-parseable outputs assigned to NULL and excluded from continuation selection.
  - Vote Histogram: c(a) = Σ_{i=1}^B 1(a_i^{draft} = a). Let a^{(1)} and a^{(2)} be top-1 and top-2 answers by vote count.
  - Stage 2 (Continuation Allocation): Select k branches to continue for T_cont tokens. Hedge allocation: k − 1 branches from a^{(1)} and 1 from a^{(2)} (if it exists); otherwise all k from a^{(1)}. Branches within clusters selected arbitrarily.
  - Final Aggregation: Extract final answers from continued branches and apply majority vote. Approximate total token budget: B · T_draft + k · T_cont.
- Hyperparameters:
  - Defaults: B = 7, k = 3, T_draft = 1024, T_cont = 1024.
  - Approximate total budget per problem: 7 × 1024 + 3 × 1024 = 10,240 tokens.
  - Sampling settings: temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95.
- Figure described: Stage 1 (B=7, T_draft=1024) forms histogram; Stage 2 (k=3, T_cont=1024) continues with hedge; final majority vote.

# Discussion
- Insight: Effectiveness of two-stage branch budgeting depends on draft length vs. task complexity.
- With T_draft = 1024 on MATH-500, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct often completes solutions within the draft, limiting the utility of continuation.
- Limitations:
  - Single dataset (MATH-500) and single model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct).
  - Hyperparameters (B = 7, k = 3, T_draft = 1024) not extensively tuned.
- Implication stated: Finding likely generalizes to settings where the draft budget suffices for most problems. Future directions include shorter drafts or harder benchmarks.
