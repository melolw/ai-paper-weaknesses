## 1. Statistical Reporting Inconsistencies (High Impact)

There is a clear numerical mismatch between the reported mean differences and the centers of the reported 95% confidence intervals (CIs). In standard statistical reporting, the point estimate (mean difference) should correspond to the center of the confidence interval (or be the primary value from which the interval is derived).

*   **ESR vs. Self-Debug:**
    *   **Reported Mean Difference:** The text and Table 1 report a mean difference of **+0.61** percentage points (87.60% − 86.99%).
    *   **Reported CI:** The text states the 95% CI is **[−0.81, 2.24]**.
    *   **Inconsistency:** The center of the interval [−0.81, 2.24] is **0.715** ($(2.24 + (-0.81)) / 2$). This does not match the reported point estimate of 0.61. The discrepancy is approximately 0.105 percentage points.
*   **ESR vs. Best-of-16:**
    *   **Reported Mean Difference:** The text and Table 1 report a mean difference of **+1.63** percentage points (87.60% − 85.98%).
    *   **Reported CI:** The text states the 95% CI is **[0.41, 3.05]**.
    *   **Inconsistency:** The center of the interval [0.41, 3.05] is **1.73** ($(3.05 + 0.41) / 2$). This does not match the reported point estimate of 1.63. The discrepancy is approximately 0.10 percentage points.

**Assessment:** The consistent offset (~0.1) between the reported mean differences and the CI centers suggests a systematic calculation error or a mismatch between the values used for the table/text and those used for the bootstrap CI calculation. This undermines the precision of the statistical claims.
