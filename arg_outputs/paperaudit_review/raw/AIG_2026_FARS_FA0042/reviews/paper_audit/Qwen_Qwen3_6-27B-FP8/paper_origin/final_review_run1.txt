1) Summary
The paper addresses the trade-off between embedding quality and streaming efficiency in text representation models. Bidirectional attention yields superior embeddings but breaks KV-cache compatibility, while causal models support efficient streaming but underperform. The authors propose a teacher-student distillation framework where a bidirectional teacher, trained via Gradient-Guided Soft Masking (GG-SM), transfers knowledge to a causal student through combined InfoNCE and MSE losses. The student maintains streaming compatibility via KV-cache reuse and incremental mean pooling. Evaluated on a 6-dataset MTEB slice and LoCoV1, the distilled student achieves 68.1% gap-closure relative to the teacher, outperforms Echo embeddings by 2.0 percentage points, and delivers a 4.1× mean streaming speedup. Task-type analysis reveals strong transfer for classification and clustering but limited gains for pair classification.2) Strengths
- **Clear Practical Motivation and Problem Formulation**
  - The paper explicitly identifies a critical production gap: bidirectional embeddings break KV-cache streaming compatibility, requiring $O(L+\Delta)$ recomputation (Section 3.1), which matters because it grounds the work in real-world constraints like conversational agents and recommendation systems (Section 1).
  - The mathematical formulation of incremental update complexity clearly contrasts causal $O(\Delta)$ vs. bidirectional $O(L+\Delta)$ costs (Section 3.1), which matters for establishing a rigorous theoretical baseline to quantify efficiency gains.
  - The streaming inference protocol using running mean pooling over cached hidden states is elegantly described and mathematically formalized (Equation 6, Section 3.4), which matters for ensuring reproducibility and clarifying how streaming compatibility is technically achieved.
- **Novel Cross-Architecture Distillation Framework**
  - The teacher-student setup bridges fundamentally different attention mechanisms (bidirectional to causal), which matters because it extends standard model compression by transferring quality across incompatible attention patterns (Section 2, Section 3.3).
  - The combined objective (InfoNCE + MSE distillation) effectively balances contrastive learning with embedding alignment, which matters for demonstrating that embedding-level distillation can recover most bidirectional quality gains (Table 1, Section 4.2).
  - The use of GG-SM for stable teacher training addresses the known instability of abrupt causal-to-bidirectional transitions, which matters for technical soundness by providing a controlled two-phase schedule detailed in Equations 1 and 2 (Section 3.2).
- **Comprehensive and Insightful Empirical Evaluation**
  - The latency analysis demonstrates constant ~22ms incremental updates versus linear growth for bidirectional models, quantifying a 4.1× mean speedup that scales to 5.5× at longer contexts (Figure 2, Section 4.3), which matters for validating the core streaming efficiency claim.
  - The long-context retrieval results on LoCoV1 reveal a surprising finding: the causal student outperforms the bidirectional teacher (0.284 vs 0.212 NDCG@10), which matters for impact by suggesting distillation transfers generalizable representation quality rather than merely mimicking attention patterns, while mitigating position-bias artifacts from short-sequence training (Table 2, Section 4.4).
  - The task-type gap-closure analysis provides nuanced insights into distillation limits, highlighting strong transfer for holistic tasks (classification: 1.03) but weak transfer for fine-grained pairwise comparison (0.30), which matters for clarity by accurately scoping the method's capabilities (Figure 3, Section 4.5).3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Model Scale and Dataset Coverage**
  - Evaluation relies solely on a 0.5B parameter model (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) and a single training dataset (sentence-transformers/all-nli), which matters for experimental rigor because it raises questions about scalability to larger LLMs and broader data distributions (Section 4.1).
  - The MTEB evaluation uses only a 6-dataset slice, which matters for impact because it may not fully represent the diversity of the full MTEB benchmark or capture edge-case retrieval scenarios (Section 4.1, Table 1).
  - No results are provided for models beyond 0.5B or for multilingual/cross-lingual embedding tasks, which matters for technical soundness as it limits claims about general applicability across model families and languages (Section 5).
- **Insufficient Ablation and Hyperparameter Analysis**
  - The distillation weight $\lambda = 0.5$ is fixed without ablation studies to justify its optimality, which matters for technical soundness because the trade-off between contrastive and distillation signals heavily influences final embedding geometry (Equation 5, Section 3.3).
  - The GG-SM training schedule parameters ($T_{\text{warm}}$, $T_{\text{total}}$) and gradient weighting function lack sensitivity analysis or comparison to alternative soft/hard masking strategies, which matters for clarity and reproducibility (Section 3.2).
  - The mean pooling strategy for streaming updates is not compared against alternatives like last-token or [CLS] pooling, which matters for experimental rigor because positional information loss may directly contribute to the observed pair classification weakness (Equation 6, Section 3.4).
- **Unaddressed Limitations in Task-Specific Transfer and Computational Overhead**
  - The significant drop in gap-closure for pair classification (0.30) is acknowledged but not mitigated, which matters for impact because fine-grained comparison tasks are critical for many production retrieval systems (Figure 3, Section 4.5).
  - The computational cost of training the bidirectional teacher (including GG-SM gradient computations) is not reported, which matters for experimental rigor as it prevents fair assessment of the overall distillation pipeline efficiency (Section 3.2).
  - The comparison to Echo embeddings focuses on quality and latency but does not evaluate Echo under true streaming conditions or analyze its KV-cache compatibility claims in depth, which matters for technical soundness to ensure a direct apples-to-apples efficiency comparison (Section 1, Table 1).4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scale and Dataset Diversity**
  - Report results on larger model scales (e.g., 1.5B, 7B) to validate scalability and assess whether gap-closure ratios hold for more capable base models.
  - Evaluate on the full MTEB benchmark or include additional task categories (e.g., reranking, multilingual retrieval) to strengthen claims about general embedding quality.
  - Include cross-lingual or domain-specific datasets to test robustness beyond the English NLI training data.
- **Conduct Targeted Ablations and Hyperparameter Studies**
  - Perform a grid search or learning curve analysis for $\lambda$ to identify the optimal balance between InfoNCE and MSE losses, and report sensitivity to this weighting.
  - Compare GG-SM against baseline masking schedules (e.g., linear soft masking, hard bidirectional switch) to isolate the contribution of gradient-guided weighting to teacher stability and quality.
  - Benchmark alternative pooling strategies (last-token, [CLS], attention-weighted pooling) under streaming conditions to determine if positional encoding preservation improves pair classification performance.
- **Address Task-Specific Weaknesses and Report Full Pipeline Costs**
  - Explore token-level distillation or attention pattern matching (e.g., KL divergence over attention matrices) to better transfer fine-grained pairwise comparison capabilities to the causal student.
  - Provide a detailed breakdown of teacher training compute (FLOPs, GPU hours, gradient computation overhead for GG-SM) to enable fair comparison with training-free or single-model baselines.
  - Clarify the streaming compatibility of Echo embeddings by testing incremental update latency under KV-cache constraints, ensuring a direct apples-to-apples efficiency comparison.5) Score
- Overall (10): 7 — The paper presents a well-motivated distillation framework with strong empirical results on streaming efficiency and quality gap-closure (Table 1, Figure 2), though limited model scale and insufficient ablation temper the overall impact.
- Novelty (10): 7 — The cross-attention distillation setup and GG-SM teacher training offer a clear methodological contribution (Section 3.2, Section 3.3), but the core distillation paradigm builds on established embedding compression techniques.
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The mathematical formulation and streaming protocol are sound (Equations 1–6), yet the lack of hyperparameter ablation, single-scale evaluation, and unaddressed pair classification weakness reduce technical rigor.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear problem formulation, intuitive figures (Figure 1, Figure 2), and precise experimental reporting, though some implementation details (e.g., GG-SM schedule parameters) require more specification.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence based on clear methodological descriptions, reproducible experimental setup, and explicit anchors to tables/figures, though limited scale evaluation slightly reduces certainty about broader generalizability.
