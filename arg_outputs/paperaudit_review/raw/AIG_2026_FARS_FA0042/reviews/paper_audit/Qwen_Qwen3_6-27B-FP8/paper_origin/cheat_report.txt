# Academic Integrity & Consistency Risk Report

## Executive Summary
The manuscript presents a coherent teacher-student distillation framework for streaming-compatible text embeddings. Core numerical claims, calculations, and experimental results are **highly consistent** across the abstract, main text, tables, and figures. Gap-closure ratios, speedup averages, and benchmark means are mathematically verified and align with reported values. However, a few **clear internal inconsistencies** and **ambiguous phrasings** were identified that require correction before publication. No evidence of data fabrication, selective reporting, or logical contradictions in the methodology was found.

---

## 1. Critical Inconsistencies & Logical Issues

### 🔴 Model Name Discrepancy (Text vs. Figure)
- **Issue:** Section 4.1 explicitly states the base model is `Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct`. However, Figure 1 labels the architecture as `Qwen2-5.0.5B + LoRA`.
- **Impact:** This is a direct factual mismatch between the experimental setup description and the primary methodological diagram. It likely stems from a typographical error in the figure but undermines precision.
- **Anchor:** Section 4.1 (Experimental Setup) vs. Figure 1 (Method Diagram).

### 🔴 "Numerically Identical" vs. Non-Zero Cosine Distance
- **Issue:** Section 4.3 claims that incremental updates produce `"numerically identical embeddings to full recomputation"`, but immediately qualifies this with `"(maximum cosine distance 1.19 × 10⁻⁶)"`. A non-zero distance mathematically contradicts the term "numerically identical."
- **Impact:** While the distance is negligible for practical purposes, the phrasing is logically inconsistent. It should be revised to `"numerically equivalent within floating-point precision"` or similar.
- **Anchor:** Section 4.3 (Streaming Efficiency), paragraph 2.

### 🟡 Ambiguous Performance Claim Phrasing
- **Issue:** Section 4.4 states the distilled student outperforms baselines `"by 7.1 percentage points over the causal baseline."` This phrasing is grammatically ambiguous. It could be misread as comparing the student to the teacher, or implying a compound margin.
- **Verification:** Student (0.284) vs. Causal (0.213) = 0.071 (7.1 pts). Student vs. Teacher (0.212) = 0.072 (7.2 pts). The numbers align with the Student vs. Causal comparison, but the sentence structure obscures this.
- **Anchor:** Section 4.4 (Long-Context Evaluation), paragraph 1.
