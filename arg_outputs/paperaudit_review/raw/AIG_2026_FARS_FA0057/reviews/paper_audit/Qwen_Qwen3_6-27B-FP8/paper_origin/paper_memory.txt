# Global Summary

This paper introduces LiveMedBench-Ask1, a controlled evaluation protocol designed to measure whether allowing medical Large Language Models (LLMs) to ask a single clarifying question improves the quality of their medical advice. The study addresses the gap between static medical benchmarks and clinical practice, where physicians routinely ask clarifying questions.

The authors evaluate GPT-4.1 and Qwen3-14B on a subset of 657 cases from LiveMedBench where exactly one critical patient information slot is masked. Models are tested under three conditions: Masked Baseline (A), Ask1 Protocol with a deterministic slot oracle (B), and Unmasked Upper Bound (C).

Key findings indicate a negative result: while both models demonstrate the ability to ask targeted questions at rates well above chance (50.1% and 37.8% slot hit rates, respectively), this capability does not translate into improved rubric scores. The confidence intervals for the improvement (B-A) span zero for both models. The authors attribute this to minimal information headroom, noting that the gap between the unmasked and masked conditions (C-A) is only 0.6–0.9 percentage points, leaving insufficient room for interactive protocols to demonstrate benefit. The paper concludes that single-slot masking creates insufficient information gaps and that models struggle to integrate newly obtained information even when they ask the correct question.

# Abstract

- **Problem:** Medical LLMs often provide advice based on incomplete information without asking clarifying questions, unlike clinicians.
- **Method:** Introduction of LiveMedBench-Ask1, a protocol where models may ask one clarifying question answered by a deterministic slot oracle.
- **Dataset:** 657 cases with one masked critical patient slot.
- **Models:** GPT-4.1 and Qwen3-14B.
- **Conditions:** Masked baseline (A), Ask1 protocol (B), unmasked upper bound (C).
- **Results:**
    - Models ask targeted questions at rates well above chance: 50.1% slot hit rate for GPT-4.1 and 37.8% for Qwen3-14B.
    - No improvement in rubric scores: B-A confidence intervals span zero for both models.
    - Minimal information headroom: The C-A gap is only 0.6–0.9 percentage points.
- **Conclusion:** Single-slot masking creates insufficient information gaps for interactive protocols to demonstrate benefit. Even when models identify the masked slot, they fail to leverage the information effectively.

# Method

- **Subset Construction:**
    - Source: LiveMedBench v202601 (2,756 cases).
    - Filtering: Cases where a single slot type is referenced in rubric criteria and can be redacted without making the case unsolvable.
    - Result: 657 qualifying cases (23.8% of original).
    - Language distribution: 336 English, 321 Chinese.

- **Experimental Conditions:**
    - **Condition A (Masked Baseline):** Model receives narrative with one slot redacted (e.g., "[PREGNANCY STATUS REDACTED]"). Single-turn response.
    - **Condition B (Ask1 Protocol):** Model receives masked narrative and may ask one clarifying question. A deterministic slot oracle responds with the ground-truth value if the question matches the masked slot type, or indicates unavailability otherwise.
    - **Condition C (Unmasked Upper Bound):** Model receives complete narrative with no masking.

- **Slot Oracle Design:**
    - Case-insensitive matching against eight predefined slot types.
    - Returns ground-truth value for matches; returns "The requested information is not available in the patient record" for non-matches.

- **Evaluation:**
    - Framework: LiveMedBench rubric-based evaluation using an LLM-as-judge (GPT-4.1 with temperature=0).
    - Scoring: Fraction of satisfied rubric criteria (0-1 scale). Average of 6.2 rubric criteria per case.
    - Metrics:
        1. Mean rubric score (Conditions A, B, C).
        2. B-A improvement (Ask1 vs Masked).
        3. C-A headroom (Unmasked vs Masked).
        4. Slot hit rate (fraction of cases where question matched masked slot).
        5. Ask rate (fraction of cases where model asked a question).

# Discussion

- **Primary Findings:**
    - The Ask1 slot-oracle protocol does not improve medical LLM performance.
    - **Minimal Information Headroom:** The C-A gap of 0.6–0.9 percentage points indicates that masking one slot has limited impact on rubric scores. This suggests rubric criteria may not be sensitive enough to the masked information, or models can infer/work around the missing info.
    - **Integration Bottleneck:** Models can identify missing information (high slot hit rates) but fail to leverage it effectively. Correctly identifying the slot does not translate to larger score improvements.

- **Limitations:**
    - Only eight slot types evaluated.
    - Single-turn protocol with one clarifying question is constrained compared to real clinical consultations.
    - Deterministic oracle does not reflect realistic noisy/incomplete information scenarios.
    - Single LLM grader (GPT-4.1) used; inter-grader reliability not assessed.

- **Future Directions:**
    - Multi-slot masking to create larger information gaps.
    - Multi-turn protocols for iterative refinement.
    - Focus on specific high-signal slot types like pregnancy_status.
