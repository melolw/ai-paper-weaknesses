1) Summary
The paper addresses rollout drift in LLM-based world models for web agents, where simulated trajectories progressively diverge from real environment dynamics. Through diagnostic analysis on WebShop, the authors identify search-result simulation as the primary failure mode, with 100% of initial divergences occurring at search observations and a 99.8% per-step divergence rate (Section 4.3, Figure 2). To mitigate this, they propose search-anchored hybrid rollouts, a minimal intervention that intercepts search actions during simulation and substitutes world model predictions with real BM25-based search results, while retaining simulation for all other observations. Experiments demonstrate that anchoring all searches improves the Consistency Ratio from 0.594 to 0.824 (+38.7%), significantly outperforming agent-side baselines like Best-of-N and ReAct prompting (Table 1). Stratified analysis confirms that compounding search errors drive drift, with multi-search episodes benefiting most (Table 2, Figure 3). The work highlights targeted observation grounding as an effective strategy for improving world model fidelity without architectural modifications.2) Strengths
- **Precise Diagnostic Identification of the Dominant Failure Mode**
  - The manuscript provides a rigorous error analysis showing that 100% of first divergences occur at search-result observations (Section 4.3, Figure 2), which matters because it shifts the research focus from general world model improvement to targeted, high-impact intervention.
  - The per-step divergence rate quantification reveals a 99.8% failure rate for search results versus 50.9% for product details (Figure 2), offering strong empirical justification that retrieval simulation, not state prediction, is the primary bottleneck.
  - The stratified analysis by search count demonstrates that compounding errors are the root cause, as first-search anchoring yields negligible gains (CR=0.598) while all-search anchoring significantly improves performance (Table 2), validating the error propagation hypothesis and guiding practical deployment.
- **Minimal and Computationally Efficient Intervention Design**
  - The method bypasses only search actions while retaining simulation for structured state updates (Section 3.3, Figure 1), which matters because it preserves the computational advantages of world models without requiring retraining, architectural changes, or increased inference latency.
  - The use of a deterministic BM25 query cache ensures reproducibility and eliminates repeated real-environment API calls during evaluation (Section 3.3), enhancing experimental rigor and deployment feasibility in resource-constrained settings.
  - The approach cleanly separates high-entropy retrieval channels from low-entropy navigation dynamics (Section 3.3), providing a principled decomposition that aligns with hybrid simulation principles in reinforcement learning and offers a reusable design pattern for other domains.
- **Comprehensive and Well-Structured Experimental Evaluation**
  - The comparison against both agent-side baselines (Best-of-N, ReAct) and observation-side variants (first vs. all search anchoring) in Table 1 isolates the specific contribution of observation grounding, demonstrating technical soundness in experimental design.
  - The inclusion of variance metrics (mean±std) across three random seeds (Section 4.1, Table 1) demonstrates methodological rigor and accounts for stochastic agent sampling, improving result reliability.
  - The stratified analysis by episode complexity (Table 2, Figure 3) provides nuanced insights into where the method excels, confirming that the 90% of multi-search tasks drive the overall performance gains and strengthening the impact claim.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Generalization Beyond the WebShop Benchmark**
  - The evaluation is exclusively conducted on WebShop (Section 4.1), which matters because the BM25-based deterministic search mechanism may not translate to dynamic, API-driven, or visually grounded environments like WebArena or Mind2Web, limiting broader applicability.
  - The manuscript acknowledges this limitation but provides no preliminary experiments or theoretical discussion on how search anchoring would interact with non-deterministic or multi-modal search interfaces (Section 5, Limitations), reducing confidence in cross-domain transfer.
  - The claim that search-result simulation is a universal failure mode (Section 3.2) lacks cross-benchmark validation, weakening the generalizability of the proposed intervention and its stated impact on web agent research.
- **Insufficient Ablation on World Model Capacity and Training Regimes**
  - The world model is fixed as Qwen2.5-7B fine-tuned on WebShop trajectories (Section 3.4), but no ablation studies examine how varying model size or training data volume affects the 99.8% search divergence rate, leaving open whether targeted grounding is necessary or merely compensates for under-training.
  - The manuscript does not report whether increasing world model parameters or extending training steps reduces search hallucination, which matters because it obscures the trade-off between computational cost of larger models versus the overhead of hybrid grounding.
  - No comparison is made against retrieval-augmented world models (e.g., R-WoM mentioned in Section 2), making it unclear how search anchoring compares to existing grounding techniques and limiting the novelty assessment.
- **Ambiguity in Consistency Ratio Interpretation and Baseline Fairness**
  - The Consistency Ratio (CR) is defined as W2R/Real (Equation 1), but the Real success rate varies across conditions (e.g., 22.8% for Pure WM vs. 30.3% for ReAct variants in Table 1), which complicates direct CR comparisons across agent-side vs. observation-side methods and reduces metric clarity.
  - The manuscript does not clarify whether the Real environment success rate is held constant or re-evaluated per agent configuration, potentially conflating agent capability improvements with world model fidelity gains and affecting experimental rigor.
  - The variance reduction claim (std=0.018 vs. 0.062 for ReAct alone in Section 4.2) lacks statistical significance testing, making it difficult to verify whether the stability improvement is robust or seed-dependent, which matters for reproducibility.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation to Additional Web Environments**
  - Include preliminary experiments on at least one additional benchmark (e.g., WebArena or a simplified API-based environment) to assess whether search anchoring generalizes beyond BM25-based retrieval systems.
  - Discuss how the method would adapt to non-deterministic search results or dynamic web pages, potentially by introducing caching strategies or probabilistic grounding mechanisms to address the limitation noted in Section 5.
  - Provide a theoretical or empirical analysis of how high-entropy observation channels manifest in other domains, strengthening the claim that search-result simulation is a broader failure mode as stated in Section 3.2.
- **Conduct Systematic Ablations on World Model Configuration**
  - Report results with varying world model sizes (e.g., 1.5B, 7B, 14B) or training data scales to determine whether search divergence decreases with increased capacity, clarifying the necessity of hybrid grounding versus model scaling.
  - Compare search anchoring against retrieval-augmented world modeling approaches (e.g., R-WoM) to position the method within the existing literature on grounding techniques as referenced in Section 2.
  - Include an ablation on cache freshness or query perturbation to evaluate robustness when real search results change over time or contain noise, addressing the deterministic assumption in Section 3.3.
- **Clarify Metric Definitions and Statistical Rigor**
  - Standardize the Real success rate across all conditions or explicitly report how agent-side improvements affect the denominator in Equation 1, ensuring fair comparison of Consistency Ratio values across Table 1.
  - Add statistical significance tests (e.g., paired t-tests or bootstrap confidence intervals) for variance claims and CR improvements to verify robustness across seeds, addressing the concern raised in Section 4.2.
  - Provide a breakdown of W2R failures to distinguish between world model prediction errors and agent execution errors, improving interpretability of the consistency metric defined in Section 3.1.5) Score
- Overall (10): 7 — The paper presents a well-motivated, minimal intervention that effectively addresses a clearly identified failure mode in world model rollouts, though generalization and ablation depth limit broader impact (Sections 3.3, 4.2, 5).
- Novelty (10): 6 — The targeted grounding of high-entropy search observations is a practical and insightful contribution, but the hybrid simulation concept builds directly on established RL principles without substantial algorithmic innovation (Section 2, 3.3).
- Technical Quality (10): 7 — The experimental design is rigorous with clear baselines and stratified analysis, yet the lack of cross-benchmark validation and world model ablations reduces technical completeness (Table 1, 2, Section 4.3).
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured, with precise problem formulation, clear architectural diagrams, and concise reporting of results and limitations (Figure 1, Section 3.1, 5).
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence due to clear methodology, reproducible setup, and transparent reporting, though limited to a single benchmark and fixed model configuration (Section 3.4, 4.1).
