1) Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of selecting which transformer layer to fine-tune for factual model editing. The authors propose GradRatio-Select, a gradient-based method that scores candidate layers using the ratio of edit-to-retain gradient magnitudes ($S(\ell) = \|g_{edit}\|^2 / \|g_{retain}\|^2$). An adaptive threshold excludes early layers to prevent catastrophic capability loss. Evaluated on Qwen2.5-7B and LLaMA-3-8B using the ZsRE dataset and WILD evaluation protocol, the method automatically identifies layers adjacent to manually-tuned heuristics. On Qwen2.5-7B, performance matches the heuristic baseline across reliability, generalization, and capability metrics. On LLaMA-3-8B, the method selects an adjacent layer but suffers a 5.26 percentage point capability degradation, primarily driven by mathematical reasoning tasks. The authors conclude that gradient-based selection automates layer identification but does not surpass carefully-tuned heuristics.2) Strengths
- **Clear and Principled Methodological Formulation**
  - The explicit mathematical definition of edit and retain objectives (Section 2.1, Equations 1–2) establishes a transparent optimization framework, ensuring the method’s goals are unambiguous and reproducible.
  - The GradRatio score formulation (Section 2.2, Equation 4) directly quantifies the trade-off between edit responsiveness and capability preservation, enhancing technical soundness by grounding layer selection in observable gradient dynamics.
  - Restricting candidates to MLP down-projection matrices (Section 2.3) aligns with established knowledge localization literature, ensuring the method targets empirically validated parameter subsets rather than searching the entire weight space.
- **Rigorous and Transparent Experimental Evaluation**
  - Comprehensive evaluation across two distinct model families (Section 3.1) using standardized WILD metrics (Table 1) demonstrates robust experimental design and facilitates direct comparison with prior fine-tuning baselines.
  - Detailed per-benchmark capability breakdown (Table 2) isolates the source of performance degradation to mathematical reasoning, providing actionable diagnostic insights that clarify the method’s failure modes.
  - Reporting mean ± standard deviation across three random seeds (Section 3.1) and explicitly noting statistical indistinguishability on Qwen2.5-7B strengthens reproducibility and prevents overclaiming on marginal gains.
- **Insightful Ablation and Diagnostic Analysis**
  - The ablation on the cosine similarity term (Section 3.5) empirically justifies the simplified ratio formulation by demonstrating near-zero gradient alignment across layers, preventing unnecessary model complexity.
  - Quantifying the impact of the adaptive threshold (Section 3.5) shows a 21.90pp capability recovery on LLaMA-3-8B, validating the necessity of early-layer exclusion and highlighting a critical design component.
  - Visualizing GradRatio scores across layers (Figure 2) clearly illustrates the scoring landscape and the threshold’s role in preventing selection of structurally critical early layers, improving interpretability.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Generalizability and Model-Specific Sensitivity**
  - The method fails to match heuristic performance on LLaMA-3-8B, showing a 5.26pp capability drop (Table 1), indicating the gradient ratio may not capture architecture-specific knowledge distribution patterns.
  - The catastrophic drop on GSM8K (−23.86pp, Table 2) suggests the scoring metric is highly sensitive to task-specific layer dependencies, limiting reliability for reasoning-heavy edits.
  - Evaluation is restricted to only two 7B/8B models (Section 3.1), leaving open questions about scalability to larger parameter counts or different architectural families, which constrains the claimed automation benefit.
- **Heuristic Nature of the Adaptive Threshold**
  - The threshold formula $\ell_{\min} = \max(3, \lceil 0.15 \cdot L \rceil)$ (Equation 5) lacks theoretical grounding and appears manually tuned rather than derived from gradient properties, undermining the principled automation claim.
  - The fixed 15% exclusion rule may be suboptimal for models with different depth-to-capability mappings, as no sensitivity analysis on the threshold parameter is provided (Section 2.3).
  - Relying on a static percentage rather than a data-driven or gradient-adaptive cutoff introduces a hidden hyperparameter that must be re-tuned for new model depths, contradicting the goal of heuristic-free selection.
- **Insufficient Analysis of Gradient Dynamics and Computational Overhead**
  - The paper does not analyze how gradient magnitudes scale with batch size or sequence length, which could affect score stability (Section 2.2 mentions using 32 edit/256 retain samples but provides no ablation).
  - While claiming computational efficiency over layer sweeps (Section 2.3), the method requires two full forward-backward passes per scoring step, with no empirical comparison of wall-clock time or FLOPs against the baseline heuristic search.
  - The high variance in GSM8K results (std=8.22, Section 3.3) is noted but not investigated, leaving uncertainty about whether the gradient ratio is inherently noisy for certain knowledge types or requires gradient smoothing.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope and Task Diversity**
  - Evaluate GradRatio-Select on at least two additional model families (e.g., Mistral, Gemma) and varying scales (e.g., 13B, 70B) to assess cross-architecture generalizability beyond the current setup in Section 3.1.
  - Incorporate reasoning-heavy and multi-hop editing benchmarks beyond GSM8K to determine if the capability degradation is specific to mathematical tasks or generalizes to complex reasoning, addressing the gap highlighted in Table 2.
  - Provide a statistical significance test (e.g., paired t-test or bootstrap CI) across all benchmarks to formally quantify whether the LLaMA-3-8B degradation is robust or seed-dependent, strengthening the claims in Table 1.
- **Derive a Principled or Adaptive Threshold Mechanism**
  - Replace the fixed 15% rule with a gradient-statistics-based cutoff, such as excluding layers where $\|g_{retain}\|^2$ falls below a percentile of the layer-wise distribution, to align with the gradient-based philosophy in Equation 4.
  - Conduct a sensitivity analysis sweeping $\ell_{\min}$ from 0.05L to 0.30L to identify the optimal exclusion range and report its impact on capability preservation, addressing the missing analysis in Section 2.3.
  - Justify the threshold theoretically by linking early-layer gradient norms to known representational bottlenecks or information flow properties in transformers, providing a principled alternative to the heuristic in Equation 5.
- **Analyze Gradient Stability and Computational Efficiency**
  - Perform an ablation study varying the number of edit/retain samples used for gradient estimation to establish the minimum data requirement for stable GradRatio scores, complementing the setup in Section 2.2.
  - Report empirical runtime and memory overhead for the two-pass gradient computation compared to a standard layer sweep, validating the claimed efficiency advantage stated in Section 2.3.
  - Investigate the source of high GSM8K variance by analyzing per-seed gradient trajectories or employing gradient smoothing techniques to improve score reliability for reasoning tasks, addressing the observation in Section 3.3.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The method provides a clear gradient-based automation for layer selection but shows inconsistent capability preservation across architectures, as evidenced by the 5.26pp degradation on LLaMA-3-8B (Table 1).
- Novelty (10): 5 — The ratio of edit-to-retain gradient magnitudes is a straightforward adaptation of existing gradient analysis techniques, lacking a fundamentally new theoretical insight beyond the adaptive threshold (Section 2.2, Equation 4).
- Technical Quality (10): 7 — The experimental setup is rigorous with proper baselines and ablations (Section 3.5), though the heuristic threshold (Equation 5) and limited model scope constrain technical depth.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear mathematical formulations (Section 2.1-2.3) and effective visualizations (Figure 2), making the method and results easy to follow.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence due to clear methodology and reproducible experimental details, though the automated generation warning and empty appendix slightly reduce certainty about omitted analyses.
