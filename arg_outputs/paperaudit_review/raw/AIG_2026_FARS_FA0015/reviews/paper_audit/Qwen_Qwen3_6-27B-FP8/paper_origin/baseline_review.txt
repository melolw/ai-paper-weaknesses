1) Summary
The paper addresses capability degradation ("brain rot") in large language models during continual pre-training on low-quality social media data. The authors propose Orthogonal Junk, a three-stage data selection pipeline that filters training samples based on the orthogonality of their gradients to a capability-preserving anchor gradient computed from diverse benchmarks (Section 2.3). Experiments on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct show mixed outcomes: the method yields modest gains in long-context understanding (RULER: +2.89pp) but degrades reasoning performance (ARC-Challenge: −5.12pp) compared to random selection (Table 1). A simple perplexity baseline outperforms the proposed method on RULER (+8.05pp). The authors identify data repetition as a dominant confound, demonstrating that reducing repetition significantly improves reasoning scores while leaving long-context performance largely unchanged (Table 3, Figure 2). The work concludes that gradient orthogonality at the LM head may not fully capture capability preservation dynamics (Section 5).2) Strengths
- **Clear Problem Formulation & Theoretical Motivation**
  - The constrained optimization objective for capability preservation is explicitly defined in Eq. 2 (Section 2.1), which grounds the empirical work in a formal mathematical framework and enhances technical soundness.
  - The gradient orthogonality hypothesis is derived from a first-order Taylor approximation in Eq. 3 (Section 2.2), providing a theoretically motivated rationale that connects continual learning principles to data selection.
  - The paper clearly links the "brain rot" phenomenon to practical continual pre-training scenarios by citing recent empirical evidence (Section 1, Xing et al., 2025), establishing clear real-world relevance and impact.
- **Rigorous Experimental Design & Ablation**
  - Multiple well-chosen baselines (Junk-Random, Perplexity, Control SFT) are included in Table 1, enabling fair comparison against both naive and quality-filtered approaches and strengthening experimental rigor.
  - A detailed RULER subtask breakdown in Table 2 reveals nuanced performance differences across retrieval, aggregation, and tracking tasks, which enhances diagnostic value and clarifies where the method succeeds or fails.
  - The systematic analysis of the data repetition confound through three optimization variants (Table 3, Figure 2) demonstrates careful control of experimental variables, isolating the impact of subset selection diversity from selection quality.
- **Transparent Reporting of Negative & Mixed Results**
  - The authors honestly report that the proposed method degrades ARC-Challenge performance by −5.12pp compared to random selection (Table 1, Section 3.2), avoiding selective reporting and fostering scientific integrity.
  - The manuscript explicitly acknowledges that a simpler perplexity baseline substantially outperforms the proposed method on RULER (+8.05pp) (Section 3.2), which guides future work toward more effective heuristics and improves impact.
  - Limitations of computing gradients only on the LM head and embedding layers are explicitly identified (Section 2.3, Section 3.4), providing actionable insights for methodological refinement and demonstrating constructive self-critique.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Theoretical Justification for Gradient Computation Scope**
  - The method computes gradients only on the LM head and embedding layers (Section 2.3), but provides no theoretical or empirical justification for why these layers alone suffice to capture capability-preserving dynamics, weakening technical soundness.
  - The first-order approximation in Eq. 3 assumes full-parameter gradients, yet the implementation restricts computation to 21.25% of parameters, creating a mismatch between the theoretical derivation and practical execution.
  - No ablation studies compare full-parameter gradient orthogonality against the restricted approach, leaving uncertainty about whether the observed limitations stem from the orthogonality criterion itself or the parameter subset choice.
- **Insufficient Analysis of Anchor Dataset Composition & Sensitivity**
  - The anchor dataset comprises 400 examples from GSM8K, MMLU, and Alpaca (Section 2.3), but no sensitivity analysis evaluates how varying the ratio or size of these benchmarks affects selection quality, limiting reproducibility and robustness.
  - The mean gradient aggregation in Eq. 4 assumes uniform importance across tasks, yet the paper does not investigate whether task-weighted or adaptive anchor construction would yield more robust orthogonality scores.
  - No discussion addresses potential bias introduced by the specific anchor examples, which could skew the orthogonality metric toward preserving certain capabilities while inadvertently harming others (e.g., ARC degradation in Table 1).
- **Overreliance on a Single Model Scale & Data Source**
  - All experiments are conducted on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (Section 3.1), limiting generalizability to larger models where brain rot dynamics and gradient geometry may differ significantly, reducing impact.
  - The junk data consists exclusively of engagement-optimized tweets (Section 3.1), raising questions about whether the method generalizes to other low-quality domains (e.g., forums, generated text, or multilingual noise).
  - The token budget is fixed at ~1.22M tokens across conditions, but no analysis explores how scaling the budget or adjusting the repetition rate independently affects the orthogonality selection advantage, as suggested by the flat RULER curve in Figure 2.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Gradient Computation Scope & Validate Theoretical Assumptions**
  - Conduct an ablation study comparing orthogonality scores computed on the LM head/embeddings versus full-parameter gradients to quantify the impact of parameter restriction on selection quality.
  - Provide a theoretical or empirical analysis justifying why first-order gradient orthogonality at the output layers correlates with capability preservation, potentially referencing layer-wise gradient alignment literature.
  - Investigate alternative gradient metrics (e.g., cosine similarity thresholds, gradient norm weighting, or higher-order approximations) to determine if the orthogonality criterion itself requires refinement.
- **Perform Anchor Dataset Sensitivity & Composition Analysis**
  - Evaluate the impact of varying anchor dataset sizes (e.g., 100, 400, 1000 examples) and task ratios to identify optimal configurations for balancing capability preservation.
  - Test task-weighted anchor gradient aggregation (e.g., inverse loss weighting or performance-gap weighting) to mitigate uniform averaging biases in Eq. 4.
  - Analyze how specific anchor tasks correlate with downstream performance shifts, particularly investigating whether GSM8K-heavy anchors contribute to the observed ARC-Challenge degradation in Table 1.
- **Broaden Experimental Scope & Scale Analysis**
  - Replicate key experiments on a larger model (e.g., 7B or 8B scale) to assess whether gradient orthogonality dynamics and brain rot vulnerability scale with model capacity.
  - Evaluate the method on diverse low-quality data sources beyond tweets (e.g., synthetic noise, multilingual social media, or web-crawl subsets) to test generalizability.
  - Decouple token budget from repetition rate by conducting experiments with fixed repetition constraints across varying pool sizes, clarifying the independent effects of diversity versus selection quality as hinted in Figure 2.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper presents a well-motivated data selection approach with rigorous ablation on repetition confounds (Table 3, Figure 2), but limited theoretical grounding for parameter-restricted gradients and mixed empirical results constrain its impact.
- Novelty (10): 5 — Applying gradient orthogonality to continual pre-training data selection is a reasonable extension of PCGrad/GEM principles (Section 2.2), yet the core idea closely follows existing gradient-based selection literature (Zhang et al., 2026) without substantial algorithmic innovation.
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The experimental design is thorough with multiple baselines and subtask breakdowns (Table 1, Table 2), but the restriction to LM-head gradients lacks justification (Section 2.3) and the single-model evaluation limits robustness.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear mathematical formulations (Eq. 1–5), intuitive pipeline visualization (Figure 1), and transparent reporting of negative results, facilitating easy comprehension.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence based on the complete method description, reproducible experimental setup, and explicit code availability, though limited model scale and data diversity slightly reduce certainty about generalizability.
