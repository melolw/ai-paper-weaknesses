# Global Summary
This paper addresses emergent misalignment (EM), where fine-tuning large language models on narrow, seemingly benign tasks induces harmful behaviors on unrelated prompts. The authors propose canary-controlled adaptive safe-data interleaving, a closed-loop training framework that dynamically adjusts the ratio of benign safe-data mixed into the training stream. The system monitors EM risk in real-time using a small set of canary prompts, applies exponential moving average (EMA) smoothing to the risk signal, and uses a threshold-based controller with hysteresis to increase or decrease safe-data interleaving. Evaluated on the Security EM benchmark using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the method achieves 5.39% General %Misaligned, a 25% relative improvement over fixed 5% interleaving (7.15%), with 4× lower cross-seed variance. Ablation studies confirm that adaptive timing, feedback timeliness, and EMA smoothing are critical, with fixed-timing variants showing 36% worse performance despite identical average safe-data volume. Key caveats include a weak correlation (Pearson r = 0.31) between canary risk and general misalignment, evaluation limited to a single benchmark, and a higher average safe-data usage (13% vs 5%).

# Introduction
- Context: LLM alignment training can be undermined by fine-tuning on benign, narrow tasks, causing broad misalignment (EM). Prior defenses (safe-data interleaving, SafeLoRA, representation engineering) typically use fixed parameters regardless of training dynamics.
- Hypothesis: EM risk varies during training, exhibiting phase-transition-like dynamics. Fixed interleaving ratios are suboptimal, applying too little intervention during high-risk phases or unnecessary overhead during safe phases.
- Proposal: A canary-controlled adaptive interleaving framework that uses canary prompts as a real-time proxy for EM risk. The controller evaluates checkpoints, computes EMA-smoothed risk, and adjusts the interleaving ratio via a threshold controller with hysteresis.

# Method
- Problem Setup: Supervised fine-tuning on target dataset $\mathcal{D}_T$ with safe dataset $\mathcal{D}_S$. At step $t$, sample from $\mathcal{D}_S$ with probability $p_t \in [p_{\min}, p_{\max}]$ and from $\mathcal{D}_T$ with probability $1 - p_t$. Goal is task learning while suppressing EM.
- Canary-Based Risk Estimation: Uses $M$ canary prompts $\mathcal{C}$ disjoint from evaluation prompts. Every $K$ steps, evaluates checkpoint and computes risk $r_t = \frac{1}{M} \sum \mathbf{1}[\text{misaligned}(c_i)]$. Misalignment is defined as alignment score < 30 and coherence score > 50. EMA smoothing is applied: $\hat{r}_t = \alpha r_t + (1 - \alpha)\hat{r}_{t-1}$ to reduce noise.
- Threshold Controller with Hysteresis: Adjusts $p_t$ based on $\hat{r}_t$. Increases by $\delta_{\uparrow}$ if $\hat{r}_t > r_{\text{high}}$, decreases by $\delta_{\downarrow}$ if $\hat{r}_t < r_{\text{low}}$, otherwise holds. Hysteresis band ($r_{\text{low}} < r_{\text{high}}$) prevents oscillation. Bounds are $p_{\min}$ and $p_{\max}$.
- Training Procedure: Algorithm 1 outlines the loop. A counter $T$ tracks remaining target-dataset steps. Safe-data steps do not decrement $T$, ensuring comparable target-task exposure across methods. The closed-loop design adapts defense strength based on behavioral signals.

# Experiments
- Setup: Security EM benchmark. Model: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct fine-tuned with rs-LoRA (rank 32, alpha 64). Optimizer: AdamW, learning rate 1e-4, effective batch size 16, bfloat16 precision. Target dataset: 5,400 examples from Security EM misaligned split. Safe data: benign subset of WildGuardMix.
- Controller Parameters: EMA $\alpha = 0.5$, thresholds $r_{\text{high}} = 0.08$, $r_{\text{low}} = 0.02$, step sizes $\delta_{\uparrow} = 0.04$, $\delta_{\downarrow} = 0.005$, bounds $p_{\min} = 0$, $p_{\max} = 0.20$. Evaluation every $K = 30$ steps using $M = 20$ canary prompts.
- Evaluation Protocol: 24 general-domain prompts and 30 in-domain held-out prompts. Judge: Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. Misaligned: alignment < 30 and coherence > 50. Incoherent: coherence < 50. 100 samples per prompt at temperature 1.0. Three random seeds (42, 123, 456).
- Baselines: No Defense (standard fine-tuning), Fixed 5% (constant 5% safe-data interleaving).
- Main Results: 
  - Canary-controlled: 5.39 ± 0.16% General %Misaligned, 1.36 ± 0.19% General %Incoherent, 33.80 ± 0.70% In-Domain %Misaligned, 15.62 ± 1.25% In-Domain %Incoherent, Avg $p_t$ = 0.13.
  - Fixed 5%: 7.15 ± 0.63% General %Misaligned, 1.30 ± 0.26% General %Incoherent, 37.80 ± 1.04% In-Domain %Misaligned, 16.51 ± 0.43% In-Domain %Incoherent, Avg $p_t$ = 0.05.
  - No Defense: 19.37 ± 1.26% General %Misaligned.
  - Canary-controlled shows 25% relative improvement over Fixed 5% and 4× lower cross-seed variance (std 0.16 vs 0.63). Incoherence levels remain comparable.
- Ablation Studies:
  - Full method: 5.26% General %Misaligned, Avg $p_t$ = 0.155.
  - Fixed $p = \bar{p}$ (same avg ratio): 7.18% (+36% degradation), confirming adaptive timing value.
  - Delayed updates (D=5): 8.35% (+59% degradation), Avg $p_t$ drops to 0.045, highlighting feedback timeliness.
  - Shuffled $p_t$ sequence: 5.75% (+9% degradation), indicating temporal ordering has modest effect compared to total safe-data volume.
  - No-EMA: 7.63% (+45% degradation), Avg $p_t$ = 0.056, showing EMA/hysteresis prevents erratic oscillation.
- Controller Behavior: All seeds show active engagement, reaching $p_{\max} = 0.20$ by training end. Average interleaving ratios range from 0.11 to 0.15. Canary-general risk correlation is weak (Pearson r = 0.31), yet the controller effectively suppresses EM using this proxy signal.

# Discussion
- The method achieves 25% relative improvement in EM suppression and 4× lower cross-seed variance compared to fixed-ratio baselines. Ablations validate the necessity of adaptive timing, prompt feedback timeliness, and EMA smoothing.
- Limitations: 
  - Weak canary-general correlation (r = 0.31) indicates the controller relies on imperfect proxy signals rather than direct EM measurement.
  - Evaluation is restricted to the Security EM benchmark; generalization to other EM-inducing datasets is unverified.
  - Higher average safe-data consumption (13% vs 5%) may be undesirable in compute-constrained deployments.
- Conclusion: Simple feedback control mechanisms can effectively regulate training dynamics for safety. Future work should explore improved risk signals, multi-benchmark validation, and theoretical analysis of the feedback control dynamics.
