### Summary
The paper investigates data selection strategies for repetition-heavy long chain-of-thought (CoT) supervised fine-tuning, specifically comparing easy-to-fit (low-NLL) and hard-to-fit (high-NLL) examples. Using OLMo3-7B and the Dolci-Think dataset, the authors train length-matched subsets of 800 examples for 32 epochs and evaluate on AIME 2024/2025 and GPQA Diamond. Contrary to initial intuition, high-NLL examples significantly outperform low-NLL examples in aggregate accuracy (33.0% vs. 23.6%) and termination rates. The authors identify a confound where low-NLL examples exhibit higher trigram repetition, leading to unstable training and poor termination. Optimization attempts, including hyperparameter tuning and trigram filtering, fail to close the performance gap, leading to the practical recommendation of selecting hard-to-fit examples for repetition-heavy regimes.### Strengths
- **Clear and Direct Experimental Comparison**
  - The paper establishes a controlled setup comparing low-NLL, high-NLL, and random selection under identical repetition-heavy training conditions (Section 3.4, Table 1), which matters because it isolates the effect of difficulty-based selection from confounding variables like dataset size or epoch count.
  - Length-matched selection via decile partitioning ensures that response length does not bias the NLL comparison (Section 3.3), which matters for technical soundness by preventing length-correlated perplexity from driving the results.
  - The consistent performance gap across mathematical (AIME) and scientific (GPQA) benchmarks (Table 1) matters because it demonstrates the robustness of the finding across distinct reasoning domains.
- **Identification of a Concrete Confound**
  - The analysis quantifies a significant trigram repetition disparity between low-NLL (0.457) and high-NLL (0.206) subsets (Section 5), which matters because it provides a mechanistic explanation for why easy-to-fit data fails in repetition-heavy regimes.
  - The strong correlation between selection strategy and termination rates (Figure 2, Table 1) matters because it links data properties directly to a critical failure mode in long-CoT generation (truncation vs. EOS).
  - The observation that low-NLL examples exhibit higher variance across seeds (Table 1) matters because it highlights the instability introduced by repetitive, low-perplexity data during many-epoch fine-tuning.
- **Rigorous Optimization Ablations**
  - The hyperparameter tuning experiment (reducing epochs, lowering LR, adding weight decay) systematically tests overfitting hypotheses (Table 2), which matters because it rules out superficial training misconfigurations as the cause of low-NLL failure.
  - The trigram filtering intervention directly targets the identified confound (Table 2), which matters for experimental rigor by demonstrating that removing repetitive examples alone is insufficient to recover performance.
  - The explicit reporting of delta metrics and termination rates for each optimization attempt (Table 2) matters because it provides transparent, quantitative evidence that the limitation is fundamental to the selection strategy.### Weaknesses
- **Limited Generalization Scope**
  - The evaluation relies exclusively on OLMo3-7B and the Dolci-Think dataset (Section 3.4), which matters because architectural differences or pre-training distributions could significantly alter NLL distributions and selection efficacy.
  - The study tests only one repetition-heavy configuration (32 epochs, 800 examples) (Section 3.4), which matters because the high-NLL advantage might not hold under different epoch/sample size trade-offs or update budgets.
  - The benchmarks are restricted to AIME and GPQA (Section 3.4), which matters because the findings may not extend to other reasoning modalities like code generation or multi-step planning.
- **Imbalanced Experimental Replication**
  - The main results report 3 seeds for Low-NLL and High-NLL but 6 seeds for Random (Table 1), which matters because asymmetric replication complicates variance estimation and direct statistical comparison.
  - The lack of formal statistical significance testing (e.g., p-values or confidence intervals beyond the bootstrap CI in Section 4.1) matters because it leaves the reliability of the 9.43-point gap unverified under standard statistical rigor.
  - The variance estimates for Low-NLL are notably large (e.g., ±12.6 on AIME 2024) (Table 1), which matters because high instability undermines confidence in the method's practical deployability without further replication.
- **Correlational Rather Than Causal Analysis of Confounds**
  - The trigram repetition analysis remains observational (Section 5), which matters because correlation does not prove that repetition directly causes the termination failure or accuracy drop.
  - The trigram filtering experiment improves termination but still degrades accuracy (Table 2), which matters because the paper does not explain whether the residual gap stems from remaining repetition, loss of diversity, or inherent NLL properties.
  - No controlled intervention matches trigram rates across subsets (Section 5), which matters because without causal isolation, it is unclear whether NLL itself is the optimal signal or merely a proxy for unmeasured data qualities.
- **Missing Evaluation and Decoding Details**
  - The manuscript omits decoding parameters (temperature, top-p, max tokens) for benchmark evaluation (Section 3.4), which matters because long-CoT generation is highly sensitive to sampling settings, affecting reproducibility.
  - The computation of Avg@16/Pass@16 and answer extraction methodology is not specified (Section 3.4), which matters because inconsistent parsing or voting strategies can significantly skew reported accuracy.
  - Validation checkpoint selection is mentioned but not detailed (Section 3.4), which matters because early stopping criteria and validation set composition directly influence final performance claims.### Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Generalization Testing**
  - Evaluate the selection strategies on at least one additional model architecture (e.g., Llama-3-8B or Qwen2.5-7B) to verify that the high-NLL advantage is not specific to OLMo3-7B's pre-training distribution.
  - Test alternative repetition-heavy configurations (e.g., 16 epochs on 1600 examples, or 64 epochs on 400 examples) to map the robustness of the finding across different update budgets.
  - Include at least one additional reasoning benchmark (e.g., MATH-500 or LiveCodeBench) to demonstrate that the high-NLL benefit extends beyond competition mathematics and graduate science.
- **Balance Seed Counts and Report Statistical Tests**
  - Run all three selection conditions with an identical number of seeds (e.g., 6 each) to ensure symmetric variance estimation and fair comparison across methods.
  - Apply formal statistical significance tests (e.g., paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests) to the seed-level results to rigorously validate the performance gaps reported in Table 1.
  - Report confidence intervals for all primary metrics, not just the aggregate accuracy, to provide a complete picture of result stability and reliability.
- **Deepen Causal Analysis of the Repetition Confound**
  - Conduct a controlled intervention where trigram repetition is synthetically matched or explicitly constrained across low-NLL and high-NLL subsets to isolate the causal effect of NLL from textual redundancy.
  - Apply diversity-aware sampling (e.g., embedding-based clustering or MinHash) to the low-NLL subset and compare results against the trigram filtering baseline to determine whether diversity or repetition is the primary driver of failure.
  - Analyze the NLL distribution and gradient norms across epochs for both subsets to provide an optimization-theoretic explanation for why high-NLL examples maintain informative signals under repetition.
- **Detail Evaluation and Decoding Protocols**
  - Explicitly state the decoding parameters (temperature, top-p, max generation length) and sampling strategy used for AIME and GPQA evaluations to ensure full reproducibility.
  - Clarify the exact computation of Avg@n and Pass@n, including whether self-consistency, majority voting, or exact match parsing is applied to extract final answers.
  - Specify the validation set composition, early stopping patience, and checkpoint selection criteria to eliminate ambiguity regarding how the best models were identified.### Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper presents a clear, counter-intuitive finding with practical value, but is limited by single-model/dataset evaluation and imbalanced experimental replication (Table 1, Section 5).
- Novelty (10): 7 — The focus on NLL-based selection specifically for repetition-heavy long-CoT SFT addresses an underexplored niche, though the core metric (NLL) is well-established in data selection literature (Section 2.1, Section 3.2).
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The experimental design is clean and includes useful ablations (Table 2), but lacks statistical rigor due to seed imbalance and missing decoding/evaluation details (Table 1, Section 3.4).
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured, with clear hypotheses, direct comparisons, and concise visualizations that effectively communicate the core findings (Figure 2, Section 4.1).
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence in the reported results and methodology, though limited by the single-model scope and missing evaluation protocol details (Section 3.4, Section 5).
