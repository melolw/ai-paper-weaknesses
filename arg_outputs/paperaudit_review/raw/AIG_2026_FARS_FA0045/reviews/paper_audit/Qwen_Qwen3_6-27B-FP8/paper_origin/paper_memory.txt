# Global Summary

This paper investigates data selection strategies for repetition-heavy long chain-of-thought (CoT) supervised fine-tuning. The authors compare selecting easy-to-fit examples (low negative log-likelihood, NLL) versus hard-to-fit examples (high NLL) under identical training conditions. Contrary to the intuition that easy examples might better teach formatting, high-NLL examples significantly outperform low-NLL examples, achieving 33.0% versus 23.6% aggregate accuracy on AIME and GPQA benchmarks. The analysis attributes the failure of low-NLL examples to a confound with high textual repetition (trigram rate 0.457 vs. 0.206), which leads to poor termination behavior (36.7% vs. 65.8% termination rate). Optimization attempts, including hyperparameter tuning and trigram filtering, failed to recover low-NLL performance. The study recommends selecting hard-to-fit examples for repetition-heavy training regimes.

# Abstract

- **Problem:** Determining which examples to select for repetition-heavy training (fine-tuning on small datasets for many epochs) in long-CoT reasoning.
- **Comparison:** Low-NLL (easy-to-fit) vs. High-NLL (hard-to-fit) examples.
- **Key Results:** High-NLL examples significantly outperform Low-NLL examples (33.0% vs. 23.6% aggregate accuracy).
- **Analysis:** Low-NLL examples are confounded with high textual repetition (trigram rate 0.457 vs. 0.206), resulting in poor termination behavior and unstable training dynamics.
- **Optimization:** Hyperparameter tuning and trigram filtering failed to recover Low-NLL performance, suggesting the limitation is fundamental to the selection strategy.
- **Conclusion:** Practitioners should select hard-to-fit examples rather than easy-to-fit ones for many-epoch repetition on small datasets.

# Experiments

- **Setup:**
    - Base Model: OLMo3-7B.
    - Dataset: Dolci-Think (filtered pool of 6,400 examples).
    - Training: 32 epochs on 800 length-matched examples (25,600 updates).
    - Benchmarks: AIME 2024, AIME 2025, GPQA Diamond.
    - Metrics: Avg@n, Pass@n, Termination rate (fraction of generations ending with EOS).

- **Main Results (Table 1):**
    - **High-NLL:** 33.00% Aggregate Accuracy.
    - **Random:** 28.66% Aggregate Accuracy.
    - **Low-NLL:** 23.57% Aggregate Accuracy.
    - High-NLL outperforms Low-NLL by 9.43 points (bootstrap 95% CI: [3.27, 20.36]).
    - Low-NLL exhibits high variance across seeds (e.g., ±12.6 on AIME 2024 vs. ±2.1 for High-NLL).

- **Termination Analysis (Table 1, Figure 2):**
    - High-NLL models achieve 65.8% average termination rate.
    - Random models achieve 48.4% average termination rate.
    - Low-NLL models achieve 36.7% average termination rate.
    - Low-NLL models frequently fail to emit end-of-sequence tokens, producing truncated outputs.

- **Optimization Attempts (Table 2):**
    - **Low-NLL (HP Tuned):** Reduced epochs (32→8), lowered LR (2e-5→1e-5), added weight decay (0.01). Result: 15.63% Aggregate Accuracy (degraded by 7.94 points).
    - **Low-NLL (Trigram Filtered):** Filtered examples with trigram repetition < 0.3. Result: 19.56% Aggregate Accuracy (degraded by 4.01 points), though termination improved to 44.5%.

# Method

- **Selection Strategy:**
    - NLL is computed per-token on response tokens under the base model before fine-tuning.
    - **Length-Matched Selection:** The pool is partitioned into 10 deciles by response token length. Within each decile, examples are ranked by NLL. A fixed quota (80 examples per decile) is selected to ensure identical length distributions for Low-NLL, High-NLL, and Random subsets.

- **Training Configuration:**
    - Learning rate: 2e-5 with cosine schedule.
    - Batch size: 1.
    - Precision: bfloat16.
    - Checkpoint selection: Best validation loss.

- **Evaluation Metrics:**
    - **Avg@n:** Accuracy averaged over n samples per problem.
    - **Pass@n:** Fraction of problems solved in at least one sample.
    - **Termination Rate:** Fraction of generations ending with EOS rather than being truncated.
