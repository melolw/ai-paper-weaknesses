1) Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of targeted code repair in diffusion language models, which traditionally rely on model-internal confidence or perturbation heuristics for remasking. The authors propose an execution-trace guided remasking pipeline that leverages runtime diagnostics—specifically exception tracebacks and line-level execution traces—to localize failure-relevant code regions. These regions are mapped to token indices and selectively remasked for conditional diffusion repair. Evaluated on HumanEval+ and MBPP+ using LLaDA-8B-Base, the method achieves 31.22% pass@1 on MBPP+, significantly outperforming no-repair and CORE baselines. The authors further demonstrate through ablation and edit-locality analysis that semantic localization enables meaningful code modifications, whereas global low-confidence remasking often yields negligible changes.2) Strengths
- **Novel Integration of Execution Feedback with Diffusion Remasking**
  - The pipeline bridges execution-guided debugging (common in autoregressive settings) with diffusion's native remasking capability, as detailed in Section 2 and Section 3.3. This matters for novelty, as it introduces a semantically grounded test-time scaling paradigm specifically tailored to discrete diffusion models.
  - The hierarchical localization strategy distinguishes between exception-based and assertion-based failures, enabling adaptive diagnostic routing (Section 3.3). This matters for technical soundness, as it ensures the method can handle both runtime crashes and logical mismatches without manual intervention.
  - Empirical validation demonstrates non-zero repair success across all error categories, including 12.1% for syntax errors and 15.6% for runtime errors (Table 3). This matters for impact, as it proves the approach generalizes beyond a single failure mode and delivers practical debugging utility.- **Rigorous Experimental Design and Baselines**
  - The evaluation strictly partitions tests into feedback (20%) and evaluation (80%) subsets to prevent leakage, as specified in Section 4.1. This matters for experimental rigor, ensuring that repair gains stem from genuine diagnostic reasoning rather than test-case memorization.
  - Comparisons are conducted against multiple relevant baselines (No Repair, Global Low-Confidence, CORE, Best-of-2) under matched or lower compute budgets (Table 1). This matters for technical soundness, as it isolates the contribution of semantic localization from mere inference-time compute scaling.
  - Statistical significance is formally validated using a McNemar test ($p < 0.001$) with explicit problem-level breakdowns (Section 4.3). This matters for impact, as it strengthens the claim that trace-guided repair consistently outperforms context-robust remasking baselines.- **Insightful Diagnostic and Ablation Analysis**
  - Edit locality comparison reveals trace-guided repair modifies ~10.01 tokens on average versus 0.95 for low-confidence repair (Figure 3, Section 4.5). This matters for clarity and technical soundness, as it empirically explains why semantic guidance is necessary to escape local minima in failing code.
  - Ablation on localization window size shows that expanding from $\pm 1$ to $\pm 5$ lines improves pass@1 by 3.18 percentage points (Table 2). This matters for experimental rigor, providing actionable hyperparameter guidance and highlighting the importance of contextual scope in diffusion repair.
  - Distribution analysis confirms that execution traces successfully localize >80% of failures across both benchmarks (Figure 2). This matters for impact, validating the feasibility and coverage of the proposed localization pipeline in realistic code generation scenarios.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Generalization to Complex and Non-Local Bugs**
  - HumanEval+ results show minimal improvement (1.83% → 2.64%) due to the model's low base performance on harder problems (Table 1, Section 4.3). This matters for technical soundness, as it suggests the method struggles when errors are deeply embedded or require architectural-level reasoning.
  - Localization relies on line-level traces and fixed $\pm w$ windows, which may miss cross-function or data-flow dependencies (Section 3.3). This matters for impact, limiting applicability to larger codebases where bugs often manifest far from the failure point.
  - No evaluation is provided on multi-file projects or longer generation lengths beyond benchmark constraints (Section 4.1). This matters for experimental rigor, leaving open questions about scalability to real-world software engineering workflows.- **Computational Overhead and Efficiency Concerns**
  - The reported NFE for trace-guided repair (294) is substantially higher than baselines (128–160) despite claims of matched compute budgets (Table 1). This matters for impact, as high inference costs may hinder practical deployment in latency-sensitive environments.
  - Iterative repair is allowed up to 3 rounds without adaptive stopping criteria, multiplying both execution and diffusion costs (Section 3.4). This matters for technical soundness, as fixed iteration limits may waste compute on irrecoverable failures or converge prematurely.
  - Line tracing via `sys.settrace` introduces runtime instrumentation overhead that is not quantified in the evaluation (Appendix A). This matters for experimental rigor, as hidden execution costs are omitted from the efficiency analysis.- **Ambiguity in Token Mapping and Repair Configuration**
  - Mapping source lines to token indices via character offsets may misalign with subword tokenization boundaries, potentially remasking irrelevant tokens (Section 3.4). This matters for technical soundness, as imprecise masking could degrade conditional diffusion repair quality.
  - The fallback mechanism for localization failures (e.g., syntax errors, missing frames) lacks detailed case analysis (Section 3.3, Figure 2). This matters for clarity, obscuring how the method behaves when execution diagnostics are unavailable or malformed.
  - Repair temperature $\tau = 0.2$ and step count $S = 64$ are fixed without sensitivity analysis across different model scales or code domains (Section 4.1, Appendix A). This matters for experimental rigor, limiting reproducibility and adaptability to varying diffusion model capacities.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation to Complex and Multi-File Scenarios**
  - Include benchmarks with longer code contexts or multi-function files (e.g., APPS or SWE-bench Lite) to assess cross-line dependency handling and non-local bug resolution.
  - Analyze failure cases on HumanEval+ to determine whether low base performance stems from initial generation quality or fundamental limits of line-level localization.
  - Explore AST-aware or control-flow graph localization to complement line-level tracing, enabling the method to capture data-flow dependencies beyond fixed line windows.- **Quantify and Optimize Computational Efficiency**
  - Report wall-clock time and memory usage per repair round alongside NFE to provide a complete and transparent efficiency profile for practitioners.
  - Investigate early-stopping criteria or adaptive round limits based on diagnostic confidence or edit convergence to reduce unnecessary iterations and save compute.
  - Benchmark the overhead of `sys.settrace` against lighter instrumentation methods (e.g., coverage tools or AST walkers) and report execution time trade-offs in the ablation study.- **Clarify Token Mapping and Provide Robustness Analysis**
  - Detail the exact algorithm for mapping line ranges to token indices, including explicit handling of subword splits, whitespace, and multi-line string literals.
  - Provide a breakdown of fallback cases (syntax errors, missing frames, timeout failures) and analyze whether alternative heuristics or static analysis could improve coverage.
  - Conduct a sensitivity study on repair temperature $\tau$ and step count $S$ across different model sizes or code domains to guide hyperparameter selection and improve reproducibility.5) Score
- Overall (10): 7 — The paper presents a compelling integration of execution traces with diffusion remasking, supported by strong MBPP+ results (Table 1) and insightful edit-locality analysis (Figure 3), though efficiency and generalization concerns temper the overall impact.
- Novelty (10): 8 — Bridging runtime diagnostics with conditional diffusion repair is a fresh direction (Section 3.3), effectively adapting execution-guided debugging paradigms to the diffusion setting.
- Technical Quality (10): 7 — The methodology is sound and well-evaluated (Section 4), but the lack of detailed token-mapping specifications (Section 3.4) and unquantified tracing overhead (Appendix A) slightly reduce technical rigor.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear pipeline diagrams (Figure 1) and concise ablation tables (Table 2), though some implementation details require tighter exposition.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence based on clear experimental setup, reproducible hyperparameters (Appendix A), and consistent results across seeds, though limited evaluation on complex codebases slightly reduces certainty.
