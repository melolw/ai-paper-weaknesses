# Global Summary
This paper investigates whether adaptive compute allocation can improve the token efficiency of large language model reasoning. The authors propose Draft-and-Continue Self-Consistency (DCS), a two-stage method that samples short reasoning drafts, aggregates interim answers via voting, and allocates additional continuation tokens to high-vote branches. Evaluation is conducted on the MATH-500 dataset using the Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct model. The study reports a negative result: DCS achieves 76.8% accuracy, matching baseline methods, but consumes 19.6% more tokens than standard self-consistency and 109% more than confidence-guided early stopping (CGES-LNS). Diagnostic analysis reveals that approximately 96.3% of drafts complete naturally within the 1024-token draft limit, causing the continuation mechanism to rarely activate. The authors conclude that simpler early-stopping methods dominate two-stage branch budgeting when draft budgets are sufficient for most problems. Caveats include evaluation on a single dataset and model, limited hyperparameter tuning, and the paper being generated by an automated research system.

# Abstract
- Problem: Standard self-consistency improves LLM reasoning but incurs high token costs from generating complete solutions for every sample.
- Approach: Draft-and-Continue Self-Consistency (DCS) uses a two-stage process. Stage 1 samples short drafts and computes vote histograms over interim answers. Stage 2 continues high-vote branches with additional tokens.
- Evaluation Scope: MATH-500 dataset, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct model.
- Key Findings: DCS achieves 76.8% accuracy, matching baselines, but uses 19.6% more tokens than standard self-consistency and 109% more than CGES-LNS. CGES-LNS Pareto-dominates DCS. Approximately 96% of drafts complete within the token limit, causing the continuation mechanism to rarely activate.
- Caveats: Paper generated by an automated research system; code is publicly available.

# Method
- Problem Setup: Self-consistency samples n independent reasoning chains and aggregates answers via majority vote. Generating n complete chains is computationally expensive for long reasoning paths.
- DCS Architecture:
  - Stage 1 (Draft Sampling): Samples B independent draft solutions with a token limit T_draft. Extracts interim answers from partial reasoning. Drafts without parseable answers are assigned to a NULL bucket.
  - Vote Histogram: Computes vote counts c(a) over interim answers. Identifies top-1 (a^(1)) and top-2 (a^(2)) answers.
  - Stage 2 (Continuation Allocation): Selects k branches to continue for an additional T_cont tokens. Uses a hedge allocation strategy: if a^(2) exists, allocates k-1 slots to a^(1) branches and 1 slot to a^(2). If all drafts agree, all k slots go to that answer. Branches within clusters are selected arbitrarily.
  - Final Aggregation: Extracts final answers from continued branches and applies majority vote. Total token budget approximates B * T_draft + k * T_cont.
- Hyperparameters: B = 7 drafts, k = 3 continuations, T_draft = 1024 tokens, T_cont = 1024 tokens. Total budget is approximately 10,240 tokens per problem. Sampling uses temperature 0.8 and top-p 0.95. Hedge allocation mode is enabled by default.

# Experiments
- Experimental Setup:
  - Dataset: MATH-500, a subset of 500 competition-style mathematics problems requiring multi-step reasoning. Evaluation uses exact match.
  - Model: Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, a 7B parameter model fine-tuned for mathematical reasoning.
  - Baselines: (1) Greedy CoT (temperature 0, max tokens 2048), (2) Uniform SC (n = 5 samples, temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95), (3) CGES-LNS (b_max = 5, confidence threshold gamma = 0.95).
  - Metrics: Accuracy (exact match), average generated tokens per problem, average API calls per problem. Experiments use three random seeds (42, 123, 456) and report mean ± standard deviation.
- Main Results:
  - Greedy CoT: 75.6 ± 0.28% accuracy, 654.3 avg tokens, 1.0 avg calls.
  - Uniform SC: 76.67 ± 0.41% accuracy, 3439.6 avg tokens, 5.0 avg calls.
  - CGES-LNS: 76.8 ± 0.30% accuracy, 1963.0 avg tokens, 2.48 avg calls.
  - DCS: 76.8 ± 0.75% accuracy, 4114.5 avg tokens, ~10 avg calls.
  - DCS matches baseline accuracy but uses 19.6% more tokens than Uniform SC and 109% more than CGES-LNS. CGES-LNS Pareto-dominates DCS by achieving identical accuracy with 52% fewer tokens and fewer API calls. DCS exhibits higher variance (0.75%) compared to baselines (0.30%–0.41%).
- Analysis of Failure:
  - Diagnostic metrics show a draft completion rate of 96.3 ± 0.19% and a fallback rate of 3.73 ± 0.19%.
  - The high completion rate indicates most drafts finish within T_draft = 1024 tokens, producing parseable final answers without needing continuation. The continuation stage rarely activates meaningfully.
  - DCS effectively degenerates into a variant of standard self-consistency with additional overhead from draft selection and allocation steps.
- Discussion and Limitations:
  - Effectiveness of two-stage branch budgeting depends critically on the relationship between draft length and problem complexity. With T_draft = 1024, the model solves most MATH-500 problems completely.
  - Limitations: Evaluated on a single dataset (MATH-500) and single model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct). Hyperparameters (B = 7, k = 3, T_draft = 1024) were not extensively tuned. Results may differ on other benchmarks or with different configurations.
