## 2. Internal Inconsistency: Method Design vs. Ablation Conclusion
**Severity:** High
**Evidence:**
*   **Method Design (Section 3.4):** The authors explicitly define Delta SVD-EQ as **"norm-preserving"** to isolate the effect of spectral redistribution from magnitude reduction. They state: *"by construction, $\|\Delta W_t^{eq}\|_F = \|\Delta W_t\|_F$, isolating the effect of spectral redistribution from magnitude reduction."*
*   **Ablation Results (Section 4.4 & Table 1):** The ablation variant "Mean-Smoothing" (which shrinks the norm by 13.3%) achieves a BWT improvement of **+2.2pp**, which is nearly identical to the proposed SVD-EQ (**+2.3pp**).
*   **Conclusion (Section 4.5):** The authors interpret these results by stating: *"This suggests that norm shrinkage contributes to the forgetting reduction... points toward norm shrinkage and disruption of interference patterns as more likely mechanisms."*

**Assessment:** There is a direct logical contradiction between the method's design and the paper's conclusion.
1.  The method is designed to **avoid** norm shrinkage to prove that spectral redistribution is the key factor.
2.  The ablation shows that a method **with** norm shrinkage performs just as well.
3.  The conclusion identifies **norm shrinkage** as a likely mechanism.
If norm shrinkage is the primary mechanism, the proposed norm-preserving method is theoretically suboptimal or its design rationale is undermined. The paper fails to reconcile why a norm-preserving method is proposed if the ablation suggests norm shrinkage is the driving force behind the improvement.

## 3. Inconsistency: Variance Reduction Claims vs. Main Results Table
**Severity:** Medium
**Evidence:**
*   **Abstract & Section 4.3:** The paper claims SVD-EQ *"reduces within-order variance by 3–10×"* and acts as a regularizer stabilizing outcomes.
*   **Table 1 (Main Results):** The standard deviation for BWT **increases** from **3.1** (Baseline) to **3.4** (SVD-EQ).
*   **Section 4.3 Explanation:** The text clarifies that the variance reduction is *"within-order"* (Table 2 shows std dropping from ~3-4 to ~1), but the overall variance in Table 1 is driven by the large performance drop in Order 6.

**Assessment:** While the text technically distinguishes between "within-order" and "overall" variance, the presentation in the main results table (Table 1) contradicts the high-level claim in the Abstract. A reader looking at Table 1 would see that the method *increased* overall variability (3.1 → 3.4), which directly conflicts with the Abstract's claim of stabilization without reading the detailed per-order analysis. This creates a misleading impression of the method's robustness in the primary summary table.
