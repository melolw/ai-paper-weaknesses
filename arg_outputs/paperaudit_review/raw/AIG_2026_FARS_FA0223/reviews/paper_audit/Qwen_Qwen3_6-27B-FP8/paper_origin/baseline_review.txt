1) Summary
The paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in sequential Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) continual learning for large language models. The authors propose Delta SVD-EQ, a post-hoc spectral intervention that equalizes the singular values of task-specific LoRA deltas at each task boundary while preserving the Frobenius norm. Evaluated on the FOREVER benchmark using Qwen3-0.6B across four text classification tasks, the method improves backward transfer by +2.3 percentage points (15% relative reduction in forgetting) without degrading overall performance. The approach is characterized as training-free, memory-free, and post-hoc applicable. Ablation studies indicate that both norm shrinkage and spectral redistribution contribute to the observed gains, while the method also reduces within-order variance by 3–10× across random seeds.2) Strengths
- **Novel Post-Hoc Intervention Paradigm**
  - (Section 1, Section 3.4): The method operates entirely after training without requiring gradient computation, replay buffers, or architectural modifications, which significantly broadens its applicability to existing LoRA checkpoints and addresses a clear gap in current continual learning literature.
  - (Abstract, Section 3.4): By simultaneously satisfying training-free, memory-free, post-hoc, and norm-preserving properties, the approach offers a lightweight, complementary alternative to regularization or replay-based methods, enhancing practical deployment feasibility.
  - (Section 2.1, Section 2.3): The clear differentiation from model merging techniques and in-training spectral regularization establishes a distinct methodological niche, contributing to the diversification of continual learning strategies for parameter-efficient fine-tuning.- **Rigorous Variance Reduction Analysis**
  - (Table 2, Figure 2): The empirical demonstration that standard deviation drops from 2.5–4.1pp to 0.4–1.0pp across task orders provides strong evidence of stabilization, which is highly valuable for reproducible continual learning pipelines.
  - (Section 4.3): Highlighting variance reduction alongside mean performance shifts addresses a frequently overlooked aspect of continual learning evaluation, strengthening the experimental rigor and practical relevance of the findings.
  - (Figure 2): The visual representation of error bars shrinking dramatically across seeds offers clear, interpretable evidence of the method's regularizing effect, improving result transparency and reproducibility.- **Comprehensive Ablation & Mechanism Investigation**
  - (Table 1, Section 4.4): The systematic comparison between mean-smoothing, random-spectrum, and SVD-EQ effectively isolates the contributions of norm shrinkage versus spectral redistribution, demonstrating careful experimental design.
  - (Figure 3, Section 4.5): Testing the initial hypothesis regarding spectral concentration ($\rho$) and openly reporting the lack of correlation ($r = -0.027, p = 0.893$) reflects scientific honesty and prevents overclaiming, which strengthens the paper's credibility.
  - (Section 4.4, Section 4.5): The combined ablation and correlation analysis provides a nuanced, multi-faceted view of the method's behavior, guiding future theoretical work and preventing misinterpretation of the equalization mechanism.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Benchmark Scope & Scale**
  - (Section 4.1): Evaluation is restricted to four text classification tasks and a single 0.6B model, which limits the generalizability of the findings to larger-scale LLMs or more complex generation/reasoning tasks.
  - (Section 4.1): Only three task orders and three random seeds are used, resulting in a small sample size ($n=9$) that may not capture the full distribution of continual learning dynamics across diverse task sequences.
  - (Section 2.1, Table 1): The absence of comparisons with established continual learning baselines (e.g., EWC, GEM, or replay methods) makes it difficult to contextualize the +2.3pp BWT improvement relative to state-of-the-art approaches.- **Marginal Statistical Significance & Order Dependency**
  - (Section 4.2): The reported paired t-test yields $p = 0.059$, which falls short of conventional significance thresholds, raising concerns about the reliability of the mean improvement claim.
  - (Table 2, Section 4.3): The method degrades performance on Order 6 (-1.0pp BWT), indicating that the intervention is not universally beneficial and may introduce task-order-specific interference.
  - (Section 4.3): The paper attributes order dependency to "inherently higher inter-task interference" but provides no empirical analysis (e.g., task similarity metrics or gradient overlap) to substantiate this claim, leaving the degradation unexplained.- **Incomplete Mechanistic Understanding**
  - (Section 4.5, Figure 3): The negative result regarding spectral concentration contradicts the initial motivation (Section 3.2), yet the paper does not propose an alternative theoretical framework to explain why equalization reduces forgetting.
  - (Section 4.4): The ablation shows that mean-smoothing (which shrinks the norm) achieves comparable BWT gains, suggesting that magnitude reduction may drive the effect rather than spectral redistribution, but this implication is not fully explored or reconciled with the norm-preserving design goal.
  - (Section 3.3, Equation 3): The mathematical formulation replaces singular values with their root-mean-square, but no theoretical analysis (e.g., bounds on gradient interference or loss landscape curvature) is provided to justify why uniform spectra mitigate catastrophic forgetting.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope & Baseline Comparisons**
  - (Section 4.1): Extend experiments to larger models (≥7B parameters) and diverse task types (e.g., instruction tuning, code generation, or mathematical reasoning) to validate scalability and cross-domain applicability.
  - (Section 4.1): Increase the number of task orders (≥10) and random seeds (≥5) to strengthen statistical power and better capture the distribution of continual learning outcomes.
  - (Section 2.1, Table 1): Include comparisons with representative continual learning baselines (e.g., EWC, GEM, or post-hoc merging techniques) to contextualize the BWT gains and clarify the method's relative standing.- **Strengthen Statistical Analysis & Investigate Order Dependency**
  - (Section 4.2): Report confidence intervals, effect sizes with corrected p-values, or conduct a power analysis to address the marginal significance ($p = 0.059$) and clarify the reliability of the mean improvement.
  - (Table 2, Section 4.3): Analyze task similarity or gradient overlap metrics for Order 6 to empirically explain why SVD-EQ degrades performance, rather than attributing it to unspecified interference.
  - (Section 4.3): Provide a sensitivity analysis or adaptive thresholding strategy to determine when SVD-EQ should be applied or skipped based on task-order characteristics, improving robustness.- **Develop Theoretical Grounding & Reconcile Ablation Findings**
  - (Section 4.5, Figure 3): Propose an alternative theoretical mechanism (e.g., spectral conditioning, gradient alignment, or Hessian trace reduction) to explain the forgetting mitigation, replacing the unsupported spectral concentration hypothesis.
  - (Section 4.4): Explicitly reconcile the norm-preserving design goal with the ablation finding that norm shrinkage drives comparable gains, and discuss whether intentional norm scaling could be a more effective variant.
  - (Section 3.3, Equation 3): Add a theoretical analysis or proof sketch linking uniform singular value distribution to reduced cross-task gradient interference or improved loss landscape smoothness, strengthening the methodological foundation.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The method offers a practical, post-hoc intervention with clear variance benefits, but its impact is tempered by marginal statistical significance and narrow evaluation scope (Section 4.2, Table 1).
- Novelty (10): 7 — Introduces a distinct training-free, norm-preserving spectral equalization approach for LoRA continual learning that fills a clear gap in post-hoc methods (Section 1, Section 3.4).
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The formulation is mathematically sound and efficiently implemented (Equation 3, Appendix A), though mechanistic insights remain empirical and partially contradictory to the initial hypothesis (Section 4.5).
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear visualizations and tables that effectively communicate the method, results, and ablations (Figure 1, Table 1, Section 3).
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence due to transparent methodology, reproducible setup, and honest reporting of negative results, though limited by small-scale experiments (Section 4.1, Figure 3).
