# Integrity Risk and Consistency Report

**Manuscript Title:** TINY-LR PROXY SFT FOR DATASET RANKING: AN EMPIRICAL INVESTIGATION
**Assessment Date:** Current
**Reviewer Role:** Expert Research Paper Reviewer

## Executive Summary
This review identifies **significant numerical inconsistencies** regarding the calculation of composite evaluation metrics. Specifically, the reported Pairwise Direction Accuracy (PDA) and Spearman correlation for the "Composite" ranking are mathematically inconsistent with the reported per-benchmark values. Given the manuscript's disclaimer that it was generated by an automated research system, these inconsistencies suggest potential computational errors or hallucinated results in the aggregation of metrics.

## Detailed Findings

### 1. Critical Numerical Inconsistency: Composite vs. Per-Benchmark Metrics
**Location:** Table 1, Table 2, Section 3.4
**Issue:** The composite metrics (PDA and Spearman $\rho$) are inconsistent with the individual benchmark metrics, violating the mathematical properties of an average-based composite score.

*   **Evidence:**
    *   **Section 3.4** states: "The composite score averages performance across both benchmarks."
    *   **Table 2** reports Tiny-LR Proxy performance:
        *   MATH-500 Only: PDA = 0.818, Spearman $\rho$ = 0.846
        *   GSM8K Only: PDA = 0.515, Spearman $\rho$ = 0.063
    *   **Table 1** and **Table 2** report Tiny-LR Proxy Composite performance:
        *   Composite: PDA = 0.500, Spearman $\rho$ = -0.091

*   **Analysis:**
    *   **PDA Inconsistency:** If the composite ranking is derived from the average of GSM8K and MATH-500 scores, the PDA of the composite ranking should generally fall between the PDAs of the individual benchmarks (or be a weighted average thereof). The reported Composite PDA (0.500) is **lower** than the GSM8K PDA (0.515). This implies that the composite ranking is *less* correlated with the target than the GSM8K ranking alone, which is counter-intuitive for an average metric unless the benchmarks are perfectly negatively correlated (which is unlikely given the context).
    *   **Spearman Inconsistency:** The Composite Spearman correlation is **-0.091** (negative), while both component correlations are positive (0.846 and 0.063). A negative composite correlation derived from two positive component correlations is mathematically impossible under standard averaging assumptions. This strongly suggests a calculation error or a hallucination in the "Composite" column.

### 2. Statistical Anomaly: Exact Random-Level PDA
**Location:** Table 1, Table 2, Figure 2, Figure 3
**Issue:** The Tiny-LR Proxy achieves a Composite PDA of **exactly 0.500**.

*   **Evidence:**
    *   **Table 1:** Tiny-LR Proxy PDA = 0.500.
    *   **Figure 2:** Bar chart shows Tiny-LR Proxy at exactly 0.500.
    *   **Section 4.1:** "Tiny-LR Proxy achieves PDA of exactly 0.500... indistinguishable from random chance."

*   **Analysis:**
    *   With 12 datasets, there are $\binom{12}{2} = 66$ pairs. The chance level for PDA is 0.5 (33 agreements, 33 disagreements).
    *   While statistically possible, achieving **exactly** 0.500 is highly improbable for empirical data. Combined with the inconsistency in Section 1, this suggests the value may be a default placeholder, a rounding artifact of a flawed calculation, or a hallucinated result by the automated generation system.

### 3. Minor Consistency Check: Learning Rate and Steps
**Location:** Section 3.2, Figure 1, Appendix B.3
**Issue:** None found.
**Analysis:** The manuscript consistently reports Standard-LR ($5 \times 10^{-5}$, 500 steps) and Tiny-LR ($1 \times 10^{-5}$, 1000 steps) across the text, figures, and appendix. The "learning budget" (LR $\times$ steps) differs (0.025 vs 0.01), which is a valid experimental design choice but is consistently reported.

## Conclusion
The manuscript contains **clear internal inconsistencies** in the reporting of composite evaluation metrics. The Composite PDA (0.500) and Spearman correlation (-0.091) are mathematically incompatible with the reported per-benchmark values (PDA 0.818/0.515, Spearman 0.846/0.063) under the stated definition of a composite score. These issues, coupled with the exact 0.500 PDA value, indicate a high risk of computational error or hallucination in the results section.

**Recommendation:** The authors must verify the calculation of the composite rankings and metrics. The current values suggest that the "Composite" results may not be derived from the stated per-benchmark data.
