# Global Summary

This paper investigates whether using tiny learning rates (Tiny-LR) during proxy model fine-tuning improves the transfer of dataset rankings to larger target models in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The authors hypothesize that reduced learning rates keep the proxy in a near-linear optimization regime, preserving transferable signals.

The study compares Standard-LR Proxy ($5 \times 10^{-5}$) against Tiny-LR Proxy ($1 \times 10^{-5}$) using Qwen2.5-1.5B as the proxy and Qwen2.5-7B as the target across 12 math-oriented datasets. The experiments refute the hypothesis: Tiny-LR Proxy achieves random-level ranking agreement (PDA = 0.500), while Standard-LR Proxy achieves significantly above-random performance (PDA = 0.712).

A key finding is benchmark-specific behavior: Tiny-LR Proxy performs well on MATH-500 (PDA = 0.818) but fails on GSM8K (PDA = 0.515). The authors conclude that reduced learning rates amplify sensitivity to surface-level features (like response length) rather than preserving quality signals, and recommend standard learning rates for proxy-based dataset ranking.

# Abstract

- **Problem:** Evaluating dataset value for SFT via full fine-tuning is expensive; proxy models are an alternative but may not transfer rankings reliably.
- **Hypothesis:** Training proxies with tiny learning rates improves ranking transfer to larger models (based on pretraining literature).
- **Method:** Comparison of Standard-LR Proxy ($5 \times 10^{-5}$) and Tiny-LR Proxy ($1 \times 10^{-5}$) on 12 math datasets.
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-1.5B (proxy) and Qwen2.5-7B (target).
- **Key Results:**
    - Tiny-LR Proxy achieves random-level Pairwise Direction Accuracy (PDA = 0.500).
    - Standard-LR Proxy achieves significantly above-random performance (PDA = 0.712, $p = 0.042$).
    - Tiny-LR Proxy shows benchmark-specific behavior: excellent agreement on MATH-500 (PDA = 0.818) but failure on GSM8K.
- **Claim:** Reduced learning rates amplify sensitivity to surface-level features rather than preserving transferable quality signals.

# Experiments

- **Experimental Conditions:**
    - **Standard-LR Proxy:** Qwen2.5-1.5B, LR $5 \times 10^{-5}$, 500 steps.
    - **Tiny-LR Proxy:** Qwen2.5-1.5B, LR $1 \times 10^{-5}$, 1000 steps.
    - **Training-Free NLL:** Base model perplexity (no training).
    - **Target Ground Truth:** Qwen2.5-7B, LR $5 \times 10^{-5}$, 2000 steps.
    - **Configuration:** All fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 16, $\alpha = 32$, dropout 0.05) with cosine schedule and warmup ratio 0.1.

- **Datasets and Benchmarks:**
    - **Datasets:** 12 math-oriented SFT datasets from OpenDataArena (50K examples each), including dart-math-hard, openmathinstruct-2, and QwQ-LongCoT-130K-math.
    - **Benchmarks:** GSM8K (exact-match scoring) and MATH-500 (math_verify evaluation).
    - **Seeds:** 3 random seeds (42, 123, 456).

- **Metrics:**
    - **Pairwise Direction Accuracy (PDA):** Fraction of dataset pairs with matching orderings (chance level 0.5).
    - **Spearman Rank Correlation ($\rho$):** Monotonic relationship between rankings.
    - **Top-1 Match:** Correct identification of the best dataset.

- **Main Results (Table 1):**
    - **Standard-LR Proxy:** PDA = 0.712 (95% CI: [0.606, 0.818]), Spearman $\rho$ = 0.594 ($p = 0.042$). Correctly identifies top-1 dataset (dart-math-hard).
    - **Tiny-LR Proxy:** PDA = 0.500 (95% CI: [0.379, 0.621]), Spearman $\rho$ = -0.091 ($p = 0.779$). Incorrectly identifies top-1 dataset (QwQ-LongCoT-130K-math, which is 8th in target ranking).
    - **Training-Free NLL:** PDA = 0.636 ($p = 0.236$), fails to identify top-1.

- **Per-Benchmark Analysis (Table 2):**
    - **Tiny-LR on MATH-500:** PDA = 0.818, Spearman $\rho$ = 0.846 ($p < 0.001$).
    - **Tiny-LR on GSM8K:** PDA = 0.515, Spearman $\rho$ = 0.063 ($p = 0.846$).
    - **Interpretation:** Tiny-LR fails on GSM8K due to sensitivity to verbose reasoning chains (e.g., QwQ-LongCoT) which inflate scores on exact-match benchmarks but do not transfer to the target model.

# Conclusion

- **Primary Finding:** The hypothesis that tiny learning rates improve proxy-to-target ranking transfer for SFT is refuted.
- **Performance Comparison:** Tiny-LR Proxy performs at random chance (PDA = 0.500), while Standard-LR Proxy provides reliable signals (PDA = 0.712, $p = 0.042$).
- **Benchmark Specificity:** Tiny-LR Proxy exhibits divergent behavior, achieving high agreement on MATH-500 (PDA = 0.818) but failing on GSM8K.
- **Recommendation:** Practitioners should use standard learning rates for proxy-based dataset ranking to ensure reliable signals across diverse evaluation settings.
- **Limitations:** Study limited to Qwen2.5 model family and math domain; hyperparameter search limited to two learning rate settings.
