1) Summary
This paper addresses the vulnerability of vision-language model (VLM) judges to prompt injection attacks embedded in candidate responses. The authors propose an "isolated solve-then-judge" defense, a two-pass architecture where Pass 1 generates a self-answer using only trusted inputs (image and query), and Pass 2 evaluates candidates against this uncontaminated reference. Evaluated on VL-RewardBench using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, the defense reduces the conditional attack success rate from 91.3% to 29.3%, with controlled experiments isolating a 4 percentage-point benefit from information isolation alone. The authors report a 10.8 percentage-point clean accuracy trade-off, category-dependent effectiveness (strongest for hallucination detection, weakest for reasoning), and persistent vulnerability to authority impersonation attacks.2) Strengths
- **Clear and Well-Structured Methodological Design**
  - The two-pass architecture is precisely defined in Section 3.3 and formalized in Equations 3-4, providing a transparent mechanism for information isolation that enhances technical soundness.
  - Experimental conditions (A, B, C) are rigorously designed in Section 3.4 and visualized in Figure 1 to isolate the effect of information isolation from prompt engineering and compute overhead, strengthening experimental rigor.
  - The problem formulation in Section 3.1 (Equation 1) and vulnerability analysis in Section 3.2 (Equation 2) establish a solid theoretical foundation for why single-pass judging is susceptible, improving clarity and logical flow.
- **Comprehensive and Rigorous Experimental Evaluation**
  - Evaluation on a substantial benchmark (VL-RewardBench, N=1,247) across three distinct categories (Section 4.1, Table 2) ensures broad applicability and demonstrates experimental rigor.
  - Use of three attack variants (Primary, Authority, Verdict) in Section 4.1 and Table 3 demonstrates robustness testing against diverse injection strategies, enhancing technical soundness.
  - Detailed failure and corruption analyses in Sections 4.5-4.6 provide mechanistic insights into defense limitations, such as weak self-answer anchors and position bias, which increases the impact of the findings.
- **Transparent Reporting of Trade-offs and Limitations**
  - Explicit quantification of the clean accuracy degradation (10.8pp, Table 1) prevents overclaiming and aids practical deployment decisions, reflecting strong technical honesty.
  - Category-dependent performance breakdown in Table 2 honestly reveals that isolation can hinder reasoning tasks where candidate context is beneficial, improving clarity regarding method boundaries.
  - Acknowledgment of persistent vulnerability to authority impersonation (63.6% ASR_cond, Table 3) guides future research directions without overstating the defense's capabilities, enhancing the paper's scholarly impact.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Generalization Across Model Architectures and Scales**
  - Experiments are exclusively conducted on a single model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, Section 4.1), leaving open whether the defense scales to larger or architecturally distinct VLMs, which limits technical soundness.
  - No ablation on model size or parameter count is provided, making it unclear if the 10.8pp accuracy drop is inherent to the method or specific to the 7B parameter scale, reducing experimental rigor.
  - The reliance on greedy decoding (temperature=0, Section 4.1) may not reflect real-world judging scenarios where sampling is used, potentially overstating defense stability and limiting practical impact.
- **Insufficient Analysis of Computational and Latency Overheads**
  - The two-pass architecture inherently doubles inference steps, but Section 4.2 and Table 1 provide no latency, throughput, or FLOPs measurements, which hinders assessment of deployment feasibility.
  - The manuscript mentions "additional compute" in Section 3.4 but fails to quantify the practical cost-benefit ratio for high-throughput judging pipelines, weakening technical soundness.
  - Without efficiency metrics, it is difficult to assess whether the 62pp ASR reduction justifies the computational doubling in production environments, limiting the work's real-world impact.
- **Ambiguity in Prompt Engineering and Implementation Details**
  - Section 3.4 mentions "data delimiters and explicit warnings" for Condition B, but the exact prompt templates are not provided in the main text, which reduces reproducibility and experimental rigor.
  - The 4pp isolation benefit (Section 4.2) is attributed to information isolation, yet the lack of prompt templates makes it impossible to verify whether prompt structure differences confound this result, affecting technical soundness.
  - The failure analysis in Section 4.6 cites 238 attack successes, which numerically mismatches the reported ASR_cond (29.33%) and CleanAcc (78.35%) in Table 1, reducing reproducibility and technical soundness.
- **Underdeveloped Related Work and Novelty Positioning**
  - The manuscript does not cite or discuss structurally similar two-pass, reference-anchored evaluation frameworks like BIRCH or StruQ, which also decouple trusted inputs from untrusted candidates, limiting the contextualization of the contribution in Section 2.
  - The core architectural mechanism (generate clean self-answer → evaluate candidates against anchor) is functionally equivalent to existing bias-mitigation pipelines, making the novelty primarily application-oriented rather than methodological, as noted in Section 2.4 and Section 3.3.
  - Without explicit differentiation from these prior works, the claimed gap in Section 1 regarding the need for a "structural defense" appears overstated, affecting the perceived impact and scholarly rigor.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Model Coverage and Decoding Strategies**
  - Evaluate the defense on at least two additional VLMs of varying scales (e.g., 3B and 13B+) to demonstrate architectural generalization and strengthen technical soundness.
  - Include experiments with non-zero temperature sampling to assess defense robustness under stochastic generation conditions common in production, improving experimental rigor.
  - Report how the clean accuracy trade-off scales with model size to clarify whether larger models mitigate the 10.8pp degradation observed in Section 4.2, enhancing practical impact.
- **Quantify Computational Efficiency and Deployment Feasibility**
  - Add latency (ms per sample) and throughput (samples/sec) measurements for Conditions A, B, and C to contextualize the two-pass overhead and improve technical soundness.
  - Provide a cost-benefit analysis comparing the ASR reduction against the doubled inference time, particularly for high-volume judging applications, to strengthen experimental rigor.
  - Discuss potential optimizations, such as caching Pass 1 outputs for repeated queries or using smaller models for self-solve, to mitigate the computational burden and increase real-world impact.
- **Release Prompt Templates and Clarify Implementation**
  - Include the exact prompt templates for Conditions A, B, and C in an appendix or supplementary material to ensure full reproducibility and improve experimental rigor.
  - Specify the exact wording of "data delimiters and explicit warnings" mentioned in Section 3.4 to isolate the contribution of prompt engineering from information isolation, enhancing technical soundness.
  - Detail the criteria for identifying "weak self-answer anchors" and provide guidelines or hyperparameters for optimizing self-answer length and quality in Section 4.6, improving clarity and reproducibility.
- **Strengthen Related Work and Novelty Claims**
  - Cite and explicitly contrast the proposed architecture with BIRCH and StruQ in Section 2 to clarify how temporal decoupling for security differs from existing reference-anchored or structured-query approaches.
  - Reframe the contribution in Section 1 and Section 3.3 to emphasize the empirical validation and security-specific threat modeling rather than architectural novelty, aligning claims with the actual methodological advance.
  - Discuss why the two-pass isolation mechanism provides unique benefits for prompt injection defense compared to prior bias-mitigation strategies, strengthening the paper's positioning and impact.5) Score
- Overall (10): 5 — The paper presents a well-motivated defense with rigorous controlled experiments (Section 3.4, Table 1), but limited model coverage, missing efficiency metrics, and insufficient related work contextualization constrain its broader impact.
- Novelty (10): 5 — The isolated solve-then-judge architecture (Section 3.3, Equations 3-4) offers a clear conceptual advance over single-pass judging, but its structural similarity to prior reference-anchored frameworks and lack of citation in Section 2 limits methodological novelty.
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — Experimental design is strong with proper controls (Figure 1, Table 2), yet the absence of latency measurements, prompt templates (Section 3.4), and a numerical mismatch in failure counts (Section 4.6 vs. Table 1) reduce reproducibility and practical assessment.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-organized with precise problem formulation (Section 3.1) and transparent reporting of trade-offs (Section 4.2), though implementation details require supplementation.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence due to clear methodology and comprehensive benchmark results (Section 4), though limited to a single model and missing supplementary prompt artifacts.
