# Global Summary
This paper investigates a mismatch in the ROCKET training-free LLM compression method: while per-layer factorization is derived from an output reconstruction objective, global budget allocation uses weight-space Frobenius error as the multi-choice knapsack problem (MCKP) cost. The authors propose ROCKET-ActCost, which replaces the allocation cost with an output-space (whitened) error metric. Evaluated on Qwen3-8B (50% compression) and Llama-3.2-1B (20% compression) using 256 calibration sequences from RefinedWeb, the method reveals an accuracy-perplexity tradeoff under aggressive compression: ROCKET-ActCost improves average accuracy by +0.8 percentage points (53.1% vs 52.3%) but increases WikiText perplexity by 16% (61.46 vs 52.98). Under milder compression (Llama-3.2-1B at 20%), both accuracy (+0.6pp) and perplexity improve. The modest effect size is attributed to a >0.99 Spearman correlation between weight-space and output-space errors, which limits allocation divergence to only 70 of 252 layers. The method adds zero runtime overhead and runs 7.7% faster on average. The authors note the paper was generated by an automated research system.

# Abstract
- Proposes ROCKET-ActCost to align the MCKP allocation cost with the output-space factorization objective in calibration-guided LLM compression.
- Reports Qwen3-8B at 50% compression results: +0.8pp higher average accuracy (53.1% vs 52.3%) across 8 zero-shot benchmarks, but WikiText perplexity increases by 16% (61.46 vs 52.98).
- Identifies an accuracy-perplexity tradeoff, noting that high correlation (>0.99) between weight-space and output-space errors limits allocation divergence (only 70 of 252 layers receive different allocations).
- Reports Llama-3.2-1B at 20% compression results: both accuracy and perplexity improve, indicating the tradeoff is setting-dependent.
- States the paper was generated by an automated research system and provides a public code repository link.

# Method
- Background: ROCKET operates in a whitened activation space ($W_L = LW$) where output reconstruction error equals Frobenius error in the whitened space. Global budget allocation solves an MCKP using weight-space relative Frobenius error as the cost: $e_{\ell,i}^{\text{weight}} = \|W_\ell - \hat{W}_{\ell,i}\|_F / \|W_\ell\|_F$.
- Proposal: ROCKET-ActCost replaces the allocation cost with an output-space (whitened) error: $e_{\ell,i}^{\text{out}} = \|W_{L,\ell} - \hat{W}_{L,\ell,i}\|_F / \|W_{L,\ell}\|_F$. This directly measures the impact of rank truncation on layer outputs for the calibration distribution.
- Implementation: The output-space error is computed from matrices ($W_L$ and $\hat{W}_L$) already available during the profiling phase. The method adds no runtime overhead, and the MCKP solver executes in identical time regardless of the cost function used.

# Experiments
- Setup: Primary evaluation on Qwen3-8B at 50% compression; secondary on Llama-3.2-1B at 20% compression. Calibration uses 256 sequences of length 1024 from RefinedWeb. Qwen3-8B results are averaged over 2 seeds (2023, 42); Llama-3.2-1B uses 1 seed. Evaluation covers 8 zero-shot benchmarks (PIQA, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, ARC-E, ARC-C, SciQ, RACE, MMLU) via lm-eval-harness. Metrics: average accuracy and WikiText-2 perplexity.
- Main Results (Qwen3-8B, 50%): ROCKET-ActCost achieves 53.1% average accuracy vs 52.3% for ROCKET-default (+0.8pp). WikiText perplexity increases to 61.46 from 52.98 (16% worse). Per-benchmark breakdown shows improvements on 6 of 8 tasks. Largest gains occur on ARC-C (+1.5pp), MMLU (+1.5pp), and LAMBADA (+1.2pp). PIQA decreases by 0.1pp; RACE remains unchanged.
- Analysis: Spearman correlation between weight-space and output-space errors exceeds 0.99 for nearly all layers. Consequently, only 70 of 252 compressible layers receive different allocations, with differences occurring at borderline decision points. This high correlation explains the limited accuracy gain.
- Secondary Results (Llama-3.2-1B, 20%): ROCKET-ActCost improves average accuracy to 54.1% from 53.5% (+0.6pp) and reduces WikiText perplexity to 13.86 from 14.66. No accuracy-perplexity tradeoff is observed in this setting.
- Runtime: Zero overhead compared to ROCKET-default. ROCKET-ActCost runs 7.7% faster on average due to different allocation decisions leading to slightly different compression configurations.
