# Global Summary

This paper addresses the issue of staleness in distributed reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs), where rollout generation lags behind the learner policy. The authors argue that standard importance sampling corrections, which apply per-token ratios, fail to account for sequential dependencies; specifically, a "bad prefix" (flawed early reasoning) renders subsequent tokens unreliable, yet standard methods may still assign them high gradient weight.

To solve this, the authors propose **Prefix-Ratio GRPO**, a modification to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that incorporates prefix information into importance ratios. The method defines a prefix-aware ratio $\tilde{\rho}_t = \underline{\rho}_t \cdot \rho_t$, where $\underline{\rho}_t$ is the minimum token ratio in the preceding prefix. This selectively dampens gradients from tokens following bad prefixes while preserving gradients from good prefixes.

Evaluated on the AIME24 benchmark using Qwen3-8B with simulated staleness $S=11$, Prefix-Ratio GRPO achieves an avg@64 score of **0.500**, outperforming vanilla GRPO (0.400) by 10 percentage points. Selectivity analysis indicates the method achieves a **4.42x selectivity ratio**, dampening 99.4% of bad-prefix tokens while only dampening 22.6% of good-prefix tokens. The authors note that while the method improves gradient quality, the stability benefits remain inconclusive as no training collapse was observed in their specific setup.

# Abstract

- **Problem:** Distributed RL systems introduce staleness where trajectories are generated by lagging behavior policies. Standard importance sampling uses per-token ratios that ignore sequential dependencies, failing to downweight tokens following "bad prefixes."
- **Method:** Prefix-Ratio GRPO incorporates prefix information into importance ratios using $\tilde{\rho}_t = \underline{\rho}_t \cdot \rho_t$, where $\underline{\rho}_t = \min_{k<t} \rho_k$.
- **Key Results:**
    - On AIME24 at staleness $S=11$, Prefix-Ratio GRPO achieves **0.500 avg@64**.
    - Outperforms vanilla GRPO (0.400) by **10 percentage points**.
    - Outperforms tight-clip GRPO (0.367) by **13.3 percentage points**.
- **Selectivity Analysis:**
    - Achieves a **4.42x selectivity ratio**.
    - Dampens **99.4%** of bad-prefix tokens.
    - Dampens only **22.6%** of good-prefix tokens.

# Introduction

- **Context:** RL post-training (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Qwen2.5) improves LLM reasoning. Distributed systems decouple rollout and learning to scale, introducing staleness.
- **Problem:** Standard GRPO uses per-token importance ratios $\rho_t$ that treat tokens independently. In autoregressive generation, a flawed early step (bad prefix) makes all subsequent tokens unreliable. Standard corrections may assign high weight to these unreliable tokens if their local ratios are high.
- **Proposed Solution:** Prefix-Ratio GRPO modifies the importance ratio to incorporate prefix information. If any prefix token becomes unlikely under the current policy, all subsequent tokens are downweighted.
- **Contributions:**
    - Proposal of Prefix-Ratio GRPO for selective gradient dampening.
    - Demonstration of performance gains on AIME24 at staleness $S=11$ (0.500 vs 0.400 for vanilla).
    - Selectivity analysis showing 4.42x selectivity ratio.

# Experiments

- **Experimental Setup:**
    - **Model:** Qwen3-8B.
    - **Dataset:** DAPO-Math-17K.
    - **Framework:** veRL in co-located mode.
    - **Hardware:** 8 A100-80GB GPUs.
    - **Staleness:** Simulated bounded staleness $S=11$ (policy snapshot delayed every $\kappa=10$ learner updates).
    - **Baselines:**
        - Vanilla GRPO ($\epsilon=0.2$).
        - Tight-Clip GRPO ($\epsilon=0.1$).
        - Prefix-Ratio GRPO ($\epsilon=0.2$).
    - **Evaluation Metric:** AIME24 avg@64 accuracy and number of problems solved (out of 30).

- **Main Results (Table 1):**
    - **Prefix-Ratio GRPO:** 0.500 avg@64; 15/30 problems solved.
    - **Vanilla GRPO:** 0.400 avg@64; 12/30 problems solved.
    - **Tight-Clip GRPO:** 0.367 avg@64; 11/30 problems solved.
    - **Stability:** All methods remained stable with `pg_clipfrac=0.0` (clipping never activated). No seeds collapsed.

- **Selectivity Analysis (Table 2):**
    - **Prefix-Ratio GRPO:** Selectivity ratio of **4.42**. Dampens **99.4%** of bad-prefix tokens and **22.6%** of good-prefix tokens.
    - **Vanilla GRPO:** Selectivity ratio of 2.27. Dampens 5.1% bad-prefix and 2.2% good-prefix tokens.
    - **Tight-Clip GRPO:** Selectivity ratio of 1.52. Dampens 15.1% bad-prefix and 9.9% good-prefix tokens.

- **Training Dynamics:**
    - All methods showed similar reward improvement trajectories (Mean Batch Reward moving average improved from ~-0.998 to ~-0.55 to -0.68).
    - The performance difference is attributed to gradient quality enhancement rather than training stability, as clipping was not activated and reward curves were similar.
