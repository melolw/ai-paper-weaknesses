1) Summary
The paper addresses the high computational cost of training DefensiveTokens—soft prompts used to defend Large Language Models (LLMs) against prompt injection attacks—which currently requires approximately 16 GPU-hours per model. The authors propose AlignDefTok, a training-free method that transfers these tokens between related models (sharing the same tokenizer and embedding dimension) using Orthogonal Procrustes alignment. This approach leverages the norm-preserving property of orthogonal transformations to maintain the high-norm characteristic critical for defense effectiveness. For transfer directions where alignment alone is insufficient, the authors introduce "tiny-adapt," a lightweight fine-tuning stage initialized with the transferred tokens. Experiments on Llama-3 and Llama-3.1 using the AlpacaFarm benchmark demonstrate that the method achieves 0% Attack Success Rate (ASR) for favorable transfers and 1.9% ASR with tiny-adapt for harder transfers, offering 133–285× speedups compared to full training.2) Strengths
*   **Significant Compute Efficiency and Practical Utility**
    *   The method drastically reduces the time required to deploy defenses across model families. As shown in **Table 1**, the Procrustes transfer requires only ~5 minutes of CPU time compared to 16 GPU-hours for full training, resulting in a 285× speedup for the Llama-3.1 → Llama-3 direction.
    *   The "tiny-adapt" stage maintains high efficiency while improving performance for harder transfers. **Section 3.4** and **Table 1** report that tiny-adapt achieves native defense performance (1.9% ASR) in ~55 minutes, representing a 133× speedup over full training.
    *   The approach enables rapid deployment in dynamic environments where models are frequently updated, addressing a key bottleneck identified in the **Introduction** regarding the barrier to deployment caused by per-model training costs.
*   **Theoretical Soundness of Norm Preservation**
    *   The choice of Orthogonal Procrustes is theoretically justified by the specific properties of DefensiveTokens. **Section 3.2** explains that the orthogonality constraint ($W^\top W = I$) ensures exact norm preservation, which is critical because DefensiveTokens exhibit unusually high $\ell_2$ norms (120–144× vocabulary average).
    *   **Section 4.4** provides empirical verification of this property, noting that the maximum norm difference after transfer is $< 10^{-5}$, confirming that the geometric properties essential for defense are maintained.
    *   This theoretical grounding distinguishes the method from naive transfer approaches, providing a principled reason for why the transfer should work, as discussed in **Section 3.2**.
*   **Effective Initialization Strategy for Harder Transfers**
    *   The "tiny-adapt" component demonstrates the value of the Procrustes alignment as an initialization strategy. **Table 2(b)** and **Figure 2** show that Procrustes-initialized tokens reach <5% ASR in 25 steps, compared to 100 steps for random initialization, a 4× convergence speedup.
    *   The initialization leads to better final performance; **Table 2(b)** indicates the best Procrustes checkpoint achieves 1.9% ASR, outperforming the best random checkpoint (2.9% ASR).
    *   This hybrid approach (alignment + lightweight fine-tuning) offers a robust fallback for cases where training-free transfer is insufficient, as seen in the Llama-3 → Llama-3.1 direction in **Table 1**.
*   **Clear Problem Formulation and Comprehensive Baselines**
    *   The problem is clearly defined in **Section 3.1**, establishing the notation for source/target models and the goal of obtaining effective tokens without expensive optimization.
    *   The experimental setup in **Section 4.1** includes a diverse set of baselines, including "No Defense," prompting baselines ("Reminder," "Sandwich"), "Direct Copy," and "Full DefensiveTokens," allowing for a thorough comparison of the proposed method's effectiveness.
    *   The use of the "Gap-Closed ratio" metric in **Section 4.1** provides a normalized way to measure defense recovery relative to the full training baseline, enhancing the interpretability of the results in **Table 1**.3) Weaknesses
*   **Narrow Scope of Model Evaluation**
    *   The experiments are limited to only two models: Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (**Section 4.1**). These models are very closely related, sharing the same tokenizer and embedding dimension, which may not represent the general case of model updates.
    *   There is no evaluation on models from different families (e.g., Mistral, Qwen) or models with different embedding dimensions, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
    *   The **Conclusion** acknowledges this limitation ("limited to models sharing the same tokenizer and embedding dimension"), but the lack of broader evaluation makes it difficult to assess the method's robustness across the wider LLM ecosystem.
*   **Single Benchmark Limitation**
    *   The evaluation relies solely on the AlpacaFarm prompt injection benchmark (**Section 4.1**). While this is a standard benchmark, it may not capture the full spectrum of prompt injection attacks or defense requirements.
    *   There is no evaluation on other relevant benchmarks such as DoNotAnswer or jailbreak datasets, which could provide a more comprehensive assessment of the defense's effectiveness.
    *   The reliance on a single benchmark raises concerns about whether the observed performance gains (e.g., 0% ASR) are specific to the attack variants in AlpacaFarm (ignore, completion, ignore+completion) as described in **Section 4.1**.
*   **Questionable Necessity of Procrustes for Close Models**
    *   **Table 2(a)** shows that for the easier Llama-3.1 → Llama-3 direction, "Direct Copy" (naive transfer without alignment) achieves 0.0% ASR, identical to the Procrustes method. This suggests that for very closely related models, the complex alignment step may be unnecessary.
    *   The marginal improvement of Procrustes over Direct Copy in the harder direction (33.7% vs 34.6% ASR, $\Delta$=0.9%) is small, raising questions about the added value of the alignment method in this specific setting.
    *   The paper does not sufficiently analyze why Direct Copy performs so well in the easy case, nor does it justify the computational overhead of Procrustes (SVD computation) when a simple copy might suffice for close models.
*   **Lack of Theoretical Analysis on Defense Mechanism**
    *   While the paper discusses norm preservation, it lacks a deeper theoretical analysis of *why* high-norm tokens combined with rotation provide effective defense. **Section 4.4** offers speculative insights ("suggests that defense effectiveness depends more on the high-norm property..."), but no rigorous proof or ablation on the role of norm magnitude is provided.
    *   There is no analysis of how the alignment affects the attention patterns or internal representations of the model when processing injected instructions, which would provide deeper insight into the mechanism of defense.
    *   The paper does not explore whether the transferred tokens might introduce new vulnerabilities or side effects, such as increased susceptibility to other types of attacks or degradation in utility on benign prompts.
*   **Numerical Inconsistency in Efficiency Claims**
    *   The manuscript claims a baseline training time of ~16 GPU-hours (960 minutes) in the **Abstract** and **Section 3.3**, but the reported speedup factors do not align with the stated execution times.
    *   For Procrustes transfer (~5 minutes), the claimed 285× speedup implies a baseline of ~24 hours or a method time of ~3.4 minutes, contradicting the stated 16 hours and 5 minutes (**Section 3.3**, **Table 1**).
    *   For tiny-adapt (~55 minutes), the claimed 133× speedup implies a baseline of ~122 hours or a method time of ~7.2 minutes, whereas 960/55 ≈ 17.5×. This discrepancy materially affects the core efficiency contribution highlighted in the **Abstract** and **Section 4.2**.4) Suggestions for Improvement
*   **Expand Model Evaluation to Diverse Families and Dimensions**
    *   Include experiments with models from different families (e.g., Mistral-7B, Qwen-7B) to demonstrate the method's applicability beyond the Llama family.
    *   Investigate transfer between models with different embedding dimensions by incorporating a projection step (e.g., using Procrustes with dimension mismatch or low-rank approximation) to address the limitation noted in the **Conclusion**.
    *   Evaluate the method on models with different tokenizers to assess the impact of vocabulary mismatch on the alignment process.
*   **Diversify Evaluation Benchmarks**
    *   Evaluate the method on additional prompt injection and jailbreak benchmarks (e.g., DoNotAnswer, JailbreakHub) to ensure the defense is robust across different attack types and datasets.
    *   Include a utility evaluation (e.g., AlpacaEval or MT-Bench) to measure the impact of the transferred tokens on the model's performance on benign tasks, ensuring that the defense does not degrade general capabilities.
    *   Test the method against adaptive attacks where the attacker knows the defense mechanism, to assess the robustness of the transferred tokens.
*   **Justify Procrustes vs. Direct Copy and Analyze Alignment Necessity**
    *   Provide a detailed analysis of why Direct Copy performs comparably to Procrustes in the easy transfer direction. Is the rotation matrix close to identity? If so, propose a simpler alignment method or a heuristic to decide when alignment is necessary.
    *   Investigate the marginal gain of Procrustes in the hard direction more thoroughly. Is the 0.9% ASR improvement statistically significant? Does it translate to meaningful security gains in practice?
    *   Consider ablation studies on the alignment method itself, such as comparing Orthogonal Procrustes with other alignment techniques (e.g., linear regression, Procrustes without orthogonality constraint) to justify the specific choice of method.
*   **Deepen Theoretical Analysis and Mechanism Understanding**
    *   Conduct an ablation study on the norm magnitude of the tokens to verify the claim that high norms are critical for defense. Test if scaling the norms of transferred tokens affects performance.
    *   Analyze the attention patterns or internal activations of the model when using transferred vs. native tokens to understand how the defense mechanism operates at the model level.
    *   Explore the potential for negative transfer or side effects, such as whether the transferred tokens might inadvertently make the model more susceptible to other types of attacks or degrade performance on specific tasks.
*   **Clarify and Correct Efficiency Calculations**
    *   Reconcile the reported training times with the claimed speedup factors, explicitly stating hardware assumptions or correcting the arithmetic to ensure consistency across the **Abstract**, **Section 3.3**, and **Table 1**.
    *   Provide a clear breakdown of compute costs (e.g., distinguishing GPU-hours for training vs. CPU-minutes for alignment) to ensure the speedup metrics are accurately calculated and reproducible.
    *   Update the efficiency claims throughout the manuscript to reflect the correct mathematical relationship between baseline and method execution times.5) Score
- Overall (10): 5 — The paper presents a practical and efficient method for transferring defensive tokens, but the limited scope of models and benchmarks restricts generalizability, and numerical inconsistencies in efficiency claims require correction.
- Novelty (10): 7 — Applying Orthogonal Procrustes to DefensiveTokens is a novel application, though the technique itself is standard in NLP; the "tiny-adapt" component adds incremental novelty as discussed in Section 3.4.
- Technical Quality (10): 5 — The method is theoretically sound and well-implemented, but the experimental evaluation is limited in scope and the arithmetic discrepancies in speedup calculations (Abstract, Table 1) undermine technical rigor.
- Clarity (10): 7 — The paper is well-written and clearly structured, but the mismatch between stated times and claimed speedups creates confusion regarding the core efficiency contribution.
- Confidence (5): 4 — The reviewer is confident in the assessment of the method's efficiency and clarity, but less confident in the generalizability due to the narrow experimental scope and unverified numerical claims.
