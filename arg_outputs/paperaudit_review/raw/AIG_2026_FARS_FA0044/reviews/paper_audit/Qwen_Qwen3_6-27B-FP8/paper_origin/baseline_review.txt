1) Summary
The paper addresses error propagation in LLM-as-a-Judge when self-generated answers are used as evaluation references. The authors observe that incorrect self-answers degrade judgment accuracy, particularly under model uncertainty. To mitigate this, they propose Selective Self-Reference (SSR-Judge), which leverages self-consistency across multiple temperature-sampled solutions as a confidence proxy. An agreement gate conditionally enables self-reference only when sample agreement exceeds a threshold (e.g., ≥4/5), otherwise defaulting to no-reference judging. Evaluated on MMLU-Pro with Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, SSR-Judge achieves 58.93% pairwise preference accuracy, outperforming both no-reference (52.07%) and always-self-reference (58.21%) baselines. The method preserves self-reference benefits on high-confidence items while avoiding error propagation on uncertain ones, supported by calibration and slice analyses.2) Strengths
- **Clear Problem Identification and Motivation**
  - The manuscript explicitly defines the error propagation phenomenon, demonstrating that incorrect self-answers significantly harm judgment accuracy on uncertain items (Section 1, Table 2 Non-unanimous+Wrong slice shows 24.50% vs 37.75% for no-reference). This precise framing establishes a well-defined gap in existing LLM-as-a-Judge literature, making the proposed solution highly relevant.
  - It quantifies the trade-off between self-reference benefits and risks, providing a concrete motivation for conditional gating rather than blanket application (Section 1, Abstract). This strengthens the technical soundness by grounding the method in a measurable failure mode.
  - The introduction clearly contrasts the proposed approach with prior self-reference work, highlighting why unconditional reference usage is suboptimal (Section 2 Related Work). This improves clarity by positioning the contribution within the existing research landscape.- **Empirically Grounded Confidence Proxy**
  - The authors validate self-consistency as a reliability signal by showing a strong monotonic relationship between agreement levels and answer correctness (Figure 3, Section 4.4: 35.5% at 3/5, 57.7% at 4/5, 78.7% at 5/5). This calibration directly justifies the gating mechanism, ensuring the method is data-driven rather than heuristic.
  - The agreement metric is formally defined and computationally lightweight, relying only on majority voting across samples (Section 3.2, Equation 1). This enhances practical impact by avoiding complex auxiliary models or fine-tuning.
  - The calibration analysis demonstrates that higher agreement reliably indicates higher probability of correctness, which strengthens the technical soundness of using sampling variance as a practical uncertainty estimator (Section 4.4, Figure 3).- **Comprehensive Experimental Validation**
  - The evaluation includes a detailed slice analysis that isolates the impact of the gate across unanimous/non-unanimous and correct/wrong conditions (Table 2, Figure 2, Section 4.3). This rigorous decomposition clearly demonstrates how the method preserves benefits while avoiding errors.
  - The paper reports multiple complementary metrics (PreferenceAcc, SliceAcc, Gate-On Rate, Gate Precision) to fully characterize the precision-coverage trade-off (Table 1, Section 4.1). This multi-metric reporting enhances experimental rigor and transparency.
  - Per-category analysis reveals domain-specific performance variations, highlighting where the method excels and where threshold tuning may be needed (Section 4.5). This adds practical utility for real-world deployment across heterogeneous benchmarks.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Baseline Comparison and Compute Matching**
  - The paper only compares against two simple baselines (no-reference and always self-reference) without evaluating against other advanced LLM-as-a-Judge methods or uncertainty-aware approaches (Section 4.1, Section 2 Related Work). This limits the ability to assess absolute performance gains and contextual impact.
  - While compute matching is claimed, the cost of generating k=5 samples for every judgment item is not quantified in terms of latency or token usage, making practical deployment feasibility unclear (Section 3.2, Section 5 Conclusion). This omission weakens experimental rigor regarding real-world applicability.
  - The absence of ablation studies on alternative gating signals (e.g., log-probability, verbalized confidence) limits understanding of why self-consistency is superior to other uncertainty estimates (Section 3.3, Section 4.4). This reduces technical soundness by leaving the choice of proxy unvalidated against alternatives.- **Fixed Threshold and Lack of Adaptive Mechanisms**
  - The agreement threshold τ=4/5 is manually selected and fixed across all domains, despite Section 4.5 explicitly noting substantial precision variations across categories (e.g., physics 89% vs law 48%). This rigidity limits the method's robustness to heterogeneous benchmarks.
  - No dynamic or adaptive thresholding strategy is proposed or evaluated, which restricts the method's generalizability to unseen domains or shifting data distributions (Section 3.3, Section 5 Conclusion). This weakens technical soundness by relying on static hyperparameters.
  - The precision-coverage trade-off is discussed qualitatively, but no formal optimization or automated threshold selection procedure is provided (Section 3.3, Table 1). This reduces experimental rigor by leaving threshold selection heuristic rather than principled.- **Narrow Evaluation Scope and Synthetic Candidate Construction**
  - The evaluation relies solely on MMLU-Pro with artificially constructed candidate pairs (one correct, one randomly incorrect), which may not reflect real-world open-ended generation evaluation scenarios (Section 4.1, Section 4.5). This limits the impact and generalizability of the findings.
  - The pairwise preference task is restricted to multiple-choice questions with 10 options, limiting applicability to free-form text generation benchmarks like MT-Bench or AlpacaEval (Section 4.1, Section 2 Related Work). This narrow scope reduces the method's broader relevance to LLM-as-a-Judge research.
  - The generator model used to create candidate responses is not specified, raising questions about response quality distribution and potential bias in the evaluation setup (Section 4.1). This omission weakens experimental rigor and reproducibility.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Baselines and Quantify Computational Overhead**
  - Include comparisons with state-of-the-art LLM-as-a-Judge methods (e.g., TrustJudge, JudgeBench baselines) to contextualize the absolute performance gains and establish stronger impact (Section 2, Section 4.1).
  - Report detailed latency and token consumption metrics for the k=5 sampling process to clarify the practical compute trade-off and deployment feasibility (Section 3.2, Section 5).
  - Add ablation experiments comparing self-consistency gating against alternative uncertainty signals like token log-probabilities or explicit confidence scoring to validate the proxy choice (Section 3.3, Section 4.4).- **Implement and Evaluate Adaptive Thresholding**
  - Develop a domain-aware or data-driven threshold selection mechanism that automatically adjusts τ based on calibration curves or validation performance to handle heterogeneous categories (Section 3.3, Section 4.5).
  - Provide a formal optimization framework for the precision-coverage trade-off, such as maximizing expected accuracy under a compute budget constraint, to replace heuristic thresholding (Section 3.3, Table 1).
  - Include experiments demonstrating how adaptive thresholds improve performance across the heterogeneous categories identified in Section 4.5, thereby strengthening generalizability (Section 4.5, Section 5).- **Broaden Evaluation to Realistic Generation Settings**
  - Extend experiments to open-ended benchmarks (e.g., MT-Bench, AlpacaEval) where candidate responses are naturally generated rather than synthetically constructed to assess real-world impact (Section 4.1, Section 2).
  - Specify the generator model and response construction pipeline, and analyze how response quality variance affects gate reliability and judgment accuracy to improve reproducibility (Section 4.1).
  - Evaluate the method on diverse task types beyond multiple-choice, including reasoning, coding, or creative writing, to verify generalizability across the LLM-as-a-Judge landscape (Section 4.1, Section 5).5) Score

- Novelty (10): 7 — The selective self-reference concept via self-consistency gating is a logical extension of existing work, offering a practical but incremental advance over always-reference and no-reference paradigms (Sections 1, 2, 3).

- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear figures, tables, and logical flow, making the problem, method, and results easy to follow (Sections 1, 3, 4, Figures 1-3).
