Academic integrity and internal consistency risk report

Scope: Review focuses on clear, evidence-based inconsistencies and material ambiguities that affect the paper’s correctness and trustworthiness.

Major issues

1) Numerical inconsistency for K = 5 results (Citation-Consistent Voting)
- Evidence:
  - Table 2 (Performance Scaling) reports at K = 5: Citation-Consistent Voting SubEM = 45.81, Majority Voting = 45.82; Δ = −0.01 (Block 24).
  - Figure 2 caption reiterates Δ = −0.01 at K = 5 (Block 24; see plotted labels in Figure 2 image, Block 27).
  - Table 3 (Ablation at K = 5) reports Citation Vote (no quote) SubEM = 46.37 and Citation Vote (with quote) SubEM = 45.73; MoA Majority Vote = 45.71 (Block 28).
- Problem:
  - At K = 5, the reported SubEM for the citation method is 45.81 in Table 2, but 46.37 (no quote) or 45.73 (with quote) in Table 3. The 45.81 value does not match either variant reported in the ablation.
  - The narrative says the two methods perform “nearly identically (Δ = −0.01)” at K = 5 (Block 25), which conflicts with Table 3 where the “no quote” variant outperforms MoA by +0.66 SubEM (46.37 vs 45.71).
- Why it matters:
  - This inconsistency obscures which variant and numbers are used for K-scaling and undermines the claim about performance at low K. It materially affects interpretation of the method’s benefits.

2) Definition and use of “evidence-valid” responses are inconsistent across sections and results
- Evidence:
  - Introduction states CCV “filters responses for evidence validity (ensuring the answer is grounded in the cited document)” (Block 4).
  - Method 3.2 defines evidence validity strictly: the quote must appear in the cited document and the answer must appear in the quote (Block 15).
  - Practical considerations 3.4 explicitly relax this to “only require valid document index (without quote verification)” and says both variants are evaluated (Block 20).
  - Main results and K-scaling tables (Table 1, Table 2; Blocks 18 and 24) do not specify which variant (with-quote vs no-quote) is used. The K = 5 discrepancy noted above suggests mixed or unspecified variants across tables.
- Problem:
  - The paper’s core positioning (evidence-grounded aggregation) is weakened if the main results use the “no quote” variant, which does not verify grounding. The lack of clear labeling for which variant is used in each table/figure leaves the reader unable to assess whether reported gains arise from truly evidence-validated citations or from a weaker proxy (index-only).
- Why it matters:
  - Whether outputs are actually grounded is central to the paper’s claim about citation consistency as a reliability signal. Ambiguity here affects the validity of conclusions.

Additional issues and ambiguities

3) Ambiguity in statistical diagnostic context
- Evidence: Diagnostic analysis reports mean agreement scores (3.04 vs 2.85) with Mann-Whitney p = 1.14 × 10^-5 (Block 29).
- Issue: The value of K and the variant (with-quote vs no-quote) for this analysis are not specified. Given that agreement scores depend on how “evidence-valid” responses are filtered, this omission limits interpretability.
- Impact: Hinders assessment of the robustness of the reported statistical relationship between citation agreement and accuracy.

4) Reference formatting inconsistency (minor)
- Evidence: Contriever is cited as “Izacard et al., 2021” in text (Block 22) but listed as “Trans. Mach. Learn. Res., 2022, 2021” in References (Block 31). The Natural Questions citation is split across two blocks (Blocks 31–32).
- Impact: Minor bibliographic inconsistency; does not materially affect the scientific claims.

Requests for clarification/corrections

- Specify, for every reported number in Table 1, Table 2, and Figure 2, whether the “with quote” or “no quote” evidence filter was used. If mixed, re-run to ensure consistent variant usage across all K for fair comparison.
- Reconcile the K = 5 Citation-Consistent Voting SubEM discrepancy:
  - If Table 2 used the “with quote” variant (45.73%), correct the 45.81% value or explain any averaging/rounding differences.
  - If Table 3’s 46.37% reflects a different sampling or setup than Table 2 (e.g., different permutation seeds or document sets), clearly state this and report comparable uncertainty (e.g., mean ± std over multiple seeds) for both tables.
- Align claims about “ensuring the answer is grounded in the cited document” with the actual variant used for main conclusions. If the “no quote” variant underpins key gains, adjust the narrative to avoid implying verified grounding, or add analyses verifying grounding quality under the relaxed filter.
- For the diagnostic analysis, report:
  - Which variant was used (with-quote vs no-quote),
  - The value of K,
  - Sample sizes for correct vs incorrect groups, and an effect size, to contextualize the p-value.

Conclusion

The manuscript contains a substantive, evidence-backed inconsistency in reported K = 5 results across tables and an important ambiguity about the definition and use of “evidence-valid” filtering in the main results. These issues materially affect the interpretation of robustness claims. Apart from these, no additional clear integrity-related contradictions were identified based on the provided text.
