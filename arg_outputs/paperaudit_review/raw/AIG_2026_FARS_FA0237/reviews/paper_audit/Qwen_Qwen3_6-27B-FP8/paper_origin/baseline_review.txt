1) Summary
The paper addresses the knowledge-conflict problem in retrieval-augmented generation, where large language models prioritize parametric knowledge over provided context. The authors hypothesize that entity surface forms trigger parametric recall and propose Entity-Anonymized Context Prompts (EACP), a training-free method that replaces entity names with placeholders before prompting. Evaluated on the ConFiQA-MC benchmark, EACP improves context-faithful answer rates by +42.28 points over a matched control condition. The method generalizes across Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, complements activation steering techniques, outperforms training-based Context-DPO, and maintains performance on non-conflict scenarios.2) Strengths
- **Rigorous Experimental Design Isolating the Active Ingredient**
  - The three-condition setup (Baseline A, Control B, EACP C) explicitly controls for output format constraints, isolating anonymization as the sole variable (Section 3.3, Figure 1). This matters because it prevents confounding effects from structured prompting, a common pitfall in prompt engineering papers.
  - The control condition B actually degrades performance (Pc=32.47% vs A=52.43%), proving that format alone is insufficient and highlighting the necessity of anonymization (Table 1, Section 4.2). This strengthens the causal claim regarding the method's mechanism.
  - The direct C vs B comparison yields a +42.28 Pc gain, providing clear quantitative evidence that entity masking is the primary driver of improvement (Figure 2, Section 4.2). This establishes a strong, reproducible baseline for future faithfulness research.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation Across Multiple Dimensions**
  - Cross-model validation on both Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B demonstrates robust generalization, with Qwen showing even larger gains (+49.20 Pc) (Table 3, Figure 3, Section 4.4). This matters for practical deployment across diverse model families and training paradigms.
  - The no-harm control on non-counterfactual contexts shows EACP improves exact match accuracy to 88.00%, surpassing both baseline and control conditions (Table 4, Section 4.5). This addresses a critical safety concern for real-world RAG systems where conflicts are not ubiquitous.
  - Integration with ContextFocus activation steering yields additive gains (Pc=76.47%), confirming that EACP addresses a distinct failure mode at the input level rather than overlapping with representation-level interventions (Table 2, Section 4.3). This expands the method's applicability in hybrid pipelines.
- **Practical and Training-Free Methodology**
  - The method requires no fine-tuning or gradient updates, relying solely on string replacement and inventory construction, which drastically reduces computational overhead compared to Context-DPO (Section 3.2, Table 1). This matters for resource-constrained deployments and rapid iteration.
  - The inclusion of phantom entity tagging and self-consistency decoding provides actionable optimizations that handle edge cases like out-of-context answer candidates (Section 3.4). This enhances robustness in multiple-choice settings without architectural changes.
  - The clear mapping and inverse decoding pipeline ensures that final outputs remain human-readable while preserving the internal anonymization benefit (Section 3.2). This maintains usability and evaluation compatibility without sacrificing faithfulness.3) Weaknesses
- **Reliance on Pre-Annotated Entities Limits Real-World Applicability**
  - The method explicitly states that entity extraction uses "entities explicitly annotated in the benchmark metadata" rather than an off-the-shelf NER system (Section 3.2). This matters because real-world RAG pipelines cannot rely on gold annotations, introducing a significant deployment gap.
  - No ablation or error analysis is provided for imperfect NER extraction, such as missed entities or false positives, which could severely degrade the anonymization effect (Section 3.2, Section 5). This leaves the method's robustness to noisy preprocessing unverified.
  - The boundary-aware string replacement strategy may fail on complex entity mentions (e.g., nested names, abbreviations, or multi-word aliases) without explicit handling rules (Section 3.2). This limits scalability to diverse document types and unstructured web content.
- **Limited Benchmark Scope and Task Generalization**
  - Evaluation is restricted solely to ConFiQA-MC, a multiple-choice QA benchmark, with no testing on open-ended generation, summarization, or dialogue tasks (Section 4.1, Section 5). This matters because knowledge conflicts manifest differently across downstream applications and output formats.
  - The benchmark focuses on English, and the paper acknowledges but does not investigate cross-lingual generalization (Section 5). This restricts the claimed broad applicability of entity-triggered parametric recall to multilingual RAG systems.
  - No analysis is provided on entity type distribution (e.g., PERSON vs. LOCATION vs. ORGANIZATION) to determine which categories benefit most from anonymization (Section 5, Table 3). This misses an opportunity to refine the method for high-impact entity classes and optimize computational cost.
- **Incomplete Baseline Comparisons and Contextual Nuances**
  - The comparison with Context-DPO uses different model architectures (Llama-3.1-8B vs. Qwen2-7B), making the direct performance comparison potentially confounded by model-specific capabilities (Table 1, Section 4.2). This weakens the claim that prompt-level interventions universally outperform training-based methods.
  - The paper does not evaluate against other recent training-free faithfulness methods (e.g., contrastive decoding variants or retrieval filtering strategies) beyond ContextFocus (Section 2, Section 4.3). This leaves the relative positioning of EACP within the broader literature unclear.
  - The self-consistency decoding uses $k=8$ samples with $\tau=0.7$, which increases inference latency and computational cost, yet no latency/throughput analysis is provided (Section 3.4). This matters for practical RAG system deployment where speed and token budget are critical constraints.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Enhance NER Robustness and Provide Error Analysis**
  - Integrate a state-of-the-art open-source NER model (e.g., spaCy or a lightweight transformer) and report performance degradation under realistic extraction noise (Section 3.2). This would bridge the gap between benchmark evaluation and production deployment.
  - Conduct an ablation study varying NER precision/recall thresholds to quantify the sensitivity of EACP to missed or incorrectly tagged entities (Section 3.2, Section 5). This would establish operational guidelines for preprocessing pipelines.
  - Implement and evaluate fallback mechanisms for complex entity mentions, such as regex-based alias expansion or context-window co-reference resolution, to handle multi-word or nested names (Section 3.2). This would improve robustness across diverse document formats.
- **Expand Evaluation Scope and Task Diversity**
  - Extend experiments to open-ended QA and summarization benchmarks (e.g., FaithEval or custom counterfactual summarization sets) to validate generalization beyond multiple-choice formats (Section 4.1, Section 5). This would strengthen claims about broad applicability.
  - Include multilingual evaluation on at least two non-English languages to test whether entity-triggered parametric recall and anonymization benefits transfer across linguistic boundaries (Section 5). This would address the acknowledged limitation and broaden impact.
  - Perform a stratified analysis by entity type (PERSON, LOCATION, ORGANIZATION, etc.) to identify which categories drive the largest faithfulness gains, enabling targeted anonymization strategies (Section 5, Table 3). This would provide actionable insights for domain-specific tuning.
- **Strengthen Baseline Comparisons and Efficiency Analysis**
  - Re-evaluate Context-DPO on the exact same model architecture (Llama-3.1-8B) to ensure a fair, apples-to-apples comparison between training-free and training-based approaches (Table 1, Section 4.2). This would solidify the claim regarding computational efficiency versus performance trade-offs.
  - Include comparisons with additional training-free baselines such as Context-Aware Decoding or Adaptive Contrastive Decoding to position EACP within the full spectrum of inference-time interventions (Section 2, Section 4.3). This would provide a more comprehensive methodological landscape.
  - Report inference latency, token throughput, and memory overhead for the self-consistency decoding variant ($k=8$) versus greedy decoding, alongside a cost-benefit analysis of the accuracy gains (Section 3.4). This would address practical deployment constraints and guide users on when to apply sampling.5) Score
