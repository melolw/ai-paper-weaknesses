1) Summary
The paper addresses parameter interference when merging multiple task-specific LoRA adapters into a single multi-task model. It identifies a critical failure mode in existing adaptive merging methods that rely on entropy minimization, arguing that this reference-free objective can produce overconfident but incorrect predictions. To mitigate this, the authors propose SourceJS-LoRA, which learns merge coefficients by minimizing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the merged model’s outputs and the predictions of individual task experts on unlabeled data. The method supports both task-wise and layer-wise coefficient optimization with L2 regularization. Evaluated on eight GLUE tasks using T5-base, SourceJS-LoRA achieves 83.10% average accuracy, surpassing DO-Merging and entropy-based baselines. The authors further demonstrate that their approach yields stable, positive merge coefficients, contrasting with the extreme and negative values observed under entropy minimization.2) Strengths
- **Clear Identification of a Fundamental Limitation in Reference-Free Objectives**
  - Section 3.2 and Equation 2: The authors formally define the entropy minimization objective and explicitly highlight its reference-free nature, which theoretically allows the model to become confident on incorrect predictions. This matters because it provides a rigorous conceptual critique that directly motivates the need for a teacher-anchored alternative.
  - Section 4.2 and Table 1: Empirical results show entropy minimization underperforms simple uniform merging (77.55% vs 79.76%), particularly on CoLA and MRPC. This matters because it transforms a theoretical concern into a validated empirical failure mode, strengthening the paper's motivation.
  - Section 3.3: The contrast between reference-free confidence optimization and source-referenced divergence minimization is clearly articulated. This matters because it establishes a principled foundation for the proposed method and clarifies the conceptual shift required to prevent confidence collapse.- **Principled and Parameter-Efficient Optimization Framework**
  - Section 3.3 and Equations 3–4: The use of Jensen-Shannon divergence to anchor merged predictions to task experts leverages a symmetric, bounded metric. This matters because it naturally prevents probability collapse and ensures stable gradient signals during coefficient optimization.
  - Section 3.4 and Equation 6: The extension to layer-wise coefficients introduces only 576 trainable parameters for T5-base while enabling fine-grained control. This matters because it demonstrates high parameter efficiency and shows that interference resolution benefits from depth-aware weighting.
  - Section 3.5 and Equation 7: Incorporating L2 regularization toward uniform initialization acts as a practical stabilizer. This matters because it complements the JS objective by preventing extreme coefficient drift, directly aligning with the stability results shown in Figure 2.- **Comprehensive and Insightful Experimental Evaluation**
  - Section 4.1: Evaluation spans eight diverse GLUE tasks with T5-base, reporting averages over three random seeds. This matters because it ensures robust benchmarking across different NLU task types and mitigates variance concerns common in adaptive merging.
  - Section 4.4 and Figure 2: Detailed coefficient stability analysis reveals positive, low-variance values for SourceJS-LoRA versus extreme/negative values for entropy minimization. This matters because it provides interpretable diagnostics that directly link the optimization objective to the observed performance gains.
  - Section 4.3 and Table 2: The ablation study comparing task-wise versus layer-wise coefficients reveals that finer granularity requires careful hyperparameter tuning. This matters because it demonstrates rigorous methodological validation and offers practical guidance for real-world deployment.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Evaluation Scope and Model Scale**
  - Section 4.1 and Section 5 (Limitations): Evaluation is restricted to T5-base (220M parameters) on GLUE tasks. This matters because it fails to demonstrate scalability or generalization to larger decoder-only LLMs or modern instruction-tuning benchmarks, which are standard in current merging literature.
  - Section 4.1: No evaluation on generation-heavy or open-ended tasks is provided. This matters because JS divergence is computed over a discrete label space (Equation 5), which may not translate well to autoregressive generation where vocabulary size and distribution shift are significant.
  - Section 4.1: Only a single base model architecture is tested. This matters because the method's effectiveness might be confounded by T5's encoder-decoder structure rather than being a general property of LoRA merging across architectures.- **Insufficient Analysis of Computational and Data Requirements**
  - Section 3.1 and Figure 1: The pipeline requires forward passes through all K expert models for every optimization step. This matters because this multiplies inference compute by K during coefficient learning, which could be prohibitive for large K, yet no complexity analysis is provided.
  - Section 3.5 and Appendix A: The method relies on 128 unlabeled samples per task sampled from the validation split. This matters because using validation data for coefficient optimization risks data leakage and overfitting to the evaluation distribution, undermining claims of practicality in data-scarce scenarios.
  - Section 4.1–4.2: No comparison of optimization time or memory overhead against baselines like DO-Merging or AdaMerging is included. This matters because practical adoption depends on efficiency; without runtime/memory metrics, the trade-off between accuracy gains and computational cost remains unclear.- **Theoretical and Implementation Gaps in the Objective**
  - Section 3.3 and Equation 5: JS divergence is computed over verbalized label distributions rather than full token probabilities. This matters because this approximation may distort the true predictive distribution, especially for tasks with nuanced label mappings, potentially biasing the coefficient optimization.
  - Section 3.5 and Appendix A: The choice of L2 regularization weight (λ = 0.0002) and learning rate (0.02) is presented without sensitivity analysis. This matters because hyperparameter sensitivity is critical for adaptive merging; without ablation on λ or LR, reproducibility and robustness across different task sets are questionable.
  - Section 3.3: No discussion on how JS divergence handles conflicting expert predictions when tasks inherently disagree on overlapping inputs. This matters because in multi-task settings, experts may legitimately disagree; forcing agreement via JS minimization could suppress valid task-specific nuances rather than resolving interference.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope and Benchmark Diversity**
  - Extend experiments to larger decoder-only LLMs (e.g., Llama-3-8B) and modern instruction-tuning benchmarks to validate scalability beyond T5-base and GLUE.
  - Include generation-heavy tasks to test the robustness of the label-space JS approximation in autoregressive settings where full vocabulary distributions are more relevant.
  - Evaluate on at least one alternative architecture (e.g., BERT or Mistral) to demonstrate that the source-referenced objective generalizes across encoder-only and decoder-only designs.- **Clarify Computational Costs and Mitigate Data Leakage Risks**
  - Provide a detailed complexity analysis and empirical runtime/memory measurements comparing SourceJS-LoRA against DO-Merging and AdaMerging to quantify the overhead of K expert forward passes.
  - Replace validation split sampling with a strictly held-out unlabeled dataset or synthetic data generation to eliminate data leakage concerns and strengthen claims about practicality in data-scarce regimes.
  - Report optimization convergence curves and step counts across different K values to help practitioners estimate the computational budget required for coefficient learning.- **Strengthen Theoretical Justification and Hyperparameter Robustness**
  - Conduct a sensitivity analysis on the L2 regularization weight λ and learning rate, reporting performance variance across a grid of values to establish robust hyperparameter ranges.
  - Investigate alternative distributional approximations (e.g., top-k token probabilities or full vocabulary sampling) to verify that the verbalized label space does not artificially constrain the optimization landscape.
  - Analyze cases where task experts inherently disagree on overlapping inputs, potentially introducing a conflict-aware weighting mechanism or discussing how JS divergence naturally balances competing signals.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The paper presents a well-motivated divergence-based merging objective with strong empirical results on GLUE (Section 4.2), but limited model scale and missing efficiency analysis (Section 4.1) temper its broader impact.
- Novelty (10): 7 — The shift from reference-free entropy minimization to source-anchored JS divergence (Section 3.3, Equation 3) offers a clear conceptual advance, though divergence-based alignment has precedents in knowledge distillation.
- Technical Quality (10): 6 — The method is sound and ablated (Section 4.3, Table 2), yet the reliance on validation data for optimization (Appendix A) and lack of hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (Section 3.5) reduce technical rigor.
- Clarity (10): 8 — The manuscript is well-structured with clear mathematical formulations (Section 3.3–3.5) and intuitive visualizations of coefficient stability (Figure 2), making the approach easy to follow.
- Confidence (5): 4 — High confidence in the critique due to clear methodological descriptions and reproducible setup details (Appendix A), though missing full table renderings slightly limit complete numerical verification.
