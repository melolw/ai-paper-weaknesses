1) Summary
The manuscript addresses citation hallucination and attribution inconsistency in long-form deep research reports by proposing QuoteVerify, an inference-time verification pipeline. The method requires base LLMs to generate structured citation triples containing explicit evidence quotes, then applies a five-stage process: source fetching, mechanical quote validity checking via substring/fuzzy matching, NLI-based entailment gating, and a repair-or-drop mechanism. Evaluated on a 20-prompt pilot subset of ReportBench using GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro, the pipeline achieves statistically significant match rate improvements over a standard baseline (+18.7pp and +12.5pp, respectively). The authors demonstrate that the structured triple format drives most gains, while quote validity remains the primary bottleneck, with LLMs producing verbatim quotes only 18–28% of the time. The work concludes by highlighting precision-coverage trade-offs and suggesting future directions for relaxed matching and improved quote generation fidelity.2) Strengths
- **Practical Inference-Time Architecture & Modularity**
  - The pipeline operates as a training-free wrapper around any base report generator (Section 3.2, Section 4.1), which matters for technical soundness and impact by enabling immediate deployment with closed-source or proprietary models without fine-tuning overhead.
  - The five-stage design cleanly separates mechanical verification from semantic validation (Figure 1, Sections 3.3–3.7), which matters for clarity and experimental rigor by creating a debuggable, interpretable verification flow that isolates failure modes.
  - The system outputs a structured JSONL audit log alongside the verified report (Section 3.7), which matters for impact and transparency by enabling downstream tracking, human review, and systematic error auditing.
- **Rigorous Empirical Evaluation & Statistical Validation**
  - Main results report paired bootstrap testing with explicit p-values (Table 1, Section 4.2), which matters for experimental rigor by establishing statistical significance rather than relying on point estimates alone.
  - Comprehensive ablation studies isolate the NLI entailment gate and repair-or-drop components (Table 2, Section 4.3), which matters for technical soundness by quantifying each module's contribution and revealing the marginal utility of the repair step.
  - Multi-metric reporting includes both quality (Match Rate, Quote Valid, Entail Pass) and coverage (Reference Recall, #Cited) indicators (Table 1, Section 4.2), which matters for impact by providing an honest assessment of the precision-coverage trade-off inherent in strict verification.
- **Insightful Error Analysis & Bottleneck Identification**
  - The authors quantify quote validity as the primary failure mode despite high source fetch success rates (Figure 2b, Section 4.4), which matters for novelty and impact by directing future research toward verbatim quote generation rather than retrieval infrastructure.
  - Model-specific failure distribution analysis reveals concentrated Quote+Entailment failures for GPT-4o versus distributed failures for Gemini-2.5-Pro (Figure 2a, Section 4.4), which matters for technical soundness by highlighting architectural differences in citation fidelity and suggesting model-specific optimization paths.
  - The identification of "irrelevant quote attacks" and their mitigation via the NLI gate (Section 3.6, Table 2) matters for experimental rigor by demonstrating that substring matching alone is insufficient and semantic gating is necessary to prevent false positives.3) Weaknesses
- **Limited Dataset Scale & Domain Coverage**
  - The evaluation relies on a pilot subset of only 20 prompts across 10 domains (Section 4.1), which matters for experimental rigor because small sample sizes risk overfitting to specific prompt templates and limit the statistical power of generalizability claims.
  - Two prompts per domain may not capture intra-domain variance or topic complexity (Section 4.1), which matters for impact because domain-specific citation patterns and source availability could significantly alter pipeline performance.
  - No evaluation is conducted on non-academic or mixed-source reports (Section 4.1, Section 4.4), which matters for technical soundness because the pipeline's robustness to varying document structures (e.g., news articles, blogs, technical manuals) remains unverified.
- **Overly Strict Quote Matching & Repair Mechanism Limitations**
  - The substring and fuzzy trigram matching (≥90% overlap) fails on minor paraphrasing or formatting differences (Section 3.5, Section 4.4), which matters for technical soundness because it artificially inflates failure rates and drops semantically valid citations, undermining practical utility.
  - The repair step demonstrates marginal gain (+0.4pp match rate) and high drop rates (Table 2, Section 4.3), which matters for experimental rigor because it suggests the repair prompt is either ineffective, too conservative, or computationally inefficient relative to its benefit.
  - No analysis of repair loop iterations, convergence behavior, or computational/API costs is provided (Section 3.7, Section 4.3), which matters for impact because deployment feasibility and latency constraints are critical for inference-time verification systems.
- **Ambiguous Baseline Comparison & Metric Operationalization**
  - The Prompt-Only baseline matches or slightly exceeds QuoteVerify in Match Rate (Table 1, Section 4.2), which matters for experimental rigor because it raises questions about whether the verification pipeline actually improves the primary metric or merely filters out uncertain citations.
  - Reference Recall drops drastically (62–66% relative decrease) without clear justification for when this trade-off is acceptable (Table 1, Section 4.2), which matters for impact because practitioners need application-specific guidance on balancing citation precision against comprehensive literature coverage.
  - The Match Rate metric is described as measuring semantic consistency but lacks explicit operationalization (Section 4.1), which matters for technical soundness and reproducibility because it is unclear whether an LLM-as-judge, embedding similarity, or manual annotation was used to compute the primary evaluation score.
- **Internal Inconsistencies & Reproducibility Concerns**
  - Conflicting model version names (Gemini-1.5-Pro vs. Gemini-2.5-Pro) appear across the introduction, tables, and experimental sections (Section 1, Table 1, Section 4.1, Figure 2), which matters for reproducibility and experimental clarity by creating ambiguity about which model was actually evaluated.
  - A numerical mismatch exists between the table data for Figure 2(a) (11.6% Quote+Entailment Fail) and the corresponding chart visualization (17.0%), which matters for technical soundness and data integrity by indicating potential errors in figure generation or data reporting.
  - The explicit disclaimer that the paper was auto-generated (Abstract) combined with these inconsistencies raises concerns about the reliability of the reported metrics and references, which matters for impact by reducing reader trust in the empirical claims.4) Suggestions for Improvement
- **Expand Evaluation Scope & Diversity**
  - Scale evaluation to the full ReportBench dataset or incorporate complementary long-form generation benchmarks (Section 4.1, Section 5) to validate generalizability across diverse domains, prompt complexities, and citation densities.
  - Increase intra-domain sampling to ≥5 prompts per domain (Section 4.1) to provide robust statistical power for domain-specific claims and capture variance in source availability and citation styles.
  - Test the pipeline on mixed-source reports combining academic papers, news articles, and technical documentation (Section 4.1) to assess robustness to varying document structures, parsing challenges, and citation conventions.
- **Refine Matching Tolerance & Repair Strategy**
  - Integrate semantic similarity thresholds (e.g., embedding cosine similarity) alongside substring matching (Section 3.5, Section 5) to reduce false negatives from minor paraphrasing while maintaining verification rigor and improving quote validity rates.
  - Analyze repair loop convergence, iteration limits, and API/token costs (Section 3.7, Section 4.3) to provide practical deployment metrics, justify computational overhead, and establish latency bounds for real-time verification.
  - Experiment with alternative repair prompts, few-shot examples, or iterative refinement strategies (Section 3.7, Table 2) to increase the marginal utility of the repair step beyond the observed +0.4pp gain and reduce unnecessary citation drops.
- **Clarify Baselines & Metric Operationalization**
  - Provide explicit prompt templates and system instructions for the Standard and Prompt-Only baselines (Section 4.1) to ensure reproducibility, enable fair comparison of citation generation strategies, and clarify the exact intervention introduced by QuoteVerify.
  - Detail the exact procedure for computing Match Rate, including the model, prompt, threshold, or annotation protocol used (Section 4.1) to resolve ambiguity in the primary evaluation metric and facilitate independent replication.
  - Discuss application-specific thresholds for the precision-coverage trade-off, such as high-stakes academic review versus exploratory literature surveys (Table 1, Section 4.2) to help practitioners determine when QuoteVerify's strict filtering is justified and how to tune pipeline parameters accordingly.
- **Resolve Inconsistencies & Clarify Reproducibility Details**
  - Standardize the model version name throughout the manuscript (Section 1, Table 1, Section 4.1) to eliminate ambiguity and ensure accurate replication of the experimental setup.
  - Correct the numerical discrepancy in Figure 2(a) to align the chart visualization with the underlying table data (Figure 2, Section 4.4) and verify all other visualizations against their source tables.
  - Provide explicit prompt templates and system instructions for the verification steps (Section 3.7, Section 4.1) to fully offset the reproducibility concerns raised by the automated generation disclaimer and strengthen methodological transparency.5) Score
- Overall (10): 6 — The pipeline offers a practical inference-time verification framework with clear modular design (Section 3.2, Figure 1), but limited dataset scale (Section 4.1) and internal inconsistencies (Table 1, Figure 2) constrain broader impact.
- Novelty (10): 6 — The quote-backed triple structure and multi-stage verification address a clear gap in long-form citation auditing (Section 1, Section 3.3), though the components build on established NLI and retrieval techniques (Section 2) and the repair step shows marginal utility (Table 2).
- Technical Quality (10): 5 — Statistical testing and ablations are rigorous (Table 1, Table 2), yet the repair mechanism's marginal utility (Table 2), undefined Match Rate computation (Section 4.1), and figure/table mismatches (Figure 2) reduce technical completeness.
- Clarity (10): 7 — The five-stage pipeline is well-illustrated and logically structured (Figure 1, Sections 3.3–3.7), with clear tables and figures supporting the analysis (Table 1, Figure 2), though conflicting model names (Section 1, Table 1) create minor confusion.
- Confidence (5): 3 — Moderate confidence based on clear methodology and results (Section 3, Section 4), though limited dataset size (Section 4.1), missing prompt details, and internal inconsistencies (Abstract, Figure 2) reduce certainty about generalizability and reproducibility.
