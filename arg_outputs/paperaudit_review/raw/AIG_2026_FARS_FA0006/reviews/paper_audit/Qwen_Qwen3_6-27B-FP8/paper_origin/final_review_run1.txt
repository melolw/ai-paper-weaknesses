1) Summary
The paper addresses the vulnerability of LLM-based judges to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) manipulation in web agent trajectory evaluation. The authors propose "view-disagreement escalation," a training-free framework that compares judgments from two counterfactual input views: one including the agent's CoT and one excluding it. The core hypothesis is that CoT manipulation causes disagreement between these views, triggering a selective escalation to a strict, evidence-anchored evaluation rubric. Evaluated on AgentRewardBench against a "Progress Fabrication" attack, the method achieves a 63% relative reduction in attack sensitivity ($\Delta$-FPR) compared to a standard CoT-inclusive judge, while maintaining competitive F1 scores and achieving the highest recall among all methods.2) Strengths
*   **Mechanistically Grounded Approach**
    *   The core insight that CoT manipulation affects CoT-dependent judgments while leaving CoT-agnostic judgments stable is logically sound and intuitively appealing (Section 3.2).
    *   The empirical validation in Figure 2 supports this hypothesis, showing a 2.15× enrichment in disagreement rates on attacked failures compared to unmodified failures.
    *   This mechanistic motivation distinguishes the method from generic self-consistency or confidence-based approaches, providing a clear rationale for the escalation trigger (Section 3.3).
*   **Effective Trade-off Between Robustness and Recall**
    *   The method successfully balances robustness and accuracy, achieving the best recall (77.63%) among all evaluated methods while maintaining a competitive F1 score (72.13%) (Table 1).
    *   Unlike the "View2-only" baseline which achieves zero attack sensitivity but suffers from lower recall (70.51%), the proposed method recovers more true successes by leveraging CoT when it is not manipulated (Section 4.2).
    *   The ablation study in Table 2 confirms that the improvement is due to the targeted disagreement signal rather than the escalation mechanism itself, as random escalation fails to reduce attack sensitivity.
*   **Practical and Training-Free Design**
    *   The framework requires no fine-tuning or additional training data, making it immediately applicable to existing judge models (Section 3.3).
    *   The selective escalation strategy limits the computational overhead of the strict rubric to only a subset of cases (~10.4% escalation rate in Table 2), offering a more efficient alternative to "Strict-always" evaluation.
    *   The use of deterministic decoding (temperature=0) ensures that disagreement stems from input view differences rather than sampling noise, enhancing reproducibility (Section 3.3).3) Weaknesses
*   **Limited Scope of Attack Evaluation**
    *   The evaluation relies solely on the "Progress Fabrication" attack (Section 4.1), which specifically targets CoT reasoning.
    *   There is no analysis of how the method performs against other potential manipulation strategies, such as hallucination in observations or action history, which might not trigger view disagreement.
    *   The robustness claims are therefore narrowly scoped to CoT manipulation, leaving open questions about general robustness to other adversarial inputs.
*   **Insufficient Cost Analysis**
    *   While the conclusion mentions a "2.1× calls per trajectory" overhead (Section 5), the main results (Table 1) do not include any metrics for inference cost, latency, or token usage.
    *   Without a direct cost comparison, it is difficult to assess the practical viability of the method compared to baselines like "View2-only" (which is cheaper and robust) or "Strict-always."
    *   The ablation study (Table 2) compares F1 and $\Delta$-FPR but omits cost metrics, making it hard to verify the claimed efficiency benefits of selective escalation.
*   **Ambiguity in "Strict" Rubric Definition**
    *   The paper describes the strict evidence-anchored rubric ($P_{strict}$) as excluding CoT and requiring explicit citation of evidence (Section 3.3), but the actual prompt or detailed criteria are not provided.
    *   The effectiveness of the escalation stage depends heavily on the quality of this rubric; without seeing the prompt, it is unclear how much of the robustness gain comes from the disagreement trigger versus the strict rubric itself.
    *   This lack of transparency makes it difficult for other researchers to reproduce the escalation component or compare it with other evidence-anchored methods.
*   **Marginal Robustness Gain Over Simple Baseline**
    *   The "View2-only" baseline achieves a $\Delta$-FPR of 0.00% (Table 1), meaning it is completely robust to the CoT manipulation attack by definition.
    *   The proposed method achieves a $\Delta$-FPR of 4.31%, which is an improvement over "View1-only" but actually *worse* than the simple "View2-only" baseline.
    *   The paper emphasizes the 63% reduction relative to "View1-only," but the comparison to "View2-only" suggests that the primary benefit of the proposed method is recall recovery, not robustness per se, which is not sufficiently highlighted in the discussion.
*   **Editorial and Citation Inconsistencies**
    *   The citation year for Khalifa et al. is listed as (2026) in the Abstract, Introduction, and References, but incorrectly cited as (2024) in Section 2.
    *   Similarly, Hong et al. is cited as (2024) in Section 2 but listed as (2026) in the References.
    *   Section 4.1 contains a typographical error citing "Lù et al., 2025" instead of "Lü et al., 2025" as correctly listed in the References. These inconsistencies reduce scholarly rigor and require correction.4) Suggestions for Improvement
*   **Expand Attack Evaluation**
    *   Include additional attack types beyond CoT manipulation, such as observation hallucination or action sequence tampering, to demonstrate the generalizability of the view-disagreement signal.
    *   Analyze whether the disagreement rate remains enriched under these alternative attacks, or if the method is specific to CoT-based vulnerabilities.
    *   Discuss the limitations of the method in scenarios where both views might be manipulated or where CoT is not the primary source of vulnerability.
*   **Provide Comprehensive Cost Analysis**
    *   Add a column or table in the main results (Table 1) reporting inference cost metrics (e.g., average tokens per trajectory, latency, or API cost) for all methods.
    *   Quantify the "2.1× calls" overhead mentioned in the conclusion and compare it against the "Strict-always" and "View2-only" baselines to provide a clear cost-benefit analysis.
    *   Include a sensitivity analysis showing how performance changes as the escalation threshold or strict rubric cost varies.
*   **Clarify the Strict Rubric Implementation**
    *   Provide the full text of the $P_{strict}$ prompt in an appendix or the main text to ensure reproducibility and transparency.
    *   Detail the specific criteria used for "explicit citation of evidence" and how the structured checklist is constructed for different tasks.
    *   Consider an ablation study comparing different strict rubric formulations to isolate the impact of the rubric quality on the final robustness gains.
*   **Reframe the Comparison with View2-Only**
    *   Explicitly discuss the trade-off between the proposed method and "View2-only," acknowledging that "View2-only" is already robust to CoT attacks.
    *   Emphasize that the primary contribution of the proposed method is the recovery of recall (77.63% vs. 70.51%) while maintaining *near*-robustness, rather than claiming superior robustness over all baselines.
    *   Analyze the specific cases where the proposed method succeeds and "View2-only" fails to provide insight into the value of CoT when it is not manipulated.
*   **Correct Editorial and Citation Errors**
    *   Standardize the citation year for Khalifa et al. across Section 2, the Abstract, and the References list.
    *   Align the citation year for Hong et al. between Section 2 and the References list.
    *   Correct the typographical error for "Lü et al." in Section 4.1 to match the References and ensure consistent proofreading throughout the manuscript.5) Score
