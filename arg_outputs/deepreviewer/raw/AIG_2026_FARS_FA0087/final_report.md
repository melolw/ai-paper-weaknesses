## Summary
The manuscript introduces RazorSFT, an on-policy supervised fine-tuning method for continual learning in large language models. It mitigates catastrophic forgetting by sampling candidate responses, verifying their correctness, and selecting the highest log-probability target (KL-minimal selection). On a 3-stage benchmark (IFEval → MMLU → Countdown), RazorSFT outperforms offline SFT in forgetting mitigation and surpasses GRPO in average accuracy. Ablation studies highlight that on-policy data drives the majority of the improvement, while KL-minimal selection provides marginal gains. The paper presents a clean methodological concept and well-executed experiments, but its novelty positioning is challenged by closely aligned prior work (Chen et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2025, 2026).

## Strengths
1. **Clean and Intuitive Methodological Design:** RazorSFT is elegantly simple—sampling, verifying, and selecting the highest log-probability target provides a clear, SFT-compatible framework for on-policy learning. The fallback to offline references ensures consistent training intensity.
2. **Compelling Main Results:** The empirical gains over offline SFT in forgetting mitigation are substantial (FM improvement of 60.4 pp), and RazorSFT demonstrably outperforms GRPO in average accuracy and current-task adaptation on the Countdown task.
3. **Strong Experimental Execution:** The 3-stage curriculum covers diverse task types (instruction following, knowledge, arithmetic), and the inclusion of variance over 3 seeds improves reproducibility. The forgetting trajectory analysis (Figure 2) and replacement rate dynamics (Figure 3) provide insightful mechanistic explanations.
4. **Well-Motivated by Recent Literature:** The paper correctly leverages the emerging consensus from Chen et al. (2025) and Shenfeld et al. (2025) that on-policy data is the primary driver of RL's forgetting mitigation.
5. **Transparent Acknowledgment:** The warning that the paper was generated by an automated research system and the open-source code availability improve transparency and reproducibility.

## Weaknesses
1. **Novelty Overlap with Prior On-Policy Work (Major):** The core insight that on-policy data mitigates forgetting is already established by Chen et al. (2025) and Shenfeld et al. (2025). Contribution C2 ("on-policy data accounts for 76% of the forgetting improvement") is a direct restatement of their findings. Furthermore, Shenfeld et al. (2026) SDFT proposes an on-policy distillation method for continual learning that is methodologically closer to RazorSFT than any RL method. The manuscript does not adequately differentiate from SDFT, leaving the residual novelty of KL-minimal selection under-foregrounded.
2. **Modest Practical Benefit of KL-Minimal Selection (Major):** The delta between First-Accepted (FM -0.042, AvgAcc 0.598) and KL-Minimal RazorSFT (FM -0.032, AvgAcc 0.619) is small. Without statistical significance testing, it is unclear whether this delta is practically meaningful or merely noise. The paper positions KL-minimal selection as a core contribution, but the evidence for its distinct value is thin.
3. **Factual Inaccuracy Regarding GRPO (Minor):** The Introduction claims that GRPO requires "reward model training." GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) does not use a separate reward model; it computes group-relative rewards directly from task verification. This inaccuracy undermines credibility with RL-savvy reviewers.
4. **Missing Differentiation from SDFT in Related Work (Major):** Shenfeld et al. (2026) is cited as concurrent work but not properly compared. SDFT's on-policy distillation mechanism is the most relevant methodological predecessor to RazorSFT, and its absence from the comparative narrative is a significant novelty gap.
5. **Incomplete Experimental Reproducibility Details (Minor):** The Experimental Setup omits learning rate schedule details, does not explicitly confirm compute budget matching for GRPO (group size k), and mixes inference-time baseline (Best-of-8) with training methods without clear demarcation.
6. **Conclusion Lacks Limitations Discussion (Minor):** The Conclusion does not acknowledge boundary conditions (e.g., verifier requirement, modest KL-minimal delta), reducing scientific credibility.

## Key Issues
1. **Novelty Positioning vs. SDFT and On-Policy SFT (Novelty/Validity Risk):** The paper's most significant risk is that reviewers will perceive RazorSFT as an incremental confirmation of the Chen 2025 / Shenfeld 2025 on-policy hypothesis, or as a variant of SDFT. The KL-minimal selection mechanism provides a small empirical gain that is not yet statistically validated. **Action:** Explicitly differentiate from SDFT in Related Work and Introduction; reframe Contribution C2 to focus on the selection criterion rather than the on-policy data finding.
2. **Unvalidated Statistical Significance of KL-Minimal Gain (Evidence Risk):** The difference between First-Accepted and KL-Minimal RazorSFT is marginal. Without significance testing, the claim that "KL-minimal selection provides meaningful additional benefit" is an overstatement. **Action:** Add a significance test or acknowledge the small magnitude of the delta.
3. **Factual Error on GRPO (Credibility Risk):** Stating that GRPO requires reward model training is incorrect. This error may undermine reviewer trust in the authors' understanding of their baselines. **Action:** Correct the description of GRPO in the Introduction.
4. **Missing Limitations Discussion (Defensibility Risk):** The absence of limitations in the Conclusion makes the paper appear less scientifically mature. Reviewers expect explicit boundary conditions. **Action:** Add 1-2 sentences on verifier dependency and potential generalization limits.

## Actionable Suggestions
1. **Differentiate from SDFT (Must):** Add a side-by-side comparison with Shenfeld et al. (2026) SDFT in both the Introduction and Related Work. Highlight the mechanism difference (KL-minimal selection among verifier-accepted candidates vs. in-context distillation), the training signal difference (verifier vs. teacher-conditioning), and the assumption difference (requires task verifier vs. requires in-context capability).
2. **Reframe Contribution C2 (Must):** Shift C2's focus from the on-policy data finding (which is Chen 2025's) to the KL-minimal selection mechanism. Example: "We demonstrate that explicit KL-minimal selection provides a measurable incremental benefit over unselected on-policy training, validating the benefit of explicit distributional shift minimization in SFT."
3. **Correct GRPO Description (Must):** In the Introduction, correct the claim that GRPO requires "reward model training." GRPO uses group-relative rewards directly from verification without a separate model.
4. **Enhance Ablation Analysis (Must):** Add a statistical significance test (e.g., paired t-test or bootstrap CI) for the KL-minimal vs. First-Accepted comparison. If the delta is not significant, soften the claim to "suggests a consistent but small benefit."
5. **Add Limitations (Must):** In the Conclusion, explicitly state the dependence on task verifiers and acknowledge that the KL-minimal delta, while consistent, is modest in magnitude.
6. **Fix Notation and Typos (Nice-to-have):** Correct $\pi \varrho_{e-1}$ to $\pi_{\theta_{e-1}}$, fix "main tains" to "maintains," and clarify the epoch-index timing in the method section.
7. **Clarify Experimental Setup (Nice-to-have):** Explicitly state whether GRPO uses the same $k$ for compute-matched comparison, and add learning rate schedule details (e.g., constant, cosine decay).

The paper presents a clean and intuitive on-policy SFT method with strong empirical gains over offline SFT in continual learning. However, the novelty of the core contribution is substantially challenged by prior work: the on-policy data hypothesis is already established by Chen et al. (2025) and Shenfeld et al.
