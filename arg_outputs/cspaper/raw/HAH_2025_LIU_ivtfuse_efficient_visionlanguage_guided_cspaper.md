## Paper Summary
The paper proposes IVTFuse, a tri-modal infrared-visible image fusion network guided by text. The architecture uses two lightweight attention modules in the IR/VIS encoders, Frequency Strip Attention (FSA) and Hybrid Pooling Attention (HPA), and conditions fusion on text embeddings from a pretrained BLIP encoder via cross-attention and channel reweighting. The system stacks three fusion blocks at full resolution and employs a compact Restormer-based decoder. Experiments on MSRS (train/val) with zero-shot testing on M³FD and RoadScene report improved quantitative metrics over 10 baselines and qualitative gains. Ablations on RoadScene isolate the contributions of FSA, HPA, and proper text guidance.

## Summary Of Strengths
- Clear, modular architecture: Figure 1 presents a straightforward tri-branch design where text features guide image features via cross-attention, followed by residual feature injection and a Restormer-based decoder. The flow through FSA/HPA and into fusion blocks is coherent.
- Lightweight attention ideas: FSA’s directional low/high-frequency decomposition with learnable recombination and residual gating is simple yet plausible for edge and blob enhancement. HPA’s grouped average/max pooling along axes is an efficient spatial saliency mechanism.
- Solid quantitative performance: Table 1 shows best or second-best results on most metrics across MSRS, M³FD, and RoadScene. The gains over strong baselines (e.g., FILM and CDDFuse) are consistent on EN, SF, AG, VIF, and Qabf.
- Useful ablations: Table 2 varies FSA, HPA, and text correctness across three seeds, showing that both modules help and that mismatched captions hurt fusion quality. The VIF/Qabf improvements when including FSA/HPA are meaningful.
- Qualitative results: Figure 2 illustrates perceptually sharper edges and target visibility compared to representative baselines; the fused outputs retain visible textures while enhancing thermal targets.
- Reproducibility basics: Training details, loss functions, datasets, and metrics are all stated; code is linked. The setup largely follows a known vision-language guided fusion baseline (FILM).

## Summary Of Weaknesses
1) Limited ACL relevance and positioning to NLP. The core problem is an image-fusion task; language is only used as a static caption embedding to reweight channels. There is no substantive language modeling question, no analysis of linguistic variability or grounding, and no NLP evaluation. The work fits computer vision more strongly than ACL’s multimodal language grounding track, and the paper does not motivate a language-centric contribution beyond using BLIP tokens for guidance.

2) Incomplete characterization of the “efficiency” claim. Table 3 shows comparable parameter count to FILM (2.08M vs 2.07M) but nearly 2× slower inference (130.14 ms vs 72.90 ms, 288×384, RTX 4090). The text still labels the method “efficient” without reporting FLOPs, memory, or throughput under varying resolutions, which weakens the title/claim. A deeper breakdown of FSA/HPA and cross-attention overheads is needed.

3) Text guidance pipeline and reproducibility gaps. Section 4 states captions are generated by ChatGPT and then encoded by BLIP, but it is unclear whether the exact captions are released, whether prompts are fixed, and how sensitive results are to caption length or style. Table 2 only studies “random text,” which is a coarse stress test. Without standardized, released captions (or a fixed rule to generate them), replicating the exact conditions is difficult.

4) Fusion mechanism analysis is shallow. Section 3.3 reduces text-conditioned cross-attention outputs by averaging across tokens and L1-normalizing to produce channel weights (Equation 2). This is a fairly crude bottleneck that discards token-level structure. There is no analysis of alternative reductions (e.g., attention pooling, learned token selectors, gating per spatial position), nor visualization of text-to-image attention to show that the semantics mentioned in the caption correlate with the fused output.

5) Missing or underspecified details in HPA. Section 3.2 describes HPA but omits exact tensor shapes, normalization details, and how the average/max branches are merged. Grouped processing (g=4) is mentioned, but the operations and residual connections are not formalized by equations, which complicates faithful reimplementation without code.

6) Qualitative analysis is light. Figure 2 shows one RowScene example; however, the text does not discuss common artifacts, failure modes, or specific structures where IVTFuse excels or fails compared to FILM/CDDFuse (e.g., halos, contrast washout, texture loss). Qualitative comparisons across diverse scenes would strengthen the narrative that text truly helps.

7) Lack of downstream task evaluation. Several recent IVIF papers report detection/segmentation transfer or target localization to demonstrate utility beyond low-level metrics. Here, only image-level fusion metrics (EN, SD, SF, AG, VIF, Qabf) are used. Since language guidance is supposed to improve “semantic fidelity,” downstream task gains would be a much stronger test of that claim.

8) Single-dataset ablation. Ablations in Table 2 are only on RoadScene. Since effects may vary with scene type and sensor pairing, reporting ablations on at least one other dataset would improve the robustness of the conclusions about FSA, HPA, and text.

9) Limited comparison to frequency- and attention-heavy baselines beyond those listed. The paper cites CDDFuse and others, but several closely related, recent frequency/spatial-attention IVIF approaches are not discussed or compared (see “Potentially Missing Related Work”). This undercuts the novelty and completeness of the positioning, especially given FSA/HPA’s conceptual overlap with prior frequency-aware or multi-attention designs.

10) Figure 1 leaves ambiguities. While the high-level pipeline is clear, the diagram does not depict token averaging and L1 normalization in Equation 2, nor does it show where channel dimension reductions (d=256 to d_proj=32 to D=32) happen relative to the Restormer blocks. For a paper that emphasizes efficiency and modularity, the figure should better reflect these key operations.

11) Metrics-only interpretation sometimes overreaches. Table 1 reports consistent gains in low-level metrics, but the text extrapolates to improved “semantic fidelity.” Without human studies, textual variation studies, or downstream tasks, this claim remains suggestive rather than established.

## Potentially Missing Related Work
1) Wang et al., “FCLFusion: A frequency-aware and collaborative learning for infrared and visible image fusion,” 2024 — Strongly relevant to FSA’s frequency emphasis. Discuss in Related Work (Section 2) and compare in Experiments or ablation; clarify differences between strip-wise frequency handling and FCLFusion’s frequency fusion and auxiliary branches.

2) Hu et al., “AFFusion: Atmospheric scattering enhancement and frequency integrated spatial–channel attention for infrared and visible image fusion,” 2026 — Attention-plus-frequency baseline that should be positioned against FSA/HPA. Add to Section 2 and, if possible, include as a baseline in Table 1.

3) Shi et al., “Infrared and visible image fusion network based on spatial-frequency domain cross-learning,” 2026 — Directly overlaps with the joint spatial–frequency rationale. Compare its cross-guidance to FSA+HPA in Section 2 and discuss expected behavior differences.

4) Li et al., “Infrared and visible image fusion method based on hierarchical attention mechanism,” 2024 — Hierarchical attention for salient target preservation; relevant to HPA and Restormer-based global context. Cite in Section 2 and contrast the attention hierarchy with HPA’s grouped pooling.

5) Qi et al., “A novel infrared and visible image fusion network based on cross-modality reinforcement and multi-attention fusion strategy,” 2025 — Conceptually close to multi-attention and cross-modality reinforcement. Add to Section 2 and discuss how text-guided reweighting differs from purely vision-based multi-attention.

6) Yan et al., “ATFusion: An alternate cross-attention transformer network for infrared and visible image fusion,” 2026 — Cross-attention variant directly relevant to your dual cross-attention and residual injection. Compare mechanisms in Section 3.3 discussion and add to Related Work.

7) “MTFusion: A semantic segmentation-guided infrared and visible image fusion network based on an attention-enhanced hybrid Mamba-Transformer architecture,” 2026 — Semantic guidance via segmentation provides a vision-only alternative to language guidance. Discuss in Related Work and consider a downstream-task comparison if feasible.

8) “A low-illumination infrared and visible image fusion network based on brightness normalization,” 2026 — Tests robustness under severe illumination, complementary to your semantic focus. Cite and, if possible, compare in low-light subsets or dedicated stress tests.

## Comments Suggestions And Typos
Actionable suggestions that could change my assessment:
- Provide released, fixed captions or a deterministic caption-generation script, and report sensitivity to caption paraphrasing, length, and noise. Include a small study where captions are varied systematically, not only “random text” (e.g., remove target nouns, replace attributes, add distractors). This would make the language guidance claim concrete and reproducible.
- Add downstream evaluations (e.g., detection/segmentation on MSRS-derived tasks or M³FD detection splits) to validate “semantic fidelity.” If language guidance matters, we should see improved target retention or task performance.
- Strengthen efficiency analysis beyond parameter count: report FLOPs, peak memory, runtime on multiple resolutions, and a per-module overhead breakdown (FSA, HPA, cross-attn). If “efficient” is a key selling point, reconcile Table 3’s slower runtime.
- Expand ablations to at least one more dataset, and visualize cross-attention maps to show where text attends in IR/VIS features. Include a variant that uses learned token pooling or spatially-varying reweighting instead of global channel weights, to test whether Equation 2’s averaging discards useful structure.
- Clarify HPA with complete equations and shapes (grouped channel dimensions, pooling outputs, fusion of average/max branches, normalization, and residual connections). Similarly, annotate Figure 1 to show the locations of the d=256, d_proj=32, and D=32 projections and the averaging/normalization step tied to Equation 2.

Typos and formatting:
- Table 1 heading “Quantitative results of on the VLF benchmark dataset” → “Quantitative results on the VLF benchmark.”
- Table 1 formatting is hard to parse; consider separate tables per dataset or a clearer multi-block layout.
- Some reference years overlap with in-text claims (e.g., [11] CVPR 2025 is cited as future work relative to the paper timeline). Ensure consistency of venues/years.
- Ensure consistent naming of baselines (e.g., SDN vs SDNet, TarD vs TarDAL, DeF vs DeFusion) and verify metric values correspond to cited papers’ official reports.

Criteria for score increase:
- Stronger language-centric experiments (caption variability, human studies on semantic alignment) and/or downstream task benefits; plus released captions or generation scripts that ensure exact reproducibility.
- Inclusion and discussion of the missing related works with additional baselines where feasible.
- A credible efficiency story with detailed profiling to justify “efficient.”

## Soundness
3: Acceptable — The design is reasonable and the experiments support main claims about metric improvements, but the language-guidance mechanism is under-analyzed, downstream validation is missing, and efficiency claims are not fully substantiated.

## Excitement
2.5: Between potentially interesting and interesting — The idea of using lightweight frequency/spatial attention with text guidance is appealing, but the contribution feels more CV than NLP and lacks deep language analysis.

## Overall Assessment
2.5 Borderline Findings: The paper may meet minimal standards but requires improvements before being accepted to Findings. While the model shows consistent metric gains and a clean design, the language component is shallow for an ACL audience, efficiency claims are not fully supported by runtime results, and important analyses (caption sensitivity, attention visualization, downstream tasks) are missing. With additional experiments addressing these points and stronger positioning relative to recent frequency/attention IVIF works, this could be a solid Findings paper.

## Limitations And Societal Impact
The paper includes a thoughtful section (Appendix A) on robustness to noisy captions and potential surveillance/privacy risks. Additional considerations worth adding:
- Bias in captions: show a mitigation strategy such as neutralization filters or confidence weighting of text features; consider auditing with adversarial or biased prompts.
- Failure modes: include qualitative examples where language guidance misdirects attention and discuss safeguards (e.g., fallback to vision-only if text–vision alignment confidence is low).
